## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: Treating equity as a straightforward cash-equivalent bonus

**What was tried:** An initial pass compared the equity grant's nominal percentage × a guessed current valuation directly against the cash being given up, as if the equity were a delayed cash bonus of known size.

**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-11 — equity is a claim that only becomes cash through a realized liquidity event — and by GT-5, which shows a majority of small businesses (a stand-in prior, per A20) do not persist even to a 5-year mark, let alone reach an exit. Treating a nominal percentage as spendable value ignores both the probability of a payout event and the dilution between now and any such event.

**What it ruled out:** Any "just multiply % by today's implied valuation" shortcut as a substitute for the probability- and dilution-adjusted Fermi bracket in chain C2.

### Dead End 2: Using team size (4 people) as a direct risk-level proxy

**What was tried:** An attempt to read "4-person startup" itself as informative about risk — either "very small, therefore very fragile" or "very small, therefore fully knowable/low-overhead."

**Why abandoned:** GT-6's own data shows an enormous interquartile spread in equity grants even at identical hire numbers (0.18%–1.00% at the 25th–75th percentile for hire #4), which is a proxy for an equally enormous spread in underlying funding, valuation, and negotiating circumstances at "4-person" startups generally. Team size carries almost no information about funding stage, revenue, or founder track record on its own.

**What it ruled out:** Using headcount alone (assumption A3) as a risk signal anywhere in the derivation chains; the analysis instead routes risk through GT-5 (failure-rate prior) and the inversion-derived preconditions (A12–A18), which name the facts that would actually distinguish a fragile 4-person startup from a well-positioned one.

### Dead End 3: Applying the theoretical-limit technique to bound a "maximum rational income sacrifice"

**What was tried:** Considering whether a Kelly-criterion-style ruin constraint could supply a hard, law-like ceiling on what fraction of income/savings it is ever rational to risk on a positive-but-uncertain-EV bet, independent of this person's specific numbers.

**Why abandoned:** Any such bound still requires exactly the inputs the estimate technique already needs (edge, odds, bet size as a fraction of net worth) — it adds a formula, not a fact, and does not strip away a convention to reveal a fixed ceiling the way theoretical-limit is meant to. The runway floor in chain C1 already does the load-bearing work of a ruin constraint using plainer, more checkable inputs.

**What it ruled out:** A dedicated theoretical-limit chain; recorded instead in the Techniques-not-applied block rather than developed into a fifth chain.
