## §4 Derivation Chains

### 4.1 Estimate pass — per-service operational overhead (Fermi/dimensional analysis)

**Target quantity and units:** recurring weekly engineer-hours consumed by "run the fleet" operational tax, as a function of N (number of independently deployable services), expressed also as a percentage of total team capacity.

**Decomposition (unit-factor cancellation):**

```
overhead(N) [eng-hr/wk] = N × (CI/CD + on-call/monitoring + infra provisioning) [eng-hr/wk per service]
                          + C(N,2) × (cross-service debugging) [eng-hr/wk per interface-pair]
```

C(N,2) = N(N−1)/2 is GT-7's combinatorial identity — the number of potential pairwise service interfaces.

**First-principles/defensible-range values per factor** (no direct measurement exists for this team; each is flagged per the estimate procedure's "assumed with a defensible range" allowance and recorded as **A13**):

| Factor | Conservative | Central | Aggressive |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI/CD pipeline maintenance (hr/wk/service) | 1.0 | 2.0 | 3.0 |
| On-call/monitoring surface (hr/wk/service) | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.5 |
| Infra provisioning/config (hr/wk/service) | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 |
| **Per-service subtotal** | **2.5** | **4.5** | **7.0** |
| Cross-service debugging (hr/wk/pair) | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.8 |

**Central-magnitude computation**, for three points on the spectrum (N=1 monolith/modular monolith, N=3 partial extraction, N=8 full microservices ≈ one service per bounded context):

- N=1: 1×4.5 + C(1,2)×0.5 = 4.5 + 0 = **4.5 hr/wk**
- N=3: 3×4.5 + C(3,2)×0.5 = 13.5 + 1.5 = **15.0 hr/wk**
- N=8: 8×4.5 + C(8,2)×0.5 = 36.0 + 14.0 = **50.0 hr/wk**

**Team capacity denominator:** GT-9's 6 engineers × 40 hr/wk (standard week, folded into **A14**) = 240 eng-hr/wk.

**Bracket (conservative → aggressive), against that 240 hr/wk denominator:**

| N | Lower (conservative) | Central | Upper (aggressive) | As % of 240 hr/wk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.5 hr | 4.5 hr | 7.0 hr | 1.0% – 1.9% – 2.9% |
| 3 | 8.4 hr | 15.0 hr | 23.4 hr | 3.5% – 6.25% – 9.75% |
| 8 | 28.4 hr | 50.0 hr | 78.4 hr | 11.8% – 20.8% – 32.7% |

(N=3 conservative: 3×2.5 + 3×0.3 = 8.4. N=3 aggressive: 3×7.0 + 3×0.8 = 23.4. N=8 conservative: 8×2.5 + 28×0.3 = 28.4. N=8 aggressive: 8×7.0 + 28×0.8 = 78.4.)

**Decision-resolution stop criterion (A14: ~15% sustainability threshold):**
N=3's entire bracket (3.5%–9.75%) sits below 15% — the estimate resolves the decision cleanly for partial extraction: it does not consume an unsustainable share of capacity at any bracket end. N=8's bracket (11.8%–32.7%) **straddles** 15% at its conservative end but clears it decisively at central and aggressive ends. Per the estimate procedure, a bracket that straddles a threshold should be tightened or the uncertainty escalated explicitly rather than silently resolved — this is escalated explicitly here (not silently resolved): full decomposition's viability under the *most* conservative reading is a live open question the estimate cannot fully close, and this uncertainty is carried forward into chain C1's confidence rating and into A13/A14's verification paths rather than papered over.

### 4.2 Trade-off analysis — monolith vs. modular monolith vs. partial extraction vs. full microservices

## Options

- **A — Unmodified monolith (status quo):** the current single deployable, continued with no deliberate internal-boundary investment (GT-11? hedges its current internal state, so "status quo" is treated conservatively as unmanaged/unmodularized).
- **B — Modular monolith:** the current single deployable, with enforced internal module boundaries (mechanically checkable — e.g., a CI dependency-direction lint) along the product's real domain seams. Still one deploy pipeline.
- **C — Partial service extraction:** 1–2 genuinely separable subdomains extracted as independently deployable services (N≈3 total deployables); the remainder stays a (modular) monolith, owned by the same single team.
- **D — Full microservices:** decomposition into services matching bounded contexts (N≈8, order-of-magnitude consistent with typical domain decomposition at this scale), each independently deployable.

**Must-haves (knockouts, applied before scoring):**

- **MH1 — Operable by the current 6-engineer team (GT-9) without a dedicated platform/SRE function.** Grounded in GT-6's stated prerequisites (rapid provisioning, monitoring, rapid deployment) and the fact that dedicating an engineer to a platform role removes them from product work in a 6-person total org.
- **MH2 — Does not require splitting the single team into multiple team-shaped ownership units to avoid a Conway's-Law mismatch.** Grounded in GT-3 + GT-4 + GT-9.

| Must-have | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MH1 (operable without dedicated platform role) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **✗** — §4.1's bracket puts N=8 at 11.8%–32.7% of total capacity, exceeding a sustainable tax (A14) at its central and aggressive ends, with GT-6's prerequisites unconfirmed as present (A12) |
| MH2 (no forced team split / Conway's-Law mirror) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (extracted service(s) stay owned by the same single team) | **✗** — 8 independently owned services with no corresponding team boundary is the textbook mismatch GT-3/GT-4 predict |

**Option D is knocked out on two independent grounds (MH1 and MH2), not scored.**

## Criteria & Weights

| Criterion (phrased so higher = better) | Weight | 1 means… | 5 means… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery velocity / deploy independence | 4 | frequent cross-feature coordination blocks every deploy | any engineer ships independently without blocking others |
| Operational overhead sustainability | 5 | ops tax >20% of team capacity (§4.1) | ops tax <5% of team capacity (§4.1) |
| Maintainability / boundary clarity | 4 | no enforced boundaries, coupling grows unchecked | boundaries enforced and mechanically checkable (lint/dependency-direction rules) |
| Blast-radius / fault isolation | 2 | one bug can degrade the entire product | failures contained to a narrow domain slice |
| Cost (infra spend) | 2 | spend scales ~linearly with N deployables | single runtime footprint, minimal redundant infra |
| Optionality (future extraction ease) | 3 | extracting later requires a full rewrite | boundaries already drawn; extraction is a deployment change only |
| Team cognitive load (6-person team) | 3 | must hold a distributed system's failure modes in mind for routine feature work | reason about one runtime for routine feature work |

Operational-overhead sustainability carries the highest weight (5) because it is the criterion §4.1's Fermi estimate was built to inform, and it is the axis on which this team's specific constraint (GT-9: 6 engineers, no stated platform function) binds hardest.

## Recommendation

**B (modular monolith) wins, 91 vs. C's 85 vs. A's 64; D is knocked out.**

**Flip test:** the smallest single-criterion weight change that flips B vs. C was checked exhaustively across all seven criteria within the 1–5 scale. The per-criterion score gaps (scoreB − scoreC) are: velocity −1, ops-overhead +1, maintainability 0, blast-radius −1, cost +2, optionality 0, cognitive load +1. B's total lead is 6 points. For every criterion, moving its weight to the boundary of the 1–5 scale in the direction that helps C most still leaves B ahead (e.g., maxing velocity's weight from 4→5 only closes the gap by 1 point, to 5; maxing cost down from 2→1 only closes it by 2, to 4). **No single-criterion weight change within the 1–5 scale flips this result** — the strongest result this test can return.

## §5 Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: Adopt full microservices now, ahead of any stated need

**What was tried:** Evaluating full decomposition (Option D, N≈8) as the primary recommendation, on the theory that decomposing early avoids a costlier future migration.

**Why abandoned:** Knocked out on two independent must-haves in the trade-off (§4.2): MH1 (C1's overhead bracket puts N=8 at 11.8%–32.7% of team capacity, exceeding sustainable tax at central/aggressive ends, with GT-6's prerequisites unconfirmed via A12) and MH2 (GT-3/GT-4/GT-9 predict a structural Conway's-Law mismatch when 8 services have no corresponding team boundary).

**What it ruled out:** Re-litigating "just decompose now to be safe" as a serious contender without first clearing both the operational-capacity and org-structure must-haves — chains C1 and C2 in §4 do this ruling-out explicitly.

### Dead End 2: A big-bang microservices rewrite, even if decomposition were eventually warranted

**What was tried:** Considering an all-at-once cutover to microservices as the migration mechanism, in case a future reversal condition fires.

**Why abandoned:** Directly contradicted by GT-5 — DORA's guidance explicitly recommends the incremental "strangler fig" pattern over big-bang rewrites, and states the loosely-coupled-architecture benefit is achievable independent of the underlying technology.

**What it ruled out:** Treating "decompose" and "rewrite everything at once" as the same decision. Any future extraction, should a reversal condition in C4 fire, should proceed incrementally (strangler fig) rather than as a wholesale rewrite.

### Dead End 3: Partial extraction (Option C) as the primary recommendation, beating modular monolith

**What was tried:** Scoring suggested C (85) was close to B (91) — a 6-point gap — raising the question of whether C should be the recommendation instead.

**Why abandoned:** The trade-off's flip test (§4.2 Recommendation) checked every single-criterion weight change within the 1–5 scale and found none that closes the 6-point gap; the result is robust, not a near-tie requiring score refinement.

**What it ruled out:** Treating the B-vs-C margin as fragile or as an artifact of arbitrary weighting. Chain C2 cites this ruling-out directly.

### Dead End 4: "Team structure doesn't matter for architecture, only technical factors do"

**What was tried:** Considering whether Conway's Law/team-topologies reasoning (GT-3, GT-4) should be treated as a soft heuristic rather than a load-bearing input to the org-structure must-have and reversal trigger.

**Why abandoned:** GT-3 is not a single claim but an empirically corroborated relationship (the MIT/HBS "mirroring hypothesis," independently replicated by two further studies per the locked Ground Truths). Discarding it as non-binding would require discarding multiply-replicated empirical evidence in favor of an unstated alternative theory with no cited support.

**What it ruled out:** Treating C3's org-structure trigger as optional or as a mere convention. Chain C3 cites this ruling-out.

### Dead End 5: Purely qualitative, pain-point-only reversal triggers (no numeric thresholds)

**What was tried:** Considering whether the four reversal conditions should be left qualitative ("revisit when it feels painful") rather than tied to specific numbers, on the theory that a numeric threshold is arbitrary and pain is the real signal.

**Why abandoned:** The Problem Essence's own success criterion 3 requires "named, checkable" reversal conditions — a purely qualitative "wait for pain" trigger is not checkable in the sense the essence statement demands, and A2/A4 (locked, Phase 2) already flag that no pain point is currently known or stated, so a pain-point-only trigger currently has nothing to watch for.

**What it ruled out:** Substituting vague future judgment for the four concrete, numeric/structural triggers in chain C4. Numeric thresholds and situational judgment are not mutually exclusive — the quarterly-review discipline in C4's fifth hop is exactly where qualitative signals (if they emerge) would also be considered — but the recommendation does not rest on qualitative judgment alone.
