## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Reading "measured across three runs" as three confirmatory measurements of one fixed number

**What was tried:** Interpreting the phrase as validating a single reported yield figure via three repeated measurements, rather than as two three-run samples (baseline vs. catalyst).

**Why abandoned:** This reading collapses the analysis into a measurement-precision question and conflicts with ordinary usage of "yield rose from 62% to 71%... measured across three runs," which most naturally describes two three-run samples. It also conflicts with the analysis the user explicitly requested (a between-condition comparison with confound/causal-attribution questions), which presupposes the two-sample reading as primary while treating the alternative as a live ambiguity (captured instead as A1, not as a fully discarded path).

**What it ruled out:** Prevents mis-analyzing the problem as single-number repeatability rather than as the between-condition comparison the confound and causal-attribution questions require.

### Dead End: Treating the ~14.5% relative gain (9/62) as self-evidently practically significant

**What was tried:** Concluding practical/economic significance directly from the relative-percentage size of the gap.

**Why abandoned:** Practical/economic significance is not a property of the percentage gap alone (GT-9, C5) — it depends on unstated cost structure. Concluding significance from relative-gap size alone would be reasoning by convention ("a double-digit percentage always matters") rather than from actual economics, violating the no-analogy-as-direct-evidence discipline.

**What it ruled out:** Rules out treating "12-15% relative improvement" as an automatic significance trigger; establishes that C5's information-gap conclusion, not a magnitude heuristic, is correct.
