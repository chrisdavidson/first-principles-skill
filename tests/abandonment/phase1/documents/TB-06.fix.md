## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that AI writing assistants make "unaided output" unverifiable in an unsupervised setting, what assessment design should a university use to verify, for each specific competence claim its credential makes, whether that claim requires unaided performance — rather than choosing uniformly between "timed exam" and "take-home"?

Reached via 5-Whys (causal mode): Why require individual assessment? → to produce a graded credential component. Why must the grade be tied to individually assessed performance? → the credential certifies the grade reflects the student's own demonstrated competence. Why must competence be the student's *own*? → downstream users (employers, licensors, grad programs) rely on the credential as a proxy instead of re-verifying every individual themselves. Why does that proxy function require re-verification-avoidance at scale? → re-testing everyone is prohibitively costly; credentialing exists to amortize verification cost. Why must that amortized verification specifically be *unaided*? → **this is where the chain forks, not reduces further**: "unaided" is only required for the subset of competencies where the real-world target task is itself performed unaided (e.g., a surgeon's hands); for competencies where real-world performance is legitimately tool-augmented, "unaided" is a convention, not the credential's actual root requirement.

**Success criteria** (checkable against section 6):
1. The recommendation names at least one assessment component that is synchronous and examiner-observed.
2. The recommendation does not rely on AI-text-detection software as the primary integrity mechanism for any high-stakes claim.
3. The recommendation distinguishes which claims require unaided demonstration from which permit disclosed AI-augmented performance.
4. The recommendation names at least one equity risk it does not fully resolve, plus a mitigation.
5. The recommendation states an observable condition that would falsify it.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Uniform return to timed in-person exams for all assessment
**What was tried:** Evaluated O2 in the trade-off (chain C6) as a full replacement for take-home across every course and assessment type.
**Why abandoned:** Scored 86 vs. the hybrid's 100, driven by a construct-validity failure (1/5 on competency coverage) for iterative, research-based, and tool-augmented competencies (A10, discarded/challenged); also carries the equity cost of test-anxiety confounds surfaced in the Phase-2 inversion pass.
**What it ruled out:** "Just go back to how it was before take-home existed" as a low-cost fix — ruled out because it degrades the credential's ability to certify a whole class of real-world competencies, independent of the AI question entirely.

### Dead End: Keep unrestricted take-home, rely on AI-detection software as the safeguard
**What was tried:** Evaluated O3 — unrestricted take-home plus mandatory AI-detection screening and a disciplinary process for flagged submissions.
**Why abandoned:** Knocked out at the must-have stage in C6 (fails low-legal-exposure requirement); GT-2?/GT-3 (chain C2) show detector evidence is less reliable than vendor claims and demographically skewed; C3's falling-ceiling argument shows this is not a temporary engineering gap.
**What it ruled out:** The intuitively cheapest fix — "buy a detection tool, keep everything else the same" — ruled out as actively unsafe from a disparate-impact and appeals-exposure standpoint, not merely suboptimal.

### Dead End: Status quo — unrestricted take-home, no explicit AI policy
**What was tried:** Evaluated O1 (the required baseline/do-nothing option) in the trade-off.
**Why abandoned:** Knocked out at the must-have stage in C6 — with unrestricted AI access and no verification mechanism, no unaided-competence claim can be supported for any take-home artifact (A1, discarded as a blanket claim; GT-1? shows this was already fragile pre-AI).
**What it ruled out:** "Wait and see, nothing has fundamentally changed" as a defensible position for any assessment carrying an unaided-competence claim.

### Dead End: Ban AI outright in take-home settings via honor-code language alone
**What was tried:** A narrower fix than full detection — prohibiting AI use in the syllabus without adding detection or any verification component.
**Why abandoned:** A9 (such bans are enforceable) is false given GT-3 combined with the structural absence of any proctoring mechanism in an unsupervised setting (C4's contrast case) — compliance cannot be verified at all.
**What it ruled out:** Symbolic policy language with no verification mechanism, functionally equivalent to the status-quo dead end above.

## 6. Conclusion

Synthesizing the chains above:

**Recommended approach:** Reject the "timed exam vs. take-home" framing entirely; adopt a hybrid, stakes-differentiated assessment design that assigns synchronous, examiner-observed verification (oral defense, live check-back, proctored components) to the specific claims a course's credential must certify as unaided competence, and redesigns take-home/project work — with a disclosed-AI-use policy and spot-audited live defense — around claims where competent, tool-augmented performance is itself the target skill (chain C5).

**Key insight:** The actual failure point is not that take-home work lacks proctoring — it is that post-hoc AI-text detection is being asked to do a job it is structurally unable to do reliably today, and structurally worse at over time, while a synchronous/observed component sidesteps the detection problem entirely regardless of how good AI generation becomes (chains C2/C3/C4).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This recommendation trades detection-based scalability for added live-verification cost and a new coaching-access equity risk that mirrors the AI-tiered-access risk it addresses (chain C6; second-order extension of chain C5, Cluster C of the pre-mortem). It also assumes the criteria weights used in the trade-off approximate typical institutional priorities, which will vary by field and institution (chain C6, `[Assumes: A13]`), and it does not fully close the covert real-time AI-relay rival on live exams (chain C4) — mitigated by standard anti-device protocols, not eliminated.
