## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Treating 11 months of savings as itself the risk metric
**What was tried:** Framing downside risk directly as "is 11 months a big or small number" in isolation.
**Why abandoned:** This restates head input GT-2 without producing a new inference; once GT-6 is introduced, the more precise reemployment-time-relative metric (chain C1) is available and materially changes the picture.
**What it ruled out:** The naive verdict "11 months feels comfortable/uncomfortable" as a standalone conclusion — C1 supersedes it.

### Dead End: Naive equity valuation (% ownership × imagined future valuation)
**What was tried:** A back-of-envelope equity value using an assumed grant % against an assumed optimistic exit valuation, with no probability weighting.
**Why abandoned:** This is exactly the convention challenged as A-EQVALUE; it produces figures roughly an order of magnitude above what GT-7's probability-weighted model supports.
**What it ruled out:** Any headline equity figure quoted without dilution, vesting, exercise cost, taxes, and failure probability applied (chain C2).

### Dead End: Survivorship-bias-driven "just say yes"
**What was tried:** Considered whether well-known anecdotes of early joiners at tiny startups who did extremely well should function as a rival conclusion favoring an unconditional "yes."
**Why abandoned:** Anecdote is not evidence under the no-analogies-as-direct-evidence rule; GT-6 and GT-8? together rule this out as a basis for a general recommendation, though they do not rule out that this specific startup could still be a good outcome.
**What it ruled out:** Using survivorship-biased narratives as a rival to the base-rate-grounded analysis in C1/C2 — this is the headline rival addressed in the Phase 5 Rival step.

### Dead End: Producing a single dollar-denominated verdict (accept iff equity EV > forgone income)
**What was tried:** Reducing the decision to one inequality — expected equity value (C2) versus cumulative forgone income (C3) — and recommending accordingly.
**Why abandoned:** C2's own confidence is LOW and only bracketed to "low tens of thousands," while C3 states the true forgone-income figure is unbounded above without benefits data; comparing a wide-bracketed LOW estimate to an unbounded-above one produces false precision the ground truths do not support.
**What it ruled out:** A single-number verdict as this analysis's deliverable — this is why C4/C5 and the Conclusion frame the answer as a decision framework and diligence list rather than a yes/no.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Do not accept the offer as literally proposed today; spend one to two weeks closing four information gaps, then re-run this same weighted framework with real numbers instead of placeholders (chain C4).
- Get the actual equity term sheet — grant %, vesting/cliff, strike price, latest valuation, cap-table dilution stack (chain C2).
- Recompute true runway using post-move burn rate, including lost health-insurance subsidy and 401(k) match (chain C3).
- Get an honest, subject-specific read on reemployment liquidity in your own role and location rather than relying on the national median (chain C1).
- Test whether the founders will tolerate a structured, time-boxed counter-proposal without withdrawing the offer (chain C4).

**Key insight:** The two numbers that feel most decision-relevant in the framing — "11 months of savings" and "30% pay cut for equity" — are both the wrong units: runway should be measured against reemployment time, not burn-rate coverage alone (chain C1), and equity should be measured as a probability-weighted expected value net of dilution and failure, not as a percentage of an imagined future valuation (chain C2).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** Joining exactly as proposed ranks lowest of five realistic options on the stated criteria (chain C4), but it is also the option that most fully captures the career-capital and ownership-experience upside identified in the second-order analysis (chain C5); negotiating or waiting preserves downside protection and optionality at the likely cost of losing some or all of that specific upside if the founders will not wait (chain C5).
