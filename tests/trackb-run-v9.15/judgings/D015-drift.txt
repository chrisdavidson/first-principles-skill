## Decision usefulness

The document doesn't hand the reader a "move" or "don't move" verdict, but that's the right call for a question this contingent — instead it converges on an actionable default: don't make the maximal, permanent decision now; take the smallest reversible step consistent with the actual (not anticipated) need, escalating only on defined triggers. It specifies who this is for (a family weighing relocation near aging parents) and under what conditions the recommendation shifts (active decline vs. anticipatory worry; high uncertainty vs. confirmed need). The closing five-point list and the named failure mode ("making a maximal, permanent decision to resolve a diffuse emotional state") give a reader concrete things to check their own reasoning against and act on. This clears the bar for **Rigorous**.

## Evidence grounding

Most of the document's claims are asserted from general plausibility rather than derived or sourced: "eldercare need is not static — it escalates," "moving is the most expensive way to solve most of them," "a cross-country move... is close to irreversible in practice." The reasoning has internal coherence, but the factual scaffolding it leans on is presented as fact without visible support anywhere in the piece. That lands at **Hand-wavy**.

## Calibration

But a few claims are stated with more flatness than their support warrants — "moving is the most expensive way to solve most of them" and "close to irreversible in practice" are delivered as settled rather than as judgment calls. The mismatch is occasional rather than pervasive, so this sits at **Sound**.

This gives a reader something to actually watch for and check against, not just an acknowledgment that things could change. That specificity earns **Rigorous**.
