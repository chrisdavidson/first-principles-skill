## Criterion assessments

**1. Decision usefulness — Rigorous.** The document doesn't just weigh in on "exam vs. take-home"; it dissolves the binary and gives a rule a reader can apply: audit each assessment against which of three purposes (certification, formative feedback, behavioral steering) it serves, then route accordingly. The routing itself is concrete — supervised formats for certification-critical courses, redesigned take-homes (disclosed AI use, evaluation of critical scrutiny rather than prose fluency, personalized prompts, oral defense) for research/synthesis assessment, and explicit retirement of the unmodified unsupervised take-home essay. A reader designing a course's assessment could execute this directly.

**2. Evidence grounding — Sound.** Most of the argument is carried by visible logical derivation rather than by assertion — the (a)/(b) threat-model test is defined and then applied transparently to each format, so the reader can check the reasoning step by step. But a couple of load-bearing factual claims escape that treatment: that chatbot access is "now ~universal" and that a grader has "no reliable way to tell" AI-assisted work from understanding are both stated flatly, with no basis given, even though support (adoption figures, detector failure rates) would have been easy to cite and both claims do real work holding up the conclusion.

**3. Assumption surfacing — Sound.** The strongest move in the piece is unbundling "timed exam" and "take-home" into three independent axes (time pressure, supervision, tool access) and showing the question conflates them — this surfaces and directly engages the most consequential hidden assumption in the prompt itself. Other assumptions the document's own conclusion leans on — that supervision reliably blocks AI-assisted work, that oral defenses actually re-establish authorship, that practitioners' real-world tool use validates redesigned take-homes — are simply asserted as part of the recommendation rather than flagged and tested.

**4. Calibration — Hand-wavy.** The document is written in a uniformly confident, declarative register throughout ("the threat model doesn't touch it," "the grader has no reliable way to tell," "these are the formats LLMs haven't degraded"), with only scattered soft qualifiers ("typical," "~universal," "ideally") that don't map onto any visible distinction between well-supported and speculative claims. Nothing in the piece signals that, say, the axis-decoupling logic is on firmer ground than the empirical claim about universal chatbot access — both are delivered with the same flat certainty.

**5. Falsifiability — Absent.** Nowhere does the document say what observation, data, or outcome would overturn its conclusion or reveal the recommendation to be wrong. The closest it comes is scope-bounding language ("unless the take-home is redesigned around the new reality") describing when the recommendation applies, not what would falsify it. The argument is presented as a settled piece of logical analysis with no acknowledgement that its premises (e.g., about detection reliability or the effectiveness of oral defenses) could themselves turn out false.
