## Criterion 1: Decision usefulness

The document lands on an explicit, actionable position: land area is a non-binding input to full solar-PV electrification for a moderate-or-lower-density 10-million-person country, with planning effort redirected toward storage/firming, grid buildout, siting economics, and capital. It states who this applies to (explicitly scoped away from small, land-scarce, high-density outliers after the adversarial pass surfaced that as a live rival) and under what conditions it holds (annual-average energy balance, not minute-by-minute supply). A reader deciding whether to worry about land availability when planning solar buildout has a clear, boundary-conditioned answer to act on, not a survey of possibilities.

## Criterion 2: Evidence grounding

Every numeric claim traces to a named source, and the document is unusually disciplined about distinguishing three tiers of support: read-at-source (quoted directly, e.g. GT-1, GT-2, GT-4), reported-by-delegate (search-summarized, not opened, e.g. GT-5, GT-6, GT-9), and outright unverified domain knowledge (GT-11). The assumptions table (A1–A12) names each assumption's type, and critically, the single most consequential one — A2, that land is computed from annual energy balance rather than instantaneous demand-matching — is not just flagged but actively tested: it is bounded in chain C8's second- and third-order extensions (what grid operators, policymakers, and landowners actually have to do to cover that gap), and the document explicitly checks what happens to the conclusion if A2 fails (the conclusion strengthens rather than breaks).
