## 1. Problem Essence

**Essence Statement:** This is not a binary "stay or go" choice — it is a question of how to
allocate financial runway, negotiate terms, and stage a commitment so as to maximize expected
long-run career and financial value while keeping the downside recoverable, given that the
person is converting a portion of guaranteed cash compensation and continued employment into
equity of uncertain, back-loaded, and probably-modest expected value.

**Success criteria a correct answer must achieve:**
- Correctly prices the financial downside — not the pay cut in isolation, but the compounded
  risk of startup failure plus job-search time relative to the stated runway.
- Values the equity realistically — discounted for dilution, exit-probability base rates, and
  illiquidity — rather than at its undiscounted, pitch-deck "if we get acquired for $X" figure.
- Treats the decision space as more than two options: tests negotiated, staged, and hybrid paths
  before comparing "stay" against "go."
- Accounts for non-monetary effects — career capital, optionality, and re-employability — not
  compensation alone.
- Produces a decision framework that names the conditions under which "join," "stay," or a
  negotiated middle path is correct, rather than asserting a single verdict independent of facts
  this analysis does not have access to (this specific startup's cap table, the person's
  household obligations and true risk tolerance).

No success criterion above requires the answer to be exactly "stay" or exactly "go" — each states
what the answer must achieve, checked against that requirement before leaving this phase.
