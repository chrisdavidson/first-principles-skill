## 1. Problem Essence

This is not a binary "stay or go" choice — it's how to allocate financial runway, negotiate terms, and stage a commitment to maximize expected long-run career/financial value while keeping the downside recoverable, given you're converting guaranteed cash comp into equity of uncertain, back-loaded, probably-modest expected value.

**Success criteria:** correctly price the financial downside as *startup failure + job-search time relative to runway*, not the pay cut alone; value equity realistically (discounted for dilution, exit-probability base rates, illiquidity); treat the decision space as more than two options; account for non-monetary effects (career capital, optionality, re-employability); produce a decision framework naming conditions under which each path is correct, rather than a single verdict independent of facts this analysis lacks.

## 2. Assumptions Table (18 total)

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Negotiate a composite — smaller cut (~15%), more equity/accelerated vesting, a short trial period, and a 6-month milestone checkpoint tied to observable startup health (runway secured, product-market signal, team stability).

**Key insight:** The dominant risk isn't the 30% cut in isolation — it's the combination of startup failure *and* a job search exceeding your 11-month runway. This reframes the real question from "can I absorb a pay cut" to "how many months would re-employment realistically take, and does my runway cover that with margin."

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The equity is very likely worth substantially less than its headline pitch once realistic exit probabilities and dilution are applied. Separately, a staged/hedged ask carries real risk — it can signal reduced commitment to a small team and could shrink or cost the offer, so frame it as mutual risk-reduction with a fallback to full commitment.

**Confidence: LOW overall.** The general framework (don't binarize, price job-search timing not the cut, discount headline equity, negotiate a staged commitment) is robust and would likely survive most factual specifics. The person-specific numeric verdict is LOW because key inputs are missing: this startup's cap table/valuation/preference stack, exact vesting terms, your true household burn rate, and whether dependents affect your risk tolerance.

## Pre-mortem: how this goes wrong in 6 months

Five structural failure clusters were identified, each with a disposition:
1. **Cliff/vesting timing** — leaving or being let go before the 1-year cliff yields zero equity. → *Plan change:* negotiate accelerated vesting on involuntary termination without cause; price a pre-cliff departure as a real total-loss scenario.
2. **Preference-stack/dilution risk** — "successful" exits can still pay common holders little. → *Plan change:* get the cap table and preference terms before finalizing.
3. **Negotiation-signaling risk** — the safety mechanism itself could cost you the offer. → *Accepted risk, mitigated:* frame the trial as time-boxed and collaborative; be ready to go full commitment if it's a dealbreaker.
4. **Runway mismatch** — true job-search duration after failure is unknown. → *Plan change:* confirm the 11-month figure against bare-bones burn, and identify a reduced-burn contingency.
5. **Role/pivot risk** — a 4-person startup's specific role may not survive even if the company does. → *Accepted risk, mitigated:* use the trial/first 60–90 days to directly assess fit rather than relying on the pitch.

**Falsification condition:** The conclusion flips from "negotiate a composite" to "decline outright" if, after obtaining the real cap table, vesting terms, and runway data, the equity shows near-zero realistic value **and** no negotiation is possible **and** your true minimum runway floor isn't met net of the pay cut.
