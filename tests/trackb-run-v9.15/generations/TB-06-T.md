## Answer

**Recommendation:** Do not fully replace timed exams with take-home assessments, and do not leave the pure-timed-exam status quo unchanged either — adopt a hybrid: a grade-load-bearing secured/timed component, take-home format for extended/authentic tasks, and an unpredictable, accommodation-inclusive sampled oral defense, with its integrity resting on the secured component and the viva, never on AI-text detection (chain C4).

**Band (from §6):** MEDIUM.

**Would change it:** A primary-source read of the retrieval-practice and accommodation-efficacy literature (chain C4); institution-specific evidence that viva-sampling or secured-component resourcing cannot be sustained (chain C5); or evidence that real-time AI coaching capable of defeating live oral defense has emerged (chain C5).

## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that generative AI can now produce unsupervised written work that is largely undetectable and often graded higher than genuine student writing, does replacing timed, invigilated written exams with take-home assessments preserve — or degrade — a university's ability to certify that a student's degree reflects that student's own demonstrated competence, and is there a format allocation that does better than either named option alone?

**Success criteria:**
1. The recommendation states, for each named option (timed-exam-only, take-home-only, and any hybrid/composite), whether it preserves the link between the credential and the individual student's own demonstrated competence, given current (2026) AI writing capability and current (2026) AI-detection reliability.
2. The recommendation names the specific assessment format(s) assigned to each of the competing goods this decision must balance — integrity/certification validity, retention/pedagogical value, authentic-task representativeness, and equity/accessibility — rather than declaring one goal an unconditional winner over the others.
3. The recommendation states an observable condition under which the recommended format mix would change (a falsification / "would change it" condition), so the answer is not a permanent verdict dressed as a first-principles conclusion.

## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1** In a blind study, GPT-4-generated submissions were injected into unsupervised, window-timed take-home exams across five undergraduate psychology modules at a UK university (Short Answer Questions: 2.5-hour window, 200-word answers; essays: 8-hour window, 1,500 words). 94% of the AI submissions went undetected by examiners (97% under stricter criteria requiring explicit mention of AI); AI submissions scored on average half a grade boundary higher than real students; there was an 83.4% chance that AI submissions on a given module would outperform a random selection of real student submissions of the same size. — source: Scarfe, Watcham, Clarke & Roesch, "A real-world test of artificial intelligence infiltration of a university examinations system: A 'Turing Test' case study," *PLOS ONE*, 2024; read-at-source: full text, PMC11206930, Results/Discussion sections (figures quoted directly).
- **GT-2** Across seven widely used AI-text detectors tested on 91 TOEFL essays written by non-native English speakers, the average false-positive rate (genuine human writing flagged as AI-generated) was 61.22%; on 88 US 8th-grade essays by native English writers, the average false-positive rate was approximately 5.19%. — source: Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu & Zou, published in *Patterns* (2023); read-at-source: arXiv:2304.02819v3, full text, Results section (figures quoted directly).
- **GT-3** In a survey of 1,041 UK undergraduates (fielded by Savanta), 88% reported using generative AI tools for assessments in 2025 (up from 53% in 2024); 18% reported including AI-generated text directly in submitted work; 76% believed their institution would detect AI use if it occurred. — source: HEPI/Kortext Student Generative AI Survey 2025; read-at-source: hepi.ac.uk release page, 2025-02-26 (figures quoted directly).
- **GT-4** GPT-4 scored approximately 297/400 on the Uniform Bar Exam, within the 68th-90th percentile of human test-takers depending on jurisdiction and exam administration; it scored 4.2/6.0 on both the Multistate Essay Examination and the Multistate Performance Test (a score of 4+ is generally passing); it scored 75.7% on the multiple-choice component, exceeding the average human test-taker by more than 7 points. — source: Katz, Bommarito, Gao & Arredondo, "GPT-4 Passes the Bar Exam," published study; read-at-source: full text, PMC10894685 (figures quoted directly).
- **GT-5?** Retrieval-practice/testing-effect meta-analytic literature reports that information retrieved under recall/test conditions is recalled on subsequent tests roughly 2.5x more often than information reviewed via restudying, with benefits persisting over weeks to years and replicated across ages and content domains. — cited to: multiple meta-analyses summarized in web-search results (e.g., work associated with Roediger/Karpicke-line testing-effect research); reported-by-delegate: search-engine summary; cited sources not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-6?** Contract-cheating prevalence research reports self-reported contract cheating rising from a historic average of approximately 3.5% to approximately 15.7% in samples from 2014 onward; unsupervised/take-home assessment format is specifically named in this literature as expanding opportunities for collusion or outsourcing; one cited institution (UNSW) saw detected cheating rates roughly double post-COVID (approximately 2% to approximately 4.5%), coinciding with expanded remote/take-home assessment. — cited to: Newton-line meta-analytic literature, *Frontiers in Education* and related sources; reported-by-delegate: search-engine summary; cited sources not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-7** A proposal for randomly sampling approximately 10% of students per assessment for a short oral defense (viva), as a scalable integrity/comprehension check layered onto existing quality-assurance processes, explicitly conditioned on building in "clear guidelines for extenuating circumstances and reasonable adjustments" for disability, anxiety, and language barriers from the outset. — source: "Sampled vivas are pivotal in combating AI cheating," *Times Higher Education*; read-at-source: full article text, timeshighereducation.com/node/735603 (proposal and conditions quoted/paraphrased directly from the fetched text).
- **GT-8?** Test-anxiety/disability-accommodation research indicates extended time is the most frequently requested and granted postsecondary testing accommodation, and that timed conditions are commonly cited as the primary anxiety trigger for test-anxious students; however, some studies report no significant difference in timed-task performance between students clinically diagnosed with anxiety/depression and controls, and equity researchers argue reactive, case-by-case accommodation does not by itself produce equitable outcomes at scale, recommending inclusive-by-default assessment design as a complement. — cited to: multiple sources summarized in web-search results (e.g., work hosted via Queen's University and Plymouth-affiliated repositories); reported-by-delegate: search-engine summary; cited sources not opened by this analysis.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Rely on AI-text detectors as the primary integrity safeguard for a take-home model

**What was tried:** Considered under assumption A1/A7 — keep take-home assessment, require submissions to clear an AI-detection tool as the safeguard against undetected substitution.

**Why abandoned:** GT-2 shows detectors are both statistically unreliable and specifically biased against non-native English speakers (61.22% average false-positive rate), and detector vendors are in an active, acknowledged arms race against paraphrasing/"humanizer" tools built specifically to defeat them (Turnitin's AIR-1 deployment). The failure is structural, not a temporary lag.

**What it ruled out:** Any recommendation of the form "keep take-home, but require AI-detector clearance" as a sufficient standalone integrity mechanism (chain C1).

### Dead End: Wait for better AI detectors before changing anything

**What was tried:** Considered treating detector-improvement timelines as a legitimate basis for deferring any decision — i.e., "do nothing yet."

**Why abandoned:** This is not actually a neutral no-cost choice. GT-1 is a direct, real-world measurement of the failure mode occurring today under a format that already exists (94% undetected substitution in an unsupervised, window-timed take-home exam), so deferral leaves a known-broken safeguard running rather than avoiding a cost.

**What it ruled out:** Treating "wait for the technology to improve" as equivalent to a safe default (feeds chain C1's rival-resolution).

### Dead End: Full replacement (Option A) — take-home for everything, honor-code enforcement only

**What was tried:** Evaluated as the literal option the original question names, initially considered for full weighted scoring alongside Options B and C.

**Why abandoned:** Eliminated at the trade-off procedure's must-have knockout stage (M1: integrity) rather than scored — GT-1 is a direct, real-world measurement of exactly this failure mode, not a hypothetical concern, so scoring it alongside viable options would have let a high score on unrelated criteria out-vote a hard constraint it fails outright.

**What it ruled out:** Answering the original question as posed with a simple "yes, replace" (see trade-off process output, Options/knockout).

### Dead End: Pure timed-exam-only status quo, kept as the default because it is the incumbent

**What was tried:** Considered defaulting to "leave timed exams as the sole/dominant format, unchanged" as the presumptively safe, conservative choice.

**Why abandoned:** Scored lower than the hybrid in the locked, weighted trade-off (76 vs. 81), driven primarily by Equity and Authentic-task-representativeness; the flip test shows this result is robust to all but an extreme, hard-to-justify devaluation of the Equity criterion (chain C4).

**What it ruled out:** Treating "keep the pre-AI status quo untouched" as automatically cost-free — it is not (chain C4; GT-8?).

### Dead End: Treat retrieval-practice value (GT-5?) as dispositive on its own

**What was tried:** While building chain C3, considered recommending pure timed exams on pedagogical grounds alone, using the testing-effect literature as sufficient justification.

**Why abandoned:** Pedagogical value is only one of six locked trade-off criteria and does not by itself settle the equity and authentic-task-representativeness costs of a pure-timed-exam regime; using a single ground truth to settle a multi-criteria decision would be reasoning by a cherry-picked fact rather than the full weighted analysis the decision's stakes require.

**What it ruled out:** A single-axis "timed exams win because retention" argument (chain C3 feeds chain C4 as one of six inputs, not as a standalone verdict).

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Do not replace timed exams outright with take-home assessments, and do not leave the pre-AI timed-exam-only status quo unchanged either — adopt a hybrid model: a grade-load-bearing secured/timed (invigilated or live-proctored) component, take-home format reserved for extended/authentic tasks, and an unpredictable, accommodation-inclusive, randomly sampled (~10%) oral defense layered on take-home submissions, with its integrity property deliberately designed to rest on the secured component and the viva rather than on AI-text detection (chain C4, chain C1).

**Key insight:** The intuitive framing of this decision as "timed exams vs. take-home" hides the fact that the safeguard most people assume would make take-home safe — AI-detection software — is not merely imperfect but measurably non-functional today, and fails worst against exactly the population (non-native English writers) it would be most damaging to wrongly accuse; a real-world blind study additionally shows the substitution problem is not hypothetical but already occurring undetected at a 94% rate in precisely this assessment format (chain C1, chain C2, chain C6).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The hybrid model does not deliver the retrieval-practice/retention benefit of an all-timed-exam regime at full strength (chain C3, chain C4 — Pedagogical value scored 4 vs. Option B's 5), and it costs more faculty time than either pure option, with its equity and integrity benefits conditional on real resourcing and design discipline that this analysis cannot verify any specific institution will actually deliver — no chain — flagged assumption only for the institution-specific claim that resourcing will in fact be sustained, since that lies outside this analysis's evidence base (chain C5 names the resourcing and design risks generally).

**Must-haves (knockouts):**
- M1 — must preserve the ability to certify the credential reflects the individual student's own demonstrated competence at a rate materially better than today's realistic detection capability.
- M2 — must not systematically disadvantage a protected group (non-native English speakers, disabled/anxious students) as an artifact of the chosen enforcement mechanism.
- M3 — must be operable at typical university scale (large lecture courses) without unbounded per-student cost.

**Knockout result:** Option A fails M1 — GT-1 directly measured 94% of AI submissions going undetected in exactly this format, with no auxiliary integrity mechanism specified; it also fails M2 on its own terms, since the standard fallback safeguard (AI detection) is shown by GT-2 to be biased against non-native English writers. **Option A is knocked out — not scored.** Options B and C pass all three must-haves (B trivially, as the pre-AI baseline; C conditionally, per A8-A13) and proceed to scoring.

## Recommendation

**C (hybrid) wins, 81 vs. 76, a margin of 5**, driven mainly by Equity (+8) and Authentic-task fit (+6), partly offset by Feasibility (−6) and Pedagogical value (−3).

**Flip test:** Integrity and AI-resilience are tied (0 diff) and can never flip the result by weight change alone. No other single-criterion move within the normal 1-5 range flips the winner. **Premise.** It is now three years since [this university] adopted the hybrid assessment model. The policy has failed — certification validity has not been restored, and the university faces a fresh integrity crisis, this time with less credibility to fix it because it already claimed to have solved the problem once.

**Causes** (unfiltered, from four viewpoints — administrator, instructor, student, rival/vendor):

*Administrator:* the secured/timed component's grade weight was negotiated down department-by-department until it was no longer load-bearing in several majors; no central budget line was created for viva administration, so it was funded out of existing TA hours that evaporated within two years; the "10% random sample" became "10% of whichever students show up to office hours," a self-selection bias defeating the design.

*Instructor:* running vivas for even 10% of a 300-person lecture course means roughly 30 oral exams per term with no rubric training provided, so grading became inconsistent examiner-to-examiner; faculty quietly stopped flagging suspected AI-written take-home work because misconduct hearings still leaned on AI-detector output as evidence and detector unreliability (GT-2) made hearings unwinnable, so referrals dropped even though the underlying behavior did not; large intro courses reverted the secured component to a light-weight "attendance quiz" rather than a real load-bearing exam, because writing and grading real secured content is unfunded extra labor.

*Student:* students discovered via shared forums which departments actually enforce viva sampling and which do not, and self-selected or complained their way into the lax ones; students used AI to pre-generate likely defense questions and answers from their own take-home essay before the viva, turning it into a memorization exercise rather than a genuine comprehension check; non-native-English and disabled students report the viva itself, not just the written exam, became their primary source of assessment anxiety, and the "accommodations built in from the start" promised by the policy were in practice a generic extended-time offer that did not address oral-communication-specific anxiety.

*Rival/vendor:* an ed-tech vendor released a real-time "interview coach" AI tool marketed for oral exams and interviews, delivered discreetly via earpiece or glasses-based display, within the three-year window; a competing university marketed "no oral defense required" as a selling point to prospective students who find vivas stressful, creating enrollment-driven pressure to water down the feature that gives the hybrid its integrity value.
