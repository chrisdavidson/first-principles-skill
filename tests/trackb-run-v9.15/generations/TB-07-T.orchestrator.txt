**Recommendation:** For a representative 10-million-person country of moderate-or-lower population density, land area is a solved, non-binding input to full solar-PV electrification: the computed requirement is roughly 60–3,600 km² (≈610 km² central case), well under 5%, typically under 1%, of a Portugal-sized country's territory. ## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** From first-principles physical quantities alone (solar irradiance, module efficiency, real-world performance losses, and panel-packing density on the ground) — not from analogy to what any specific country currently does — how much land area would a representative 10-million-person country need to devote to utility-scale solar PV generation to cover its total annual electricity consumption, and does the resulting figure make full solar-PV electrification land-constrained, or does it shift the binding constraint elsewhere?

**Success criteria:** (1) a land-area figure derived from an explicit physical chain, not asserted; (2) bracketed across high-latitude/high-consumption and sun-belt/low-consumption cases; (3) cross-checked against a real-world empirical land-use survey; (4) compared against a concrete national land area and land-use category; (5) an explicit statement of whether land is the binding constraint, and what the calculation leaves out.

## 4. Derivation Chains

**C1 — Land-yield, central case:** GHI(1,500) × efficiency(20%) × PR(82%) = 246 kWh/m² panel/yr → × GCR(40%) = **≈98 GWh/km²/yr**. *(MEDIUM)*

**C2 — Independent cross-check:** GT-1's 2.8 acres/GWh/yr converts to ≈88 GWh/km²/yr — agrees with C1 within **≈12%**, with the gap direction matching the expected efficiency improvement since the 2012–13 survey. *(MEDIUM)*

**C3 — Realistic yield range:** best case (desert GHI, high efficiency) ≈228 GWh/km²/yr; worst case (high-latitude, low efficiency) ≈45 GWh/km²/yr — a **~5-fold range**, driven mostly by latitude. *(MEDIUM)*

**C4 — Central-case land requirement:** 10M people × 6,000 kWh/yr (Germany-like) = 60,000 GWh/yr demand ÷ 98 GWh/km²/yr = **≈610 km²**. *(MEDIUM)*

**C5 — Full country-archetype range:** pairing best/worst yield with lowest/highest demand cases gives **≈62 km² to ≈3,580 km²**, a ~58-fold spread. *(MEDIUM)*

**C6 — Theoretical-limit sensitivity:** the absolute Shockley–Queisser thermodynamic ceiling (ideal GCR=PR=1) gives an ultimate floor of **≈74 km²**; a "best demonstrated" real-technology tier gives **≈325 km²**. Ordering: 610 km² (conventional) > 325 km² (best demonstrated) > 74 km² (ideal). Most of the reachable headroom is *already captured* by current commercial tech — land isn't going to shrink by orders of magnitude through better cells. *(MEDIUM)*

**C7 — As a share of national territory:** central case = **0.66%** of Portugal's land area; worst case = **3.9%**. In every scenario, well under 5%, typically under 1%. *(MEDIUM)*

**C8 — The headline finding:** Since agricultural land alone covers over a third of a typical country's territory, a ≈0.66% solar footprint is smaller still relative to already-disturbed land. **Land area is not the binding physical constraint** — but this rests on an annual-average-balance assumption (A2), not minute-by-minute supply. Second-order: grid operators must overbuild capacity and add storage/transmission beyond the land number; the real bottleneck shifts to permitting/interconnection-queue position; cheapest grid-adjacent sites get used first, creating local land-price/conflict pressure even as the national percentage stays low. *(LOW — see below)*

**C9 — Rooftop can't substitute:** even at a generous US-derived rooftop-potential ceiling (≈39% of demand), at least ≈61% of demand — and most of C4's land figure — still needs ground-mounted PV. *(MEDIUM)*

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

- **CSP instead of/alongside PV** — abandoned: the question specifies PV alone; CSP uses a different resource (DNI) and land-use profile.
- **Deriving land from nameplate capacity + generic capacity factor** — abandoned: that would reason from an already-aggregated industry statistic rather than first principles; reserved instead as the independent cross-check (C2).
- **Treating annual-average balance as sufficient evidence that land "solves" full solar supply** — abandoned: would silently assume unlimited storage/grid backstop; this is exactly why A2 is flagged everywhere and why C8's second-order extension exists.
- **Anchoring to a single named country** instead of a bracketed generic case — abandoned: the user asked for a generic case with latitude/consumption sensitivity; a single point estimate would hide the legitimate order-of-magnitude spread.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** For a representative, moderate-or-lower-density 10-million-person country (Portugal-like, not a small land-scarce, high-density outlier), treat land area as a **solved, non-binding input**. The computed requirement — roughly 60–3,600 km², ≈610 km² central case — is well under 5%, typically under 1%, of national territory and smaller still against agricultural land. Planning effort belongs elsewhere: diurnal/seasonal storage and firming capacity, grid and transmission buildout, siting/interconnection-queue economics, and capital/manufacturing throughput.

**Key insight:** The popular framing "solar needs too much land" inverts the actual constraint ordering. A first-principles physics estimate and an independent 2013 empirical survey agree within ≈12% that solar's land footprint is a rounding error against national territory — and even jumping to the absolute thermodynamic ceiling only shrinks the requirement a further ~8-fold, not by orders of magnitude, because most of the reachable headroom is already captured by current commercial modules. The question that actually gates full solar-only supply is not "is there enough land" but "how is the sun's absence at night and in winter covered" — and land-area arithmetic cannot answer that.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This analysis does not solve the storage/firming problem it identifies — the land figures are panel footprint under an annual-average-balance assumption, not a fully islanded minute-by-minute solar-only grid; real firming requires nameplate overbuild, weeks-scale storage, or import interconnection, each with its own uncosted footprint. The low end of the bracket rests on an unverified desert-irradiance figure, and the rooftop-offset figure is a single US-derived study extrapolated to a generic country. Even the "small percentage of national territory" framing understates local land competition, since usable, grid-adjacent sites are a much smaller subset of total territory than the percentage suggests.

**Confidence: LOW.** Not because the arithmetic is shaky (an independent recompute during the adversarial pass reproduced every figure with no error), but because of a genuine unresolved rival surfaced during self-adversarial testing: **a small, land-scarce, high-density 10-million-person country could have land as a real, binding constraint.** Substituting a Bangladesh-like density (≈1,265 people/km²) instead of Portugal's (≈117 people/km²) turns the central case into ≈7.7% of territory and the worst case into ≈45% — no longer "well under 5%." The conclusion above is therefore explicitly scoped to moderate-or-lower-density countries; that scope qualifier is load-bearing, not decorative.
