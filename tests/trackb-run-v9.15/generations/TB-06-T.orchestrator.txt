## Answer

**Recommendation:** Do not fully replace timed exams with take-home assessments, and do not leave the pure-timed-exam status quo unchanged either — adopt a hybrid: a grade-load-bearing secured/timed component, take-home format for extended/authentic tasks, and an unpredictable, accommodation-inclusive sampled oral defense, with its integrity resting on the secured component and the viva, never on AI-text detection (chain C4).

**Band (from §6):** MEDIUM.

**Would change it:** A primary-source read of the retrieval-practice and accommodation-efficacy literature (chain C4); institution-specific evidence that viva-sampling or secured-component resourcing cannot be sustained (chain C5); or evidence that real-time AI coaching capable of defeating live oral defense has emerged (chain C5).

## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that generative AI can now produce unsupervised written work that is largely undetectable and often graded higher than genuine student writing, does replacing timed, invigilated written exams with take-home assessments preserve — or degrade — a university's ability to certify that a student's degree reflects that student's own demonstrated competence, and is there a format allocation that does better than either named option alone?

**Success criteria:**
1. The recommendation states, for each named option (timed-exam-only, take-home-only, and any hybrid/composite), whether it preserves the link between the credential and the individual student's own demonstrated competence, given current (2026) AI writing capability and current (2026) AI-detection reliability.
2. The recommendation names the specific assessment format(s) assigned to each of the competing goods this decision must balance — integrity/certification validity, retention/pedagogical value, authentic-task representativeness, and equity/accessibility — rather than declaring one goal an unconditional winner over the others.
3. The recommendation states an observable condition under which the recommended format mix would change (a falsification / "would change it" condition), so the answer is not a permanent verdict dressed as a first-principles conclusion.

## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1** In a blind study, GPT-4-generated submissions were injected into unsupervised, window-timed take-home exams across five undergraduate psychology modules at a UK university. 94% of the AI submissions went undetected by examiners (97% under stricter criteria); AI submissions scored on average half a grade boundary higher than real students; 83.4% chance AI submissions would outperform a random selection of real student submissions. — Scarfe et al., *PLOS ONE*, 2024 (read at source, PMC11206930).
- **GT-2** Across seven AI-text detectors tested on 91 TOEFL essays by non-native English speakers, average false-positive rate was 61.22%; on 88 US 8th-grade essays by native speakers, ~5.19%. — Liang et al., *Patterns*, 2023 (read at source, arXiv:2304.02819v3).
- **GT-3** Survey of 1,041 UK undergraduates: 88% used GenAI on assessments in 2025 (up from 53% in 2024); 18% included AI text directly; 76% believed their institution would detect AI use. — HEPI/Kortext 2025 (read at source).
- **GT-4** GPT-4 scored ~297/400 on the Uniform Bar Exam (68th-90th percentile); 4.2/6.0 on both essay and performance-test components; 75.7% on multiple-choice. — Katz et al. (read at source, PMC10894685).
- **GT-5?** Retrieval-practice literature reports ~2.5x better recall under test conditions vs. restudying, with durable benefits — reported-by-delegate, sources not opened.
- **GT-6?** Contract cheating rose from ~3.5% to ~15.7% historically; take-home/unsupervised format specifically implicated; UNSW detected cheating roughly doubled post-COVID — reported-by-delegate, sources not opened.
- **GT-7** Proposal to randomly sample ~10% of students per assessment for a short oral defense, with accommodations for disability/anxiety/language built in from the outset. — *Times Higher Education* (read at source).
- **GT-8?** Extended time is the most common testing accommodation; timed conditions cited as a top anxiety trigger; but evidence on performance differences is mixed and equity researchers favor inclusive-by-default design over reactive accommodation — reported-by-delegate, sources not opened.

## 4. Derivation Chains (summary)

- **C1 (HIGH):** Detector-gated take-home is not a working safeguard — it misses most AI submissions (GT-1) and wrongly flags non-native English writers (GT-2).
- **C2 (MEDIUM):** The AI-substitution problem already extends beyond take-home's current share and is largely invisible (C1 + GT-3).
- **C3 (MEDIUM):** Timed, closed-resource recall has distinct pedagogical value that pure take-home replacement would forfeit (GT-5?).
- **C4 (MEDIUM):** A hybrid model beats both pure options in a locked, weighted trade-off (81 vs. 76 vs. status quo; full take-home knocked out on the integrity must-have).
- **C5 (MEDIUM):** The hybrid's integrity is durable only under specific design/resourcing conditions and is time-bound against future AI capability.
- **C6 (HIGH):** LLM capability already spans complex, professional-level unsupervised writing tasks (GT-4), discarding A6.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning
- Relying on AI-text detectors as the primary safeguard — ruled out by GT-2's structural bias and the detector-evasion arms race.
- "Wait for better detectors" as a neutral default — not neutral; GT-1 shows the failure is happening now.
- Full replacement (Option A) — knocked out on the integrity must-have before scoring.
- Unmodified status quo — scored lower than the hybrid (76 vs. 81) in the weighted trade-off.
- Treating retrieval-practice value alone as dispositive — would be a single-axis argument ignoring equity and authenticity costs.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Adopt a hybrid model — grade-load-bearing secured/timed component + take-home for extended/authentic tasks + unpredictable, accommodation-inclusive sampled (~10%) oral defense, with integrity resting on the secured component and the viva, never on AI detection.

**Key insight:** The intuitive "timed exams vs. take-home" framing hides that the assumed safeguard for take-home — AI detection — is measurably non-functional today, and fails worst against exactly the population it would be most damaging to wrongly accuse; the substitution problem is already occurring undetected at a 94% rate in real-world use.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The hybrid forgoes some retention benefit of an all-timed regime and costs more faculty time; its equity and integrity benefits are conditional on resourcing and design discipline this analysis cannot verify any specific institution will deliver.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — capped by two unverified ground truths (GT-5? retrieval-practice size, GT-8? accommodation efficacy). The trade-off margin (5 points) is real but not large, and sensitivity analysis shows it is specifically the Equity criterion — resting on GT-8? — that would need to flip for the status quo to win instead.

**Would change it:** (a) primary-source reads of the retrieval-practice and accommodation-efficacy literature; (b) institution-specific evidence that resourcing for the hybrid can't be sustained; (c) evidence that real-time AI coaching capable of defeating live oral defense has emerged.
