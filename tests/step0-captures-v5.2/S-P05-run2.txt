# Trade-Off Analysis: PostgreSQL vs MongoDB vs DynamoDB

**Recommendation: managed PostgreSQL (RDS/Aurora) — HIGH confidence.**

## The decisive insight
The analysis flagged and rejected the load-bearing false assumption: that **500 writes/s implies a need for NoSQL scale-out**. A single modern Postgres instance handles 5,000–10,000+ writes/s — 500/s is ~7% of capacity (~14× headroom). The premise that justifies reaching for DynamoDB/MongoDB **never activates at this scale**.

## Weighted decision matrix

| Criterion | Wt | PostgreSQL | MongoDB | DynamoDB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transactional correctness | 5 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| Throughput fit (500 w/s) | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 |
| Cost fit & predictability | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| Query flexibility | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Operational simplicity / team fit | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| Vendor portability | 2 | 4 | 4 | 1 |
| **Weighted total** | | **97** | **71** | **59** |

*Throughput is deliberately weighted low (2) — not because speed doesn't matter, but because all three options trivially clear 500 w/s, so it can't discriminate.*

## Why PostgreSQL wins
- **Correctness (highest weight):** Native, idiomatic multi-entity ACID for money-touching flows (order + line items + inventory + payment). Mongo and Dynamo support ACID but with constraints — Dynamo caps at 100 items/4MB single-region at 2× cost; Mongo's multi-doc transactions run against its aggregate model.
- **Cost:** ~$700–1,200/month for multi-AZ HA — roughly a third of the $3k budget, and *fixed/predictable* vs Dynamo's consumption-based, hot-partition-sensitive billing.
- **Query flexibility:** Full SQL for the ad-hoc/relational needs e-commerce always grows into (admin search, refunds, fraud rules, reporting). DynamoDB scores lowest here — access-pattern-locked, expensive scans.

## Robustness
The 97 vs 71 vs 59 margin is wide, not a near-tie. The only weight that could flip the ranking is Throughput — and that would require write volume *far* above 500/s. Result is robust for the 12-month horizon.

## Caveats
- **Expiry condition:** If writes grow >10×, revisit (read replicas, partitioning, Aurora). Not a present concern.
- **One unverified input:** team SQL competency was assumed (e-commerce default), not confirmed. It doesn't change the ranking — Postgres wins on correctness, cost, and flexibility regardless — but worth verifying.
