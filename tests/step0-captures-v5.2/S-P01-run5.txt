## Pre-Mortem: Payments-Rewrite Launch

**Premise:** It's next Friday. The full EU cutover has already failed. Working backward from that.

### Three structural weaknesses (the real findings)

1. **No reversibility — the dominant risk.** "Replacing" the legacy Stripe integration implies the fallback is being removed, not kept warm. Same-day full cutover with no escape hatch means *any* failure becomes a forward-fix under fire. This amplifies every other risk.

2. **First contact with reality is in production.** No staged rollout means there's no shadow traffic, no canary, no percentage ramp — correctness is *assumed* until 100% of EU money flows through it. And payment defects are money-movement defects (double-charges, dropped captures, ledger drift), where being wrong is unusually expensive.

3. **Worst-possible timing.** Friday + EU + weekend banking windows maximizes detection-to-resolution time exactly when on-call staffing is lowest and SEPA/banking windows are closed.

### The questions a go/no-go meeting would suppress
- "Do we actually have a tested rollback?"
- "Has anyone reconciled the legacy and new ledgers on real production data?"

These are precisely the two that turn a bad day into a financial-integrity incident.

### Plan changes the pre-mortem demands
- **Keep legacy Stripe warm** as a hot fallback with a tested traffic-switch.
- **Stage the rollout:** shadow/replay traffic → 1% → one small EU market → full.
- **Move the cutover off Friday** to Tuesday morning (full runway, full staffing, open banking windows).
- **Gate go/no-go** on two artifacts: a tested rollback runbook + a completed ledger-reconciliation dry-run on production-shaped data.

### One question it flagged for you
The whole severity assessment hinges on one load-bearing fact: **when you say "replacing," is the legacy Stripe integration being fully decommissioned at cutover, or can it be switched back on?** If it can be switched back, the top risk drops from critical to manageable. If it's truly gone, the rollback-and-reconciliation gate is non-negotiable.
