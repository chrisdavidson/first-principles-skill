# Pre-Mortem: Payments-Rewrite EU Launch

**The headline finding isn't a bug — it's the plan.** The launch design strips away every containment mechanism at the same time: 100% exposure (no canary), no proven rollback, and a low-coverage weekend window. That means a defect of *any* origin converts directly into an unbounded, hard-to-detect, slow-to-mitigate full-EU revenue and compliance event.

## Critical failure modes (imagining it's already failed Saturday morning)

- **No rollback path (most likely fatal):** A defect surfaced and there was no tested way back to legacy Stripe — the team had to *build* a rollback during the outage instead of *executing* one. Legacy may already be torn down (keys rotated, webhooks gone).
- **Cutover-state corruption:** In-flight transactions dropped or double-processed; stored tokens / SEPA mandates / saved cards failed to migrate, breaking recurring + one-click payments for existing customers.
- **EU regulatory:** SCA/3DS challenge flow subtly wrong → legitimate payments declined at scale, or transactions completing *without* required auth (compliance breach). Plus FX/VAT amount mismatches and GDPR data-handling drift.
- **No early-warning signal:** With no canary, the team couldn't tell "new-service bug" from "normal Friday-night noise" until losses were large.
- **Friday timing:** Problems surfaced into a thin weekend on-call, stretching time-to-mitigate to hours.

## Recommended plan changes (priority order)

1. **Build and rehearse a rollback before launch** — keep legacy Stripe warm and instantly re-activatable. Highest-leverage change; if it's impossible, that alone should pause the launch.
2. **Introduce a canary** (1% → 10% → 100% over hours) even though the brief said "no staged rollout" — near-zero timeline cost, buys the early-warning signal you currently lack.
3. **Move the cutover off Friday** to Tuesday/Wednesday morning for full coverage during the risky first 48 hours.
4. **Verify the load-bearing unknowns first:** token/mandate migration, in-flight transaction draining, SCA/3DS conformance, observability parity.
5. **Run a 72-hour reconciliation job** comparing new-service charges against expected amounts to catch silent errors fast.

**Key caveat:** The CRITICAL ratings rest on two unverified assumptions — that rollback isn't engineered and migration paths are untested. If your team can demonstrate (a) a tested instant rollback and (b) validated token/in-flight handling, the entire pre-mortem's severity drops sharply and a same-shape launch becomes defensible. **That's where to spend the time between now and Friday.**
