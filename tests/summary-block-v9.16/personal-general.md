## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Does any available way of acting on this offer leave the two-person household better off, over the next several years, than the status quo, once the $70K gross raise is converted to after-tax money and the partner's income, relocation costs, time and reversibility are counted?

The trigger is "Should I take this job?", but that is not what the analysis has to answer. The offer is a stimulus. What has to be decided is a household allocation: one partner's gain against the other's career. The named options (take / don't take) are not the only ones. Splits and combinations exist: take it and super-commute from Portland; take it only once the partner has an SF offer; decline and use the offer to re-price the current role.

**Success criteria:**

1. Each viable option is compared on **household** after-tax, cost-adjusted cash, with every computed figure's arithmetic shown and recomputed independently.
2. The partner's career enters as a quantified term, a break-even partner-income figure, not a footnote.
3. The option set includes the status quo and at least one composite option.
4. The analysis names the specific observation that would change the recommendation.
5. The analysis separates what the arithmetic settles from what is a household value judgment it cannot settle.
