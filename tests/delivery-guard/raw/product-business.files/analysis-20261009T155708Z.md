## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given an outbound-only, $10K-ACV B2B SaaS business with no self-serve signup/onboarding infrastructure and zero internal free-to-paid conversion data, what is the lowest-risk path (if any) to capture whatever benefit competitors' free tiers are providing them, and does a full freemium launch clear its own build-and-run cost before the company commits to it?

**Success criteria:**
1. The answer names which specific job (or jobs) a free tier would actually need to do for this business — not merely "match competitors" — before any cost/benefit claim is made.
2. The answer prices the free tier's own cost (ongoing infra/support plus one-time engineering build) against a computed break-even conversion rate, using the given $10K ACV, rather than assuming a free tier is low-risk because non-paying users "don't cost anything."
3. The answer does not require picking only "launch a full public free tier" or "do nothing" — a staged, reversible, or composite path is evaluated on equal footing and chosen if it dominates.
4. The answer names concrete, cheaper experiments that would produce real conversion/cost data before a full build is committed, given that no self-serve channel exists today.
5. The recommendation states a confidence level and names exactly what evidence would move it.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Launch a full public self-serve freemium tier now, matching competitors directly

### Dead End: Treat the published 2-5% industry freemium conversion benchmark as this company's expected conversion rate

**What it ruled out:** Confirms GT-7? may be used only as an outside-view sanity bracket (as done in chain C1's theoretical-limit framing), never as a planning number.

### Dead End: "Free-tier users don't pay, so there's no real downside to trying" (the cost-free framing)

**What was tried:** Treating a free tier as a low-risk, reversible experiment with no material downside, since non-converting free users generate no revenue but also (it was assumed) no real cost.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Do not launch a full public self-serve free tier now. Instead, run a bounded, sales-assisted, capped, time-boxed pilot (6-12 months, targeted at a defined segment) with pre-committed, numeric success/kill criteria tied to measured conversion and cost-per-user data, and use that data to make the full-freemium build decision from evidence rather than from competitor-matching (chain C6).

**Key insight:** The blocking question is not "should we have a free tier" but "which job would a free tier actually do for us" (chain C5) — and the second-largest unknown after that, the free-to-paid conversion rate, can be bounded (0.45%-6%, central ~1.6% breakeven, chain C1) and is more plausibly below industry benchmarks than at them for this specific GTM motion (chain C2), which is why committing $150K-400K of engineering plus an open-ended cost burden (chain C3) before testing that number is the real risk — not the absence of a free tier today.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This path delays the public competitive-positioning benefit of being able to say "we have a free tier" (chain C6, competitive-positioning criterion) and defers, rather than avoids, the self-serve engineering investment if the pilot succeeds (chain C1); it also accepts a small risk that the pilot leaks externally before the company has a confident public position (named in the Adversarial pass record, Cluster 3).
