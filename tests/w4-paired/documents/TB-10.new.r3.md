## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: Pure financial cost-benefit modeling
**What was tried:** Scoring the decision as a single NPV — career dollars lost vs. cost of paid caregiving.
**Why abandoned:** It treats care quality, the relationship, and children's welfare as fungible with money, which the family's actual objective function does not support; the headline decisive factors (parents' actual preference, GT-2's trajectory) aren't monetizable inputs.
**What it ruled out:** Using a single financial metric as the primary decision engine — money remains one criterion among several in C4's trade-off, not the whole model.

### Dead End 2: Treating proximity's value as linear in distance
**What was tried:** Assuming "closer is always better, proportionally."
**Why abandoned:** C2 showed EMS/professional response dominates acute outcomes regardless of family distance in nearly all cases (GT-4 + A-14), so the value curve is concave, not linear.
**What it ruled out:** "Get as physically close as possible" as the optimizing rule — the wrong shape of the value function for most of what actually needs proximity.

### Dead End 3: Treating this as a moral/guilt question resolved by intensity of felt obligation
**What was tried:** Using how strongly guilty or obligated the adult children feel as the operative selection criterion.
**Why abandoned:** GT-7 — felt intensity of guilt is not the same variable as measured marginal impact on parent welfare; using it as the decision engine substitutes an affect heuristic for an actual welfare calculation.
**What it ruled out:** Guilt as a *decisive* input (it remains a legitimate personal value the family may still choose to honor — see Assumptions Table A8 — just not evidence about which option is best).

### Dead End 4: "Full relocation" as the unconditional default answer
**What was tried:** Treating full relocation as strictly dominated by staged/targeted alternatives in all cases.
**Why abandoned:** It is not strictly dominated. C4's own flip-test shows it is the correct branch precisely when GT-2 resolves to "acute" and/or GT-10 resolves to "parents clearly want it" — a nameable, not arbitrary, condition.
**What it ruled out:** "Full relocation is always the wrong/right answer" as a fixed verdict — it ruled in full relocation as the correct answer *under a specific, checkable condition*, which is what C6's conditional rule encodes; this also disposes of the strongest rival to C6 rather than leaving it unresolved.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach — five steps, before any relocation decision is made:**
1. Ask the parents directly what they want and need; do not infer it (chain C6).
2. Get a realistic clinical/practical read on the current care trajectory rather than assuming it (chain C2).
3. Price a staged or targeted alternative — a remote-first job transition, increased visit cadence, paid local care, or a small living-arrangement change — against full relocation before committing to the full package (chain C3, chain C4).
4. Set a hard, calendared reassessment date and have the sibling/family-role conversation up front, before starting (chain C5).
5. Commit to full relocation specifically when the trajectory has already resolved to acute or the parents have clearly stated they want proximity — not by default (chain C6).

**Key insight:** Proximity's value on the one thing that is physically, unnegotiably distance-bound — emergency response — is concave and threshold-shaped, not linear: most of the achievable risk reduction comes from being "reasonably reachable," and comparatively little further reduction comes from closing the remaining distance to full co-location (chain C1, chain C2). Reasoning by convention ("just move closer, obviously") misses this shape entirely, and guilt is not a reliable stand-in for the calculation it would take to see it (chain C1).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The staged approach trades certainty and speed for optionality, and it requires real upfront work — an explicit partner career-negotiation and an explicit sibling conversation — that a default full move would let the family skip (chain C4, chain C5); if the family doesn't actually follow through on that upfront work, the second-order effects in chain C5 can silently erase the staged option's advantage and convert it into an unplanned full commitment nobody chose — no chain — flagged assumption only for the residual "the family actually follows through" risk.
