## First-Principles Analysis: Should the family relocate to be near ageing parents?

**The core reframe:** the scenario poses this as a binary ("move or don't"), but that's a false framing. Support-intensity and residence-location are logically separable levers — money, coordination, technology, and physical presence can each increase independently of where anyone lives. Once you see that, the real choice is a **menu of at least five options**, not a switch:

1. Full co-location (both adults change jobs, kids change schools)
2. A shorter-distance proximity move (less disruptive than full co-location)
3. Intensified remote support while staying put (money, coordination, tech, more frequent visits)
4. The parents relocating instead
5. A staged wait-and-see plan pegged to care-need triggers

**Key finding:** scoring these against care-responsiveness, reversibility, career impact, children's disruption, family equity, and cost — under several different weightings — **full co-location ranks lowest almost every time.** It buys a care-responsiveness gain that cheaper options mostly match, at the cost of two careers and a school change. The options that quietly get erased by the binary framing (parents relocating, or a shorter move) usually deliver most of the same benefit for far less cost.

**What should actually drive the decision:**
- **The parents' real care trajectory and severity** — not vague worry. Care needs typically *progress* rather than sitting fixed, and reversing a full move doesn't refund the switching costs already paid — it's a second move, not an undo. That argues strongly for a **staged, trigger-pegged plan** over a single irreversible commitment.
- **Whether cheaper options are genuinely unavailable** — verify this before defaulting to co-location, don't assume it.
- **What the parents themselves actually want** — ask directly; don't decide on their behalf.
- **Each adult's actual career portability**, sized honestly rather than assumed symmetric — an unequal cost needs to be negotiated explicitly, not absorbed silently by one partner.
- **Financial runway, the children's ages, and existing alternative-care capacity** (siblings, paid care) — none of which the scenario specifies, and all of which materially change the ranking above.

**Guardrails against the decision going badly:** four failure modes stood out — deciding for the parents instead of with them, defaulting to co-location without checking cheaper options, skipping the trigger-condition planning until a crisis forces a rushed move, and leaving an unequal career sacrifice undiscussed between the adults.

**Confidence:** the reframing (menu, not binary) and the "not moving isn't cost-free either" finding are solidly established. The staged-approach recommendation is medium-confidence — it rests on general research about care trajectories and school/career-disruption costs that wasn't independently verified against primary sources in this pass, and on facts this hypothetical doesn't supply (actual care severity, finances, ages, career portability). Supplying those would let the trade-off scoring be re-run with your family's real numbers instead of illustrative ones.
