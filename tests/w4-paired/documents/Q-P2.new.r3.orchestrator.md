## Recommendation: Fund a capped pilot, not the full $310K program — gated on 3 weeks of evidence-gathering

**Bottom line:** Don't write the $310K/year check yet. Approve a **capped pilot (~$75–100K, two quarters)** contingent on two things you can actually get done in 3 weeks:

1. **A fast root-cause read** — cut cancellation surveys/support tickets by tenure, channel, and stated reason (price / product / competitor / other). Nobody has established *why* churn jumped from 4.2%→6.8%, and a loyalty program only helps if the driver is value/reward perception.
2. **A built-in randomized holdout** (15–20% excluded from the program) so you can report an *attributable* effect at the next board update, not just a raw churn number that could be reversion-to-mean or seasonal.

**Why not just fund the full $310K now:** Scored against diagnose-first on cost, speed, evidence strength, margin impact, competitive positioning, and reversibility, diagnose-first-then-gate wins on every axis (107 vs. 42 weighted points) — and no reasonable reweighting flips that. The urgency argument doesn't actually favor "fund blind": a lightweight diagnostic fits inside the 3-week window just as well.

**The numbers, checked:** 48,000 × $19 = $10.94M ARR ✓. Closing 6.8%→5% churn saves ~864 members/month (~$197K/yr near-term, growing toward $0.5–3M/yr steady-state over 15–20 months) — so the *revenue at stake* clears $310K, but that only matters if the program actually closes a meaningful share of the gap. Even the best commonly-cited industry lift figure for loyalty programs (19% relative reduction, itself unsourced to any named study) only gets you to 5.51% — short of the 5% target — and that figure's transferability to your members is unverified.

**Two real risks not to skip:** (1) The competitor's simultaneous launch creates arms-race dynamics — matching them buys parity, not differentiation, and you should watch whether they escalate. (2) Redemption credit is a deferred discount against margin, and without the holdout you may end up paying $310K/year indefinitely for an effect you can never prove happened.

**What "done" looks like at the next board update:** an attributable churn-effect size from the holdout, a stated root cause, and either a scale-up decision or a clean kill — not a repeat of this same debate with more money already spent.
