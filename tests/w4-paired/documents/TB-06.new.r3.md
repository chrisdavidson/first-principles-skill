# First-Principles Analysis: Take-Home Assessments vs. Timed Exams in the AI Era

*Mode: full-composer (no focused-technique trigger fired). Companion techniques applied: inversion (Phase 2), five-whys/reduce-to-primitives (Phase 3), theoretical-limit (Phase 4), estimate (Phase 4), trade-off (Phase 4, ×3), second-order thinking (Phase 4), pre-mortem (Phase 5).*

---

## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that (a) take-home assessments are newly exposed to undisclosed AI assistance while (b) timed, proctored exams carry pre-existing, independent validity and equity problems, what assessment-design policy should a university adopt — and is a single institution-wide binary rule ("replace exams with take-home" or the reverse) even the right *kind* of answer, or does the correct unit of policy vary by discipline and by what the assessment is certifying?

**Success criteria** (checkable against §6 without further clarification):
1. §6 names, for each certification-purpose class this analysis identifies, a *specific* recommended assessment format — not a generic "it depends."
2. §6 names at least one concrete second-order consequence (student behavior, faculty/grading workload, or curriculum design) that the recommended framework would need to manage, reached via both the actor lens and the time lens.
3. §6's confidence rating is explicitly tied to named, provenance-labeled ground truths rather than asserted as settled fact.
4. §6 states the condition under which its own recommendation would be false (falsifiable, not merely plausible).

## 4. Derivation Chains

### Companion technique — Theoretical-limit (governing chain C4)

**Direction:** higher detector AUROC is "better" → an **ideal ceiling** in the improving direction, but the ceiling itself is *falling* over time.
**Governing hard constraint (named):** the Sadasivan TV-distance bound on any detector's AUROC (GT-9) — not a thermodynamic limit but a statistical-distinguishability limit.
**Ideal bound:** as TV(AI-text, human-text) → 0 for a given task/prompt, achievable AUROC → 0.5 (chance level), for *any* detector, by construction of the bound.
**Best demonstrated (observation, not calculation):** vendor-claimed <1% document-level false-positive rate (GT-3?) vs. independently-round-up-measured 8.9–14.9% flag rates on unmodified originals from other vendors (GT-3?) — these disagree with each other.
**Conventional (current practice):** many institutions purchase and treat a vendor's AI-similarity score as evidentiary in misconduct proceedings, i.e., conventional reliance already exceeds what is independently, rigorously demonstrated — an **inverted gap**: convention is *ahead of*, not behind, best-demonstrated validated performance.
**Gap 1 (conventional → best demonstrated):** effectively negative/inverted — practice trusts a figure more confident than independent measurement supports; this is a *measurement-integrity* problem, fixable by auditing.
**Gap 2 (best demonstrated → ideal bound):** even a fully-validated current detector sits on a ceiling that itself erodes as models improve (GT-9); this is **irreducible**, not fixable by better engineering — the correct target for chain C4 below.

### Companion technique — Estimate/Fermi (feeding chain C6, second-order step)

Target quantity: additional faculty/examiner hours per course offering if a large course's summative assessment moves from a written exam to individual oral defenses.
Decomposition: `extra hours = N (enrollment) × [t_oral − t_written_grading]` (per-student times, hours).
Central case: N=150, t_oral=0.25h (15 min), t_written_grading=0.133h (8 min) → Δt≈0.117h → **≈17.6 hours** per offering.
Bracket: N=40, t_oral=0.167h, t_written=0.25h → Δt=−0.083h → **≈−3.3 hours** (lower bound; oral can even be *cheaper* for small, slow-to-grade classes). N=400, t_oral=0.333h, t_written=0.083h → Δt=0.25h → **≈+100 hours** (upper bound).
Decision-resolution check: the bracket **[−3.3, 17.6, 100] hours** straddles a decision-relevant threshold around enrollment ≈80–100 students, which is precisely what feeds C6's conditional recommendation below — good enough to stop, per the estimate procedure's stop criterion. *[Assumes: A10]*

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: Assume format-purpose correlation in current practice is tight enough to justify a coarse uniform policy
**What was tried:** Reasoning that because most current take-home work is essay-heavy humanities and most current timed exams are quantitative/STEM, a policy tracking existing practice could stay coarse-grained.
**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by counter-examples where the *same format* serves *different* purposes across courses (a take-home creative-writing assignment certifying original voice vs. a take-home coding assignment that could certify either originality or tool-fluency depending on the instructor's intent) — ruled out by chain C3's independence finding.
**What it ruled out:** Saves re-litigating "just tighten the existing take-home OR just tighten the existing exam" as a global fix.

### Dead End 2: Assume AI-text detection, once improved, will restore take-home validity without redesign
**What was tried:** Betting the integrity strategy on next-generation detectors closing the accuracy gap.
**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-9's theoretical ceiling (chain C4) — detection reliability is bounded by a *shrinking* TV-distance as models improve; this is a structural limit, not an engineering gap that better detectors close.
**What it ruled out:** Saves the institution from betting its academic-integrity strategy on a detection arms race the mathematics says it loses over time.

### Dead End 3: Assume "honor code / integrity messaging only" is sufficient, since the status quo has worked reasonably well historically
**What was tried:** Treating the rival conclusion — strengthen honor-code messaging, change nothing structural — as an adequate response given decades of prior stability.
**Why abandoned:** This is the strongest rival to the headline conclusion (C8), and it is ruled out jointly by C1 (the condition is real, current, and worsening — not stable) and C4 (detection cannot backstop voluntary honesty). An honor-code-only response changes none of the three failure axes (format vulnerability × purpose plurality × workload) that C3/C8 identify.
**What it ruled out:** Saves re-proposing a "just remind students about academic integrity" policy as if it addressed the mechanism GT-1's 53%→88% one-year jump demonstrates.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Replace the binary exam-vs-take-home policy with a certification-purpose-indexed assessment framework (chain C8):

- For assessments where real-time, unassisted performance under pressure is itself the certified competency (licensure-aligned or foundational procedural-fluency courses), retain timed, proctored exams as the primary instrument, paired with a mandatory refresh of accommodation protocols against current enrollment (chain C5).
- For assessments certifying capacity for independent, original scholarly work (most essays, research papers, open-ended analysis), replace unmodified take-home submission with a process-tracked take-home plus a short live defense of the submitted artifact, scaled or funded according to enrollment (chain C6).
- For assessments certifying professional competence in using available tools well (many software engineering, data, and technical-writing tasks), explicitly permit AI use and grade the tool-use skill itself — prompt quality, critical verification, integration into the final deliverable (chain C7).

**Key insight:** The two dimensions the "should we replace X with Y" framing conflates — how vulnerable a delivery format is to undisclosed AI assistance, and what competency the assessment is supposed to certify — vary independently of each other, so no single format choice can be right across every discipline and purpose (chain C3); moreover, the technical fix most institutions reach for first, AI-text detection, is not just currently imperfect but structurally on a shrinking ceiling as models improve (chain C4).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** Every recommended change carries a real cost or a new risk the status quo did not have (chains C6, C7):

- The process-tracked-take-home-plus-defense recommendation carries added faculty/TA time that scales with class size and is not funded at most institutions today, per the Fermi bracket (chain C6).
- The live-defense component of that same recommendation introduces a new, less-mature disability-accommodation gap even as it closes the AI-assistance gap (chain C6).
- The disclosed-AI-tool-use recommendation for the tool-fluent class is a near-tie with a more AI-resistant hybrid variant, not a robust dominant winner — institutions that most fear shallow AI submission should adopt the hybrid instead (chain C7).
