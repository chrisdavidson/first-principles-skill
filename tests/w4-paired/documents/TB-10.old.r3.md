# First-Principles Analysis: Should the family relocate to be near ageing parents?

## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given a family weighing whether to relocate near ageing parents at the cost of both adults' jobs and the children's schooling, what decision criteria and process should determine the choice among the full range of proximity/support options — not just "move or don't" — given that the parents' actual care trajectory and the family's actual constraints are not yet specified?

**Success criteria** (checkable directly against section 6, Conclusion):
1. Section 6 names specific decision variables (care trajectory/severity, financial runway, each adult's career portability, children's ages, existing alternative-care capacity) rather than a generic checklist.
2. Section 6's recommended approach treats "moving closer" as a menu of distinct options, not as synonymous with full co-location plus both jobs and both schools changing.
3. Section 6 states explicitly which named unknowns the recommendation is contingent on, rather than issuing an unconditional verdict.

## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1** Response time to a home-based event (a fall, a hospitalization) is bounded below by travel time between the caregiver's and the parents' locations, and travel time is a function of distance and transport mode. — Provenance: **definitional**, verified by construction (a direct consequence of distance/rate/time; true regardless of which caregiving option is chosen; established via the reduce-to-primitives irreducibility test).
- **GT-2?** Once an ageing parent's care needs become significant, they typically progress over time (functional-decline trajectories) rather than remaining fixed at one severity level. — cited to: general geriatric/functional-decline trajectory literature (e.g., work on disability/end-of-life trajectories such as Lunney et al.); **reported-by-delegate**: reflects general field knowledge carried into this analysis; no primary trajectory study was opened in this session.
- **GT-3** Reversing a full relocation after committing to it does not refund the costs already paid (job tenure, school continuity, moving logistics); the reversal is a second move, not an undo. — Provenance: **definitional**, verified by construction (switching costs of this kind are not fungible).
- **GT-4** Research on school transitions reports a negative wellbeing impact that persists regardless of the transitioning student's age. — source: ACAMH, "Adolescent school transition: How moving school impacts teenage wellbeing"; **read-at-source**: article body, key-claims paragraph, quoted directly — *"evidence suggests there is a negative impact of school transitions on wellbeing, regardless of the age of the student transitioning"* (opened via WebFetch this session, quote located and confirmed).
- **GT-5?** A study of adolescent school mobility (Langenkamp, 2016, *Sociology of Education*/SAGE) found transfer students reported fewer school friendships and more failed courses, alongside increased closeness to teachers. — **reported-by-delegate**: located via WebSearch summary only; primary article not opened this session.
- **GT-6?** Labor-economics research documents wage/earnings penalties from career interruptions and job changes, varying by voluntary/involuntary status and, in some studies, by gender. — cited to: e.g., a ZEW discussion paper on West German career-interruption wage penalties, and an arXiv working paper on gender differences in job-switching earnings effects. — **reported-by-delegate**: located via WebSearch titles/summaries only; neither primary source opened this session.
- **GT-7** A care need the family does not meet directly does not disappear — it is absorbed by some other supply source (sibling, paid care, the parents' own remaining capacity) or it goes unmet; there is no third option where it is met by nobody's action. — Provenance: **definitional**, verified by construction (an accounting identity: total need = supply from all sources + unmet residual).

### Conclusion C1: The real choice is a menu of at least five options, not a binary

`GT-8 (support ≠ residence, separable levers) + GT-1 (response time bounded by travel time)`
→ raising parental support can happen through money, coordination, technology, or physical proximity — four independent levers, not one *[Assumes: A13]*
→ physical proximity itself ranges continuously from same household to a short drive to a long flight, not a sharp co-located/not-co-located switch
→ because both the support lever and the distance variable are continuous rather than binary, the real choice is not a single move-or-not switch
→ the real choice set spans at least five distinct options — full co-location, a short-distance proximity move, intensified remote support while staying, the parents relocating instead, or a staged wait-and-see plan *[Assumes: A14]*

**Confidence:** HIGH (no `GT-N?` input; GT-1 and GT-8 are both definitional, verified by construction).

### Conclusion C2: The decision should be staged and trajectory-pegged, not one-shot

`GT-2? (care needs typically progress rather than sit fixed) + GT-3 (reversal compounds switching cost, does not undo it)`
→ a decision sized to today's severity will predictably be miscalibrated once severity changes
→ because reversing an early full move re-triggers the same job/school switching costs rather than cancelling them, an early irreversible commitment is expensive to correct later
→ the decision should instead be staged and pegged to named, observable care-trajectory milestones, so support intensity can escalate or de-escalate without repeating the full switching cost *[Assumes: A15]*

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — GT-2? is reported-by-delegate. Raising to HIGH requires either a primary functional-decline-trajectory source opened directly, or a direct clinical/geriatric assessment of these specific parents confirming a progressive (not static) need.

### Conclusion C3: The evidentiary bar for accepting full relocation should be high

`GT-3 (switching costs compound on reversal) + GT-4 (school transitions cost wellbeing regardless of age) + GT-5? (adolescent transfers cost peer ties/coursework) + GT-6? (career interruption/relocation carries an earnings penalty)`
→ neither the schooling cost nor the career cost shrinks reliably just because the children are younger or a career seems flexible
→ because those costs compound rather than cancel if the family later reverses course, the bar for accepting them should be evidence, not assumption *[Assumes: A15]*
→ the family should require proximity-dependent care needs that cheaper options provably cannot meet before accepting a full relocation, not general worry or a sense of obligation alone

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — GT-5? and GT-6? were located via search summary only, not opened at source. Raising to HIGH requires opening the Langenkamp (2016) study and the cited labor-economics working papers directly.

### Conclusion C4: "Not moving" is not a cost-free default

`GT-7 (unmet need is routed to some other supply source, or goes unmet)`
→ if the family adds no support, the parents' need is either absorbed elsewhere — a sibling, paid help, the parents' own remaining capacity — or left unmet *[Assumes: A13]*
→ an unmet need remaining quiet is not the same as it being resolved — silence is not evidence of adequacy
→ not moving is therefore not cost-free — it routes the parents' care need through some other channel, and that channel deserves the same scrutiny as the moving option

**Confidence:** HIGH (GT-7 is definitional, verified by construction; no `GT-N?` input in this chain's head).

### Conclusion C5: Full co-location is usually the option of last resort, not the default

`C1 (real choice set is a five-option menu) + GT-3 (switching costs compound on reversal)`
→ scoring the five options against care responsiveness, reversibility, career impact, children's disruption, family equity, and cost ranks full co-location lowest under every weighting tested *[Assumes: A16]*
→ co-location scores lowest because it pays for two career changes and a school change to buy a care-responsiveness gain that cheaper options mostly match
→ the two options that scored best across weightings — the parents relocating instead, or a shorter-distance proximity move — buy most of the same response-time gain at far lower cost *[Assumes: A17]*
→ full co-location is therefore the option of last resort — chosen only when the parents' own relocation is infeasible and a shorter move cannot reach an adequate response threshold

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — the illustrative criteria weighting (A16) is this analysis's judgment call, not this family's own stated values. Raising to HIGH requires the actual family to set and lock their own weights and re-score, and requires confirming A17.

Raising care-responsiveness to reflect acute/imminent need (weight 8) produced A=72, B=89, C=91, D=98, E=95 — D and B rose to the top, A stayed last in both scenarios. The ordering between "act now" (B/D) and "wait and monitor" (E) is genuinely sensitive to how urgent the parents' real need is, which this hypothetical does not specify — full co-location (A) scored lowest under every weighting tested, which is the one part of this finding that is not sensitivity-fragile.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Fermi/quantitative cost estimate
**What was tried:** Attempted to size the decision quantitatively — estimate relocation cost (moving, income gap during job search, home sale/purchase) against paid in-home care cost, to produce a break-even threshold.
**Why abandoned:** The hypothetical supplies no numbers — no salaries, distances, home equity, local care costs, or ages. Any estimate would require inventing placeholder figures, manufacturing false precision; the estimate procedure's own decision-resolution stop criterion cannot be met without real inputs.
**What it ruled out:** Confirms no numeric verdict is available at this level of generality; the analysis instead names the inputs (A10, A13) a real family must supply before running this estimate themselves.

### Dead End: Theoretical-limit framing of response time
**What was tried:** Considered a theoretical-limit derivation for "fastest possible response time to a home emergency" — physics-permitted floor versus what current arrangements achieve.
**Why abandoned:** The floor is already fully captured by GT-1 as a direct definitional consequence of distance/rate/time. Unlike an engineering ceiling, there's no institutional convention artificially holding response time below what physics allows — running the full procedure would just re-derive GT-1 with extra ceremony.
**What it ruled out:** Confirms GT-1 needs no second, heavier technique to be established.

### Dead End: Lexicographic priority of children's wellbeing over parents' care
**What was tried:** Considered building chain C5 around a fixed rule that children's disruption always outranks every other criterion, rather than a weighted sum.
**Why abandoned:** This assumes a value-ordering the scenario never establishes. A genuinely acute, safety-critical parental need can legitimately outweigh a school-year disruption for some families, and the reverse can hold for others — a fixed rule would smuggle a values judgment into the method as if it were a fact.
**What it ruled out:** Confirms children's disruption belongs in C5 as one weighted (family-specific, A16) criterion, not an analysis-imposed trump card.

### Dead End: Symmetric career-cost assumption
**What was tried:** Initially modeled both adults' career disruption as roughly equal for simplicity.
**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by common real-world asymmetry (portability, seniority, remote-compatibility differ between careers) and by the labor-economics literature located for GT-6?, which shows career-interruption costs are not evenly distributed even in aggregate. A symmetric shortcut would hide a real negotiation (A7) inside a false average.
**What it ruled out:** Confirms A7 and chain C7's fourth guardrail (explicit negotiation of unequal cost) are necessary, not optional.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Treat this as a menu-selection-plus-process decision, not a binary. First, separate "how much support does proximity actually need to buy" from "should we move" (chain C1). Second, score the five real options — full co-location, a shorter-distance proximity move, intensified remote support while staying, the parents relocating instead, or a staged wait-and-see plan — using this specific family's own weights, not generic ones (chain C5). Third, whichever option is chosen, build it as a staged process pegged to named, observable care-trajectory trigger conditions rather than a single irreversible commitment (chain C2), and run the four guardrail checks before acting: ask the parents directly, verify cheaper options are genuinely unavailable before defaulting to co-location, build the trigger plan before a crisis forces one, and negotiate any unequal career cost explicitly (chain C7).

**Key insight:** The scenario's own framing treats support-intensity and residence-location as one variable, but chain C1 shows they are logically separate (GT-8), and chain C5's scoring shows full nuclear-family co-location is usually the most expensive way to buy a given amount of proximity — not the natural reading of "moving closer" (chain C5). The two options that most directly answer "how do we get the parents more support" — the parents relocating instead, or a shorter-distance move — are routinely invisible inside the binary the question poses.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** A staged, option-preserving approach (chain C2) trades a faster, more decisive-feeling response for lower total switching cost and better fit to a need that will likely change over time; it demands more up-front work from the couple — explicit trigger-planning, an uncomfortable conversation about unequal career sacrifice — in exchange for a lower chance of a rushed, crisis-driven move later (chain C7). Choosing anything short of full co-location also accepts a real response-time and visit-frequency gap relative to full co-location (chain C1, chain C5) — a genuine cost if the parents' need turns out more acute than currently visible, a risk this analysis cannot size on the information given (no chain — flagged assumption only; see A4, A9).

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. The reframing conclusion (chain C1, HIGH) and the accounting-identity conclusion (chain C4, HIGH) hold regardless of family specifics. The process/ranking recommendations (chains C2, C5, C6, C7) are MEDIUM because they depend on GT-2? (care needs typically progress rather than staying fixed — reported-by-delegate, not read at source this session), and GT-5?/GT-6? (adolescent-transfer and career-interruption research, located only via search summary). Raising this to HIGH requires: (a) opening the specific studies behind GT-5 and GT-6 directly, (b) an actual geriatric/functional assessment of these parents confirming GT-2's progressive-not-fixed pattern applies to them, and (c) the family supplying facts this analysis was never given — care severity/trajectory, financial runway, the children's exact ages, each adult's career portability, and whether alternative caregiving capacity already exists (A4, A9, A10, A12, A13).
