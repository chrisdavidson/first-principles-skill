## Recommendation: Don't fund the $310K/year program as proposed — fund a capped pilot instead

**Bottom line:** Don't commit the full $310,000/year now. Fund a small, randomized pilot (well under $100K) with a real control group, run it alongside an immediate root-cause diagnostic, and bring the full-year decision back to the board in 60–90 days with actual data.

### Why not the full commit

**The timing kills the tidy story.** Churn rose over the last ~6 months; the rival's loyalty program has existed for only ~1 of those months. It can't be the dominant cause of the spike, which means a loyalty program isn't a proven fix — it may just be treating a symptom of whatever the real driver is (price, product/outages, onboarding, support, seasonality, or plain cohort churn). Nobody has run the basic diagnostic yet (exit surveys, cohort breakdown, outage/pricing timeline, support-ticket trend), and that's cheap and fast to do in three weeks.

**The economics are genuinely uncertain, not clearly favorable.** In the idealized case (churn instantly and fully drops to 5%, sustained all year), the incremental margin-adjusted value comfortably clears $310K. But realistic ramp-up (partial adoption, not instant), plus likely adverse selection (an undifferentiated points program tends to reward members who'd have stayed anyway, not the ones actually at risk), pulls the realistic year-one benefit down into a range that straddles the $310K cost — and turns negative if there's any unbudgeted redemption/credit liability on top of the stated figure. Worth confirming with Finance whether $310K is fully loaded.

**A before/after launch can't tell you if it worked.** Churn could drift back toward 5% on its own (reversion, seasonality) and you'd credit the program either way. Only a treatment/control pilot answers the actual causal question.

**Competitive framing matters for the board narrative.** Since the rival moved first, your own program — even a good one — now reads as defensive parity, not a differentiator. That's a legitimate reason to fund *something*, just not a reason to believe it fixes the churn spike.

### What to bring to the board in 3 weeks
1. A root-cause diagnostic (data you already have) — this is the actual answer to "why did churn climb," and it's answerable before the board meeting.
2. A capped, randomized pilot design with a control cohort, sized for statistical significance, budgeted a fraction of $310K.
3. A follow-up checkpoint in 60–90 days to make the full-year call on real response data instead of a hope.

Confidence in this recommendation: moderate-high on the "don't blind-commit" call itself; the underlying ROI estimate stays uncertain until margin, true program cost, and root cause are confirmed — which is exactly what the pilot and diagnostic are for.
