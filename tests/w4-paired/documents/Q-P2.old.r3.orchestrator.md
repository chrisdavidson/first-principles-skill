**The core problem:** even in the best case, the numbers don't clear.

- Current MRR is $912,000 (48,000 × $19). At the current 6.8% churn you lose ~3,264 members/month; hitting the proposed 5% target saves ~864 members/month, worth **~$197,000/year in retained revenue**.
- The program costs **$310,000/year** — a shortfall of ~$113,000/year even if it fully hits its target.
- Working backward, the churn rate needed to actually break even on cost is **~3.97%** — below your *pre-crisis* 4.2% baseline. So the 5% target CS picked isn't a validated number; it doesn't even pay for itself.

**Why the lever may not fit the problem:** a points/credit program can only plausibly move *voluntary* cancellations (value perception), not involuntary churn (failed payments, expired cards). You don't currently know that split — and that's the single most important gap, because if a meaningful chunk of the churn rise is involuntary, this program can't touch it at all.

**On the competitor:** your churn started rising two quarters ago; the rival's program launched a month ago. Timing rules it out as the cause of most of your increase, and there's no evidence yet that *their* program is even working — "they launched one" isn't evidence this lever works, it's analogy.

That fits inside the deadline; the deadline binds when you announce, not when you can start gathering data.
