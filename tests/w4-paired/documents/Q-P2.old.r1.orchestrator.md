## Recommendation: Fund conditionally — not yes, not no

**Don't approve the full $310k/year yet.** Instead, greenlight two things now, inside the 3-week window:

1. **A 2-week churn-driver diagnostic** — segment the churn rise by cohort, plan, and cancellation reason.
2. **A capped pilot** of the loyalty program on a bounded cohort, specifically watching whether redeemers are members who'd have stayed anyway (adverse selection) vs. genuinely at-risk members.

Full funding gets gated on those two coming back clean, timed to land at the board update.

### Why not just fund it (or reject it outright)

**The timing tells you the rival isn't the main cause.** Churn started rising ~2 quarters (≈6 months) ago; the rival's program launched ~1 month ago. A cause can't precede its effect — the rival's launch explains at most a recent sliver of the trend, not its origin. Building a matching program because a competitor just did is answering the wrong question.

**The math is the harder problem, and it doesn't clear on its own logic.** $310k/year = ~$25,833/month, ÷ $19/member = ~1,360 members/month you need to retain just to cover cash cost — that's a **2.83pp** churn reduction required on a pure revenue basis. CS's own bet is only **1.8pp** (6.8%→5%), about 64% of that bar. Layer in gross margin (65–75%, industry-typical since no real figure was given) and the required cut rises to **~3.8–4.4pp** — more than even a full reversion to the original 4.2% baseline would deliver. On the numbers as stated, even total success doesn't obviously break even.

**Loyalty programs have a structural weak spot here:** redemption requires ongoing engagement, which is exactly the behavior disengaging members stop showing first — so credits often flow to members who were never going to leave, not the ones you're trying to save. That's untested for your case, but it's exactly what the pilot should measure before you scale spend.

**The $310k is also stickier than it looks.** Once members expect redeemable points, pulling the program back is reputationally costly — so this isn't a one-year bet you can cleanly unwind if the diagnosis turns out wrong.

- You lose the "we acted fast" story for this board cycle — the answer becomes "here's the diagnostic and pilot in flight," not "here's what we shipped."
