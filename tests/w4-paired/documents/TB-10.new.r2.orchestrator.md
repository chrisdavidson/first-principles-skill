## What Should Actually Drive the Decision

**The core reframe:** this isn't one binary choice (move / don't move). It's really three separable sub-decisions — job, housing, schooling — that differ sharply in how reversible and time-sensitive they are, plus a caregiving question that "proximity" only partly answers.

**Key insight:** "Moving closer" and "arranging paid care" get treated as substitutes when they actually solve different problems. Proximity reduces *response time* (how fast someone can be there). It does nothing for *labor-hours* — and once care needs cross a real threshold (dementia-level care averages ~100 hrs/month), the binding constraint is hours and skill, not distance. Both need budgeting regardless of where anyone lives.

**Recommended approach (from a weighted trade-off across five options — move now, stay+paid care, staged/trigger, reverse relocation, one spouse relocates):**
1. **Stay put now**, fund professional local care sized to the parents' *actual assessed* need (not "ageing" as a vague label).
2. **Define a concrete, written trigger** — a clinically-assessable checklist, reviewed annually, co-developed with a geriatric-care professional — not a feeling like "if things get bad."
3. **Pre-position groundwork** while waiting: job-market contacts kept warm, target schools researched, a dedicated transition fund on autopilot. This is what makes "staying" different from "waiting and hoping" — delay without a defined trigger is genuinely risky (an unpredictable acute event — a fall, a stroke — can force a rushed decision with none of this in place).
4. **If triggered, sequence the sub-decisions independently** rather than forcing all three at once — a school change during a fixed exam year is not equivalent to a house sale or a job switch in cost or reversibility.

**Two things this can't settle for you, and shouldn't paper over:**
- Whether the parents actually *want* this — commonly assumed, rarely asked directly, and load-bearing for the whole plan.
- Whether other siblings will actually honor a support-sharing agreement — families that "look covered" locally often see other siblings quietly disengage. Get this explicit and in writing at the same time as the trigger.

**Practical test for "how close is close enough":** rough bracket is 0–120 minutes one-way drive time depending on your real discretionary hours, centering near ~45 minutes. Under ~20–45 min, weekly hands-on support is sustainable without moving; over ~90–120 min, it isn't, regardless of intent — that's the range where "move" actually becomes the answer.

Confidence on the overall recommendation is **medium** — mainly because the ranking uses illustrative scoring your family should redo with real numbers, and because whether early relocation has value *beyond* the labor-hours math (just being present while parents are well) is a values question no fact settles either way.
