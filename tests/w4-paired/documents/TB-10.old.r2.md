### Conclusion C2: The move is justified only if it is the lowest-cost route to the needed care capacity

```text
GT-3 (dual job + school switching costs are real and non-symmetric) + GT-7? (paid in-home eldercare exists as a market alternative)
```

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Using job-portability alone as the deciding heuristic

**What was tried:** An early framing considered simply picking whichever path has the least logistical friction — e.g., move if one partner's job happens to be remote-portable — and treating that as the answer.

**Why abandoned:** This is circular: it assumes the sacrifice is acceptable because it is easy to execute, rather than testing whether it is acceptable. It ignores GT-8?'s named relationship-risk factor around unexamined asymmetric sacrifice, and no genuine intermediate claim could be stated from "low friction" alone that reached a justified conclusion about what the family should value.

**What it ruled out:** Using job-portability as a standalone decision heuristic. Any acceptable version of this path must be routed through explicit symmetric-cost negotiation (A9, chain C4), not substituted for it.

### Dead End: Treating remote communication as a full substitute for physical proximity

**What was tried:** Considered whether video calls and other remote-communication technology make GT-1 (distance → travel time/response latency) irrelevant, i.e., that "being closer" carries no real logistical benefit once communication is instantaneous.

**Why abandoned:** This directly contradicts GT-1. Remote communication cannot perform hands-on physical tasks (medication administration, mobility assistance, emergency transport), so the claim "distance doesn't matter" is falsified by a named ground truth, not merely weak.

**What it ruled out:** The "fully remote support is equivalent to proximity" framing as a basis for dismissing relocation outright. It also, symmetrically, rules out treating proximity as delivering unlimited benefit (see chain C3) — the truth sits between these two abandoned extremes.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach — build the decision around five concrete drivers, not sentiment:**
- Get an honest, current assessment of the parents' actual care-task and response-latency needs before treating proximity as the answer (chain C1).
- Bracket the relocation's total cost against the cost of buying equivalent care locally, and choose the cheaper route to the same care outcome (chain C2).
- Recognize that moving converts travel time into potentially usable time but does not create new hours beyond what two new full-time jobs leave free (chain C3).
- Convert the family's specific failure modes — job risk, financial cushion, asymmetric sacrifice, children's timing — into named go/no-go checks before committing (chain C4).
- Score the full option set, including staged, remote, and non-relocation alternatives, on weighted criteria rather than defaulting to full relocation (chain C5).

**Key insight:** The real lever in this decision is caregiving *capacity*, not geography — and geography only solves the fraction of that capacity problem governed by travel time and emergency-response latency (chain C3). The rest of what "help aging parents" bundles together — actual care hours, emotional/relational proximity, and family-duty values — can be, and should be, negotiated and sourced separately, including through non-relocation alternatives (chains C2, C7). Most framings of this decision treat "be near parents" as one indivisible goal; decomposing it is what makes the decision tractable.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:**
- Choosing to gather information (care-trajectory assessment, cost brackets, go/no-go checks) before deciding trades away the emotional relief of a fast, decisive move (chains C4, C5).
- If the parents' actual condition later proves urgent, this delay carries a real cost this analysis cannot currently quantify — no chain — flagged assumption only.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — six of seven chains (C1, C2, C4, C5, C6, C7) rest on at least one unverified `GT-N?` input (GT-2?, GT-4?, GT-7?, or GT-8? respectively); only chain C3 is HIGH. Resolving the Missing Ground Truths — especially MGT-2 (parents' actual care trajectory), MGT-3/MGT-4 (financial cushion and job portability), and MGT-1 (children's ages) — would raise most chains to HIGH. This MEDIUM rating is the honest ceiling given what the prompt withholds; it is not a defect to be argued away.
