Source: standard result in parallel-computing theory (Amdahl, 1967); read-at-source: derived directly here from the definition of speedup = T_old/T_new applied to a two-fraction workload model — self-verifying by algebraic derivation, not sourced from an external document opened in this analysis.

Read-at-source: true by construction of what a CI pipeline is; no external document required.

# 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: "Hardware improvements always help proportionally, regardless of pipeline composition"

**What was tried:** Initially considered evaluating the hardware-purchase plan on the premise implicit in leadership's framing — that faster CPUs shrink total pipeline time roughly in proportion to the speed increase (e.g., "2× faster runners → roughly half the pipeline time").

**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-1 (Amdahl's Law): any pipeline with a non-negligible non-compute-bound fraction (GT-2) cannot be sped up proportionally by compute alone. The assumption is false whenever (1−P) > 0, which GT-2 establishes is generically true for CI pipelines.

**What it ruled out:** Saves a future analyst from re-deriving the naive "just multiply by the speed factor" estimate, and establishes why any hardware-sizing exercise must first establish P (chain C1, C5).

### Dead End 2: "Skip instrumentation and recommend architecture-only, since documented interventions (GT-8?) are well-established anyway"

**What was tried:** Considered short-circuiting directly to "invest in caching + parallelization + test-impact selection + merge queue" as the recommendation, since C4's trade-off analysis scored it a strong second place (90).

**Why abandoned:** This skips exactly the diagnostic step C5's pre-mortem identifies as the structural weakness in *any* undiagnosed plan, hardware or architecture — committing to a specific architectural fix without knowing where the 47 minutes actually goes risks solving the wrong bottleneck (e.g., investing in test parallelization when the dominant cost is actually queue-wait from too few runners, which parallelization alone does not fix). The intermediate claim "architecture-only is safe to commit to" could not be established without the same measurement C5 requires.

**What it ruled out:** Saves a future analyst from assuming that knowing the industry-standard fixes substitutes for knowing which of those fixes *this specific pipeline* needs — the two are not the same claim.

# 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Instrument per-stage CI pipeline timing before committing to any single fix (chain C5); then apply whichever of caching, test parallelization/sharding, test-impact selection, and merge-queue/fast-required-plus-deferred-full gating the diagnosis shows is load-bearing (chain C4), reserving hardware procurement for any residual portion diagnosis shows to be genuinely and durably compute-bound (chain C1, C5).

**Key insight:** Batching is not simple developer non-compliance to be corrected by policy — it is a rational adaptation to a binary, all-or-nothing 47-minute gate (chain C3, C6) — and buying faster runners is being proposed to fix it without anyone having measured the one variable (the compute-bound fraction of those 47 minutes) that determines whether hardware can reach the problem at all (chain C1). A realistic hardware upgrade plausibly lands at 18–40 minutes, not near-zero (chain C2), which may not even clear the threshold that made batching worthwhile in the first place (chain C6).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This recommendation accepts a slower time-to-first-relief than an immediate hardware purchase, in exchange for spending on a diagnosed rather than guessed-at bottleneck (chain C4). It accepts that the diagnostic step itself rests on an unverified claim about how cheaply this org's specific CI platform can be instrumented (GT-11?) — no chain — flagged assumption only. It also accepts that even the diagnose-then-target sequencing recommendation is corroborated by two techniques that were not run independently of each other, which is a real but disclosed limit on how strongly their agreement can be read (chain C7).
