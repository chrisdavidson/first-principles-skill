## Should a university replace timed exams with take-home assessments?

**Short answer: no, not as a blanket swap — that's the wrong question.** The framing hides a false dichotomy: what actually determines AI-vulnerability is *supervision and authentication design*, not duration or location. A 90-minute unsupervised remote exam is just as exposed to an AI assistant as a take-home essay; a proctored oral defense is AI-resistant whether it follows a timed test or a week-long project.

**Why the binary breaks down:**
- Assessment isn't one thing with one purpose — it does three distinct jobs: certifying competence, giving formative feedback, and shaping study behavior. These have different AI-exposure profiles, so no single format serves all three.
- Plain unsupervised take-home essays with no authentication step currently can't be trusted for high-stakes certification: OpenAI discontinued its own AI-text classifier in 2023 for low accuracy, and independent research found detectors falsely flag non-native English writers' essays as AI-generated at roughly a 61% rate. Detection is not a viable backstop, and it's specifically biased against a vulnerable group.
- 56% of students already report using AI on assignments or exams (BestColleges, 2023) — this isn't a hypothetical risk, it's already the baseline.

**What actually works better:** A differentiated portfolio, not a uniform rule —
- **High-stakes certification of extended writing/reasoning:** take-home work paired with a *structured* oral defense or in-class process checkpoint. Structured oral exams show meaningfully higher reliability (α≈0.75–0.80) than unstructured ones (α≈0.50) in the evidence available, and live unscripted questioning is structurally hard to fake with AI.
- **Where the actual target skill is AI-augmented judgment** (increasingly the real-world skill): explicitly AI-permitted tasks with mandatory disclosure and critique.
- **Where the certified construct is genuinely unaided procedural/recall fluency:** keep supervised timed assessment.
- **Low-stakes formative work:** take-home essays remain fine — nothing is being certified, so the integrity problem doesn't bite.

**Trade-offs, honestly priced:** this portfolio costs more than either extreme and requires the added labor to be funded, not absorbed by already-stretched instructors — a pre-mortem exercise surfaced this as the most likely single cause of the policy collapsing (unfunded oral-defense mandates get quietly abandoned by large or under-resourced departments). No authentication method is permanently AI-proof — this is an arms race, so format choice needs periodic re-evaluation, not a one-time fix. And oral/live formats carry their own equity risk (anxiety, English-as-additional-language students) that has to be actively designed around with accommodation pathways, not just accepted as a scoring trade-off.

**What would change this recommendation:** if AI-text detection someday becomes reliably accurate (>98%) with no demographic bias under adversarial conditions, "unsupervised take-home + detection" re-enters as a serious contender. That hasn't happened, and the recommendation is conditioned on today's detection landscape, not a permanent verdict.

**Bottom line:** the real lever isn't timed-vs-take-home, it's supervision/authentication tied to what the course is actually trying to certify. A university should pick format per-course against that question, not adopt one rule for all of it.
