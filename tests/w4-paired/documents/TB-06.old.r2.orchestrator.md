## Should a university replace timed written exams with take-home assessments?

**Short answer: not as a blanket swap.** The right unit of decision isn't "exam vs. take-home" — it's purpose-by-purpose, and the fix for AI-era vulnerability is different from what most people reach for first.

**Why not just detectors:** The instinct to lean on AI-text detectors as the safeguard doesn't hold up. Beyond being empirically unreliable (OpenAI's own classifier ran near coin-flip and was pulled), there's a real ceiling here: detector accuracy is bounded by how statistically distinguishable AI text is from human text, and that gap narrows under paraphrasing — so it's partly a mathematical limit, not an engineering problem that better software fixes. Detectors also show real bias against non-native English writers, making them a liability, not just an imperfect tool.

**The actual mechanism at stake:** A credential only means something if the submitted work is statistically tied to the specific student who gets certified. Unverified take-home work breaks that link once AI can produce competent prose on demand — regardless of how well-designed the assignment is.

**What actually restores the link:** Two independent routes work — (1) controlling the production environment (timed, proctored, no network access), or (2) a live post-hoc check, like an oral defense of take-home work. Full-cohort oral defense doesn't scale, but a *sampled or suspicion-triggered* version does (rough Fermi check: sampling ~10-15% of a 200-student class keeps faculty time in the single-digit hours rather than 30+).

**Recommendation, split by purpose:**
- **Certification / high-stakes ranking** — keep take-home for authenticity, but pair it with sampled/triggered oral defense (or equivalent individual authentication). Don't revert to pure timed exams — they score worse on authenticity and formative value in a weighted comparison, and the hybrid beats both pure options even after a sensitivity check.
- **Purely formative work** (feedback, low-stakes practice) — plain take-home remains fine. The learning-science mechanism behind good assessment (effortful, repeated retrieval) doesn't depend on modality, and the hybrid's advantage nearly vanishes once certification stakes are removed.

**Two things a pure "add oral defense" fix misses:**
1. **Equity** — economically advantaged students disproportionately have paid AI-tool access. An AI-permitting take-home policy without addressing this reintroduces income as a confound in what's supposed to be a competence signal. Needs either restricted AI use or institution-funded equal access.
2. **Accommodations** — if disability/language accommodations simply *waive* the oral-defense requirement rather than substituting another individually-authenticating channel, the accommodated subgroup silently reverts to the unauthenticated case the policy was built to fix.

**Confidence caveat:** This came out at MEDIUM confidence overall — several supporting facts (detector accuracy figures, the equity-access study, one university's oral-viva policy) were sourced via web search summaries rather than fully verified at the primary source in this session (one lookup was blocked by a 403, another exceeded fetch limits). The core logical argument (the information-theoretic attribution requirement, the scaling arithmetic, the trade-off ranking) doesn't depend on those unverified figures, but institution-specific numbers should be checked before this becomes policy.
