## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Full reversal to proctored-only exams for everything

**What was tried:** Considered as the most enforcement-safe response — if take-home authorship can't be verified, eliminate take-home assessment entirely and rely solely on invigilated, in-person exams (the "return to paper" pattern several institutions have adopted, per the search evidence behind GT-4's broader context).

**Why abandoned:** Fails construct validity for independent-synthesis competencies (GT-2, GT-10 — a recall/fluency-only format structurally cannot observe the synthesis competency at all, regardless of how well enforcement works), and scores below the hybrid design on the trade-off (C5) — it would score comparably to Option A on most criteria and worse on future-skill fit (C6) and wellbeing (C7 of the trade-off's own criteria).

**What it ruled out:** Treating enforcement-maximalism alone as sufficient justification for a format choice, absent any construct-validity analysis — saves a future reader from re-treating "just go back to paper" as a complete solution rather than a partial one that trades away an entire measured competency.

### Dead End: Process-logging/keystroke analytics as the authorship-verification mechanism

**What was tried:** Considered as an alternative to sampled oral defense for Option C's verification component, on the theory that logging typing behavior over time might scale better than live oral checks (avoiding GT-6?'s scalability ceiling).

**Why abandoned:** Keystroke/process logging can show that text was typed over time but not that the reasoning behind it was the student's own — a student can type out AI-dictated content, or draft on a separate untracked device. The intermediate claim ("logging verifies authorship of reasoning, not just of data entry") could not be established; this path was speculative with no verification path this session could establish, so it was not cited on any chain's head and was replaced by the oral-defense/spot-check design (GT-9), which directly probes live reasoning rather than typing behavior.

**What it ruled out:** Treating "more monitoring/analytics" as an adequate substitute for direct, live verification of reasoning — saves a future analyst from assuming that technical surveillance alone solves the authorship problem detection software already failed to solve.

### Dead End: Pinning an exact, disclosed false-positive figure for Turnitin below its 20%-composition threshold

**What was tried:** Two direct WebFetch attempts against Turnitin's own blog to locate a specific false-positive percentage for the harder, lower-AI-fraction regime that matters most for partial-assistance cases.

**Why abandoned:** Both attempts returned HTTP 403 (Phase 3 failure record, GT-5). ## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Adopt a hybrid, matched-format assessment design rather than fully replacing timed exams with take-home work: retain timed/proctored components wherever a course's stated learning outcome is unaided recall or procedural fluency under real-world time pressure; redesign take-home work to pair with a sampled, live verification component (a short oral defense or equivalent) and an explicit AI-use disclosure-and-permission norm; demote automated AI-detection software from decisive evidence to an internal triage signal; and govern the choice at the course/learning-outcome level under one thin, funded, institution-wide floor (a mandatory disclosure norm and a minimum-verification expectation), rather than either a single monolithic institution-wide format mandate or unconstrained course-by-course discretion (chain C5, chain C6).

**Key insight:** Timed exams and unmodified take-home assessments were never actually competing to answer the same question — they measure different constructs by design (GT-1, GT-2, GT-10) — and the arrival of capable LLMs did not make one format simply "better" than the other. It specifically destroyed the one thing an unmodified take-home format silently assumed it could still deliver: a verifiable link between the submitted text and the student's own reasoning (chain C4, chain C1). The non-obvious finding is that the real fix is not choosing between formats but restoring that lost authorship signal — a narrower, more tractable problem than "replace vs. don't replace" (chain C4).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This design costs more faculty time than either an unmodified take-home-only regime or the pure status quo (chain C5's workload criterion), and it delivers on its equity promise only if the institution funds universal baseline AI-tool access and continues to resource disability-accommodation infrastructure for the retained timed components — a named precondition, not a guarantee (chain C7, LOW confidence, explicitly conditional). It also does not fully solve cross-section comparability if adopted unevenly course-by-course with no institutional floor (chain C6's second-order caveat) — and whether a "thin floor" is sufficient in practice to prevent that erosion was not independently tested this session (no chain — flagged assumption only).

**Decision-maker's pre-decision checklist** (what to measure/know before deciding institution-wide vs. course-by-course, per Essence success criterion 5): (1) For each course, what specific construct does its assessment certify — recall/fluency, independent synthesis, or both (drives GT-1/GT-2/GT-10 format-matching)? (2) What licensure/accreditation constraints apply to this discipline (A-11) — do they mandate unaided-performance proof? (3) What is the institution's actual capacity for sampled oral verification at this class size (GT-6?, resourcing, not assumed) — has funding for calibration training and load credit been committed (pre-mortem Cluster 1)? (4) Is universal baseline AI-tool access funded, or will disclosed-and-permitted AI use reproduce a paid-tier equity gap (GT-11?, chain C7)? (5) Is there an institution-wide disclosure/minimum-verification floor, published and auditable, to prevent transcript incomparability across sections (chain C6, pre-mortem Cluster 3)? An institution that cannot answer (3) and (4) affirmatively should pilot at small scale before committing institution-wide.
