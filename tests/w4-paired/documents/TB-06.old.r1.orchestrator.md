## Should timed exams be replaced by take-homes?

**Reject the binary.** LLMs break a hidden assumption on *both* sides, not just the take-home side:

- **Take-home's broken assumption:** unmonitored prose was ever a safe proxy for individual ability. It isn't anymore, and AI-text detectors can't safely patch that — a verified source (Liang et al. 2023, arXiv:2304.02819) found detectors flag non-native English writers as AI-generated at **~61% false-positive rate vs. ~5% for native writers**. Using detectors as integrity evidence would discriminate against exactly the students most vulnerable to it.
- **Timed exam's broken assumption:** that unaided, time-pressured recall is still the capability worth certifying. That's only true for a narrower set of courses (safety-critical judgment, foundational fluency) than universities currently apply it to. For courses that actually certify tool-augmented judgment or research synthesis, a tools-banned exam measures the wrong thing entirely — not weakly, but not at all.

**Recommendation, by construct rather than by blanket policy:**
- Replace exams with a redesigned take-home (disclosed/permitted AI use, graded on judgment, **plus a live oral-defense or spot-check**) wherever the target is synthesis/judgment/research — *if* the institution can staff that oral component at the course's enrollment scale.
- Don't do a naive take-home swap that keeps essay grading unchanged and leans on detectors for integrity — that's the one dead end this analysis rules out with high confidence.
- Keep a bounded timed component, in a hybrid, for courses where unaided recall or safety-critical judgment is the actual thing being certified.

**Honest caveats:** the recommendation costs real faculty time and risks decaying back to the cheap default within a few years without sustained investment; live oral verification may carry its own bias (accent, interview anxiety) that wasn't measured against the detector-bias figure above; and the choice between "fully redesigned" vs. "hybrid with a timed slice" is a near-tie that turns on an institution-specific fact (oral-assessment capacity) — not something resolvable in the abstract. Overall confidence: **MEDIUM** (one sub-finding — detector unreliability — is backed at HIGH confidence by a directly-verified source; the rest rests on plausible but unverified premises, disclosed rather than glossed over).
