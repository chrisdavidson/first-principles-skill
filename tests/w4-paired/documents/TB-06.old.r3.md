# 1. Problem Essence

**Essence Statement:** Given that LLM availability has changed what an unsupervised, artifact-based writing task can validly certify, the real question is not "exam or take-home?" but: *for a given course/assignment, which combination of assessment-design axes (supervision, time-budget, connectivity, AI-permission) correctly matches the specific competence that assessment must certify, for the specific stakeholders (student, instructor, accreditor, employer) who rely on that certification — without paying unacceptable costs in equity, faculty load, or credential trust?*

**Success criteria (checkable against the conclusion):**
1. States what each format actually measures, and for whom.
2. Names the causal mechanism by which LLM availability degrades take-home validity — not "students might cheat" as an unexamined premise.
3. Enumerates alternatives beyond the binary and explains why the binary framing is false.
4. Traces second-order effects (equity, anxiety, grading load, arms race, trust, signaling) per option.
5. Delivers a decision framework/provisional recommendation differentiated by discipline/construct-type, backed by named chains — not a bare "it depends."

# 3. Ground Truths

`?`-marked: GT-1, GT-3, GT-4, GT-5, GT-6, GT-7, GT-8, GT-9 (8 of 10).

- **GT-1?** — Pre-LLM, producing a fluent, correct, well-organized take-home artifact without possessing the underlying competence required either genuine mastery or a costly/risky substitute (ghostwriter, plagiarism, a collaborator doing the work), making artifact quality a reasonably reliable, low-cost proxy for competence. *Provenance: unverified — general domain knowledge, no source opened this session.*

- **GT-2** — Seven widely-used GPT detectors (Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu & Zou, 2023) showed an average **61.22% false-positive rate** on non-native-English (TOEFL) essays vs. near-perfect accuracy on native-English (US 8th-grade) essays; a single self-edit prompt cut detection of GPT-3.5-generated essays from 100% to 13%. *Provenance: **read-at-source** — arXiv:2304.02819, "Results" §1 ("GPT detectors exhibit bias against non-native English authors") and §3 ("Simple prompt can easily bypass current GPT detectors"), p.2–3 of the PDF, confirmed by direct read.*

- **GT-3?** — At least 25+ universities (including MIT, Yale, NYU, UC Berkeley, Vanderbilt per secondary reporting) have banned or restricted AI-text detectors in disciplinary decisions. *Provenance: reported-by-delegate (search summary), not opened at primary source.*

- **GT-4?** — Subject-matter experts grading real coursework could not reliably distinguish LLM-generated essays from genuine student work across multiple disciplines (Bodnick/Harvard 2023; corroborated by a 2024 institutional-response study). *Provenance: reported-by-delegate.*

- **GT-5?** — GPT-4, zero-shot, on the full Uniform Bar Exam (MBE + MEE essay + MPT performance-test sections) scored ≈297/400, above the passing threshold (≈260–272) in every U.S. jurisdiction; on USMLE it scored ≈92–95% across Steps 1–3, ≈20 points over the passing threshold. *Provenance: reported-by-delegate — corroborated across 4 independent secondary sources (SSRN, Royal Society journal record, author's GitHub summary, press coverage); the primary journal page returned HTTP 403 this session.*

- **GT-6?** — Test anxiety is a documented source of construct-irrelevant variance in timed testing: anxiety-driven performance differences are not differences in the construct measured, and the effect is non-uniform across test-takers (a fairness concern). *Provenance: reported-by-delegate (educational-measurement literature summary).*

- **GT-7?** — Institutions permitting unrestricted, undisclosed generative-AI use on previously-unsupervised assessments without task redesign reported grade-distribution compression and rising integrity caseloads (2023–2025); researchers describe this period as one where "the social contract underlying take-home assignments has been altered by a tool the instructor cannot directly see or control." *Provenance: reported-by-delegate.*

- **GT-8?** — Faculty time to grade a substantive written submission with meaningful feedback commonly runs ~10–20 min/student; a comparable-depth oral spot-check runs ~5–15 min/student. *Provenance: unverified — general instructional-workload knowledge, used only as a bracketed Estimate input.*

- **GT-9?** — Signaling theory (Spence, 1973): a credential's value to an external evaluator derives from being a signal costly enough to correlate reliably with the unobservable quality it certifies; a cheap-to-fake signal loses informational/market value regardless of whether average underlying competence changed. *Provenance: unverified this session — canonical, largely uncontested economic theory, used as an analytic lens.*

- **GT-10** — A take-home's operating conditions are, by construction, unsupervised, loose-timed relative to the task, and typically internet/device-connected. A proctored, in-person, closed-device timed exam removes device/internet access and compresses the time budget, so any attempt to consult an external assistant competes with the same clock the student's own unaided work would use. *Provenance: definitional/analytic — follows directly from the two formats' own operating definitions; no external source applicable.*

# 4. Derivation Chains

**C1 — Core mechanism (why LLM availability breaks take-home validity)**
```text
GT-1? (artifact quality was a valid proxy pre-LLM) + GT-10 (take-home's resource conditions)
→ before LLMs, the take-home's looseness was harmless because faking a quality artifact without competence was itself costly and risky
→ GT-2 and GT-4? show a capable assistant can now produce a quality artifact cheaply, quickly, and largely undetectably
→ the take-home's own conditions — no supervision, no time pressure, internet access — are exactly the conditions under which that cheap production is least constrained
→ the mechanism is a resource-environment mismatch, not abstract cheating: the format's validity depended on an economic fact GT-1? has falsified
→ an unmitigated take-home can no longer isolate unaided individual competence from the ability to operate a capable assistant, for any task the assistant can perform
```
Confidence: HIGH on the structural mechanism (anchored in GT-10, definitional); MEDIUM on magnitude (GT-1?, GT-4? unverified).

**C2 — False binary**
```text
GT-10 (three independent resource axes) + C1 (mismatch mechanism)
→ supervision, time-budget, and connectivity are three independent switches, not one binary, yielding at least eight combinations
→ several combinations are already standard practice: open-book timed exam, remotely-proctored take-home, oral defense with no artifact to outsource
→ a fourth axis is entirely omitted by the binary framing: whether AI assistance is disallowed, tolerated, or the explicit object of evaluation
→ the real decision space is four-axis, and the binary framing specifically hides the one axis (AI-permission) that resolves C1's mismatch for tool-inclusive tasks
```
Confidence: HIGH — combinatorial/definitional argument, not dependent on `?` facts.

**C3 — Discipline/construct differentiation**
```text
GT-9? (signaling theory) + GT-5? (LLMs already clear licensure-exam content) + C1 + C2
→ a course's assessment must certify one of two distinct targets: an unaided-competence floor, or tool-inclusive professional competence
→ an unaided floor matters when the profession or regulator requires certified capability without tools — public-safety or foundational-skill contexts
→ GT-5? shows even classic gatekept professional exams are within an assistant's reach, so task difficulty no longer signals which target applies
→ only the regulatory/professional purpose of the specific assessment decides the target, independent of how hard the content looks [Assumes: A16]
→ the correct format is a property of which target a specific course or assignment certifies, decided case by case, not a university-wide default
```
Confidence: MEDIUM (GT-5?, GT-9? both `?`); the qualitative two-target structure is robust to imprecision in GT-5?'s exact figures.

**C4 — Detection/arms-race dead end**
```text
GT-2 (61.22% false-positive rate; 100%→13% evasion) + GT-3? (25+ universities banning detectors) + GT-6? (anxiety as comparable equity concern)
→ detectors are simultaneously unreliable on protected populations and trivially evadable by sophisticated misuse
→ detection-as-primary-control therefore adds equity harm without reliably solving the validity problem it targets
→ a policy response consisting mainly of "add a detector" should be rejected as a primary control; at most a low-weight triage signal [Assumes: A17]
```
Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH — GT-2's figures are read-at-source; weak link is generalizing a 2023 GPT-2-era study to 2026 detector products (A17, flagged).

**C5 — Cost/scalability (Estimate technique)**
Target quantity: faculty-hours per 100-student course per cycle. Unit factors: (minutes/student ÷ 60) × N.
```text
GT-8? (10–20 min written grading; 5–15 min oral, bracketed) [Assumes: A18]
→ full oral, N=100: central 10 min/student = 16.7h; bracket [8.3h, 25h]
→ thorough written grading, N=100: central 15 min/student = 25h; bracket [16.7h, 33.3h]
→ spot-check oral audit, 20% of N=100: central 10 min/student = 3.3h; bracket [1.7h, 5h]
→ both bracket ends agree: full oral is feasible-and-comparable to grading; spot-check is an order of magnitude cheaper
→ cost is not decisive against a hybrid (spot-check layered on grading); it is decisive against full-oral-for-everyone stacked on top of full written grading (33–58h/100 students)
```
Confidence: MEDIUM — GT-8? is an unverified bracketed estimate, but the Estimate procedure's decision-resolution stop criterion is met (both bracket ends agree on direction).

**C6 — Deterrence sufficiency of spot-check audit**
```text
C5 (spot-check cost) + GT-7? (observed integrity degradation under unmitigated policy)
→ model E[cost of illegitimate use] = P(audited) × P(caught|audited) × Penalty, vs. benefit = effort saved [Assumes: A19]
→ with P(audited)≈0.20 (C5), P(caught|audited) high because a real-time oral follow-up is harder to fake than the original artifact, and Penalty severe
→ E[cost] can exceed benefit for most students without auditing every submission
→ the audit layer works by reintroducing the supervised, real-time condition GT-10 identifies as missing, not by replacing the take-home's ecological validity [Assumes: A20]
```
Confidence: MEDIUM — sound deterrence-theory structure; specific thresholds are for institutional calibration, not derived here; A20 (covert real-time assistance during the oral itself) is an explicit, currently emerging weak link.

**C7 — Theoretical limit (observability ceiling)**
```text
C2 (four-axis space) + C6 (probabilistic deterrence, not certainty)
→ an assessment cannot certify "produced without unauthorized assistance" with certainty in any setting not observed in real time — an information/observability limit
→ task redesign can raise this ceiling only by changing what is certified (tool-inclusive competence, directly evidenced by the artifact), not by better-detecting what was certified
→ "a sufficiently clever prompt is cheat-proof" is bounded above by this ceiling for any task an assistant can perform, however the prompt is worded
→ only two moves structurally close the gap: real-time supervision (oral/proctored), or redefining the certified construct (AI-permitted design); everything else is probabilistic mitigation below the ceiling
```
Confidence: HIGH on the structural/logical claim (near-definitional, information-theoretic); MEDIUM on C6's calibration feeding into it.

**C8 — Second-order/pre-mortem: cost of doing nothing**
```text
GT-7? (grade compression, rising caseloads) + GT-9? (signaling theory)
→ [2nd] as artifact quality decouples from competence (C1) at scale, grade distributions compress upward even without average competence changing
→ [3rd] per signaling theory, employers/programs observing compressed grades discount the credential's value, regardless of individual competence [Assumes: A21]
→ [3rd, alt.] students who did not use AI (choice or unequal access) are graded on the same curve as those who did — a new inequity replacing the anxiety-based one take-homes fixed
→ doing nothing is not neutral: it degrades the credential's signaling value while trading one equity problem for a different one
```
Confidence: MEDIUM/LOW — both anchor GTs are `?`, and the grade-compression → employer-discounting causal link (A21) is plausible but not directly measured here; flagged as the weakest chain.

**C9 — Recommendation / hybrid framework**
```text
C1 (mismatch mechanism) + C2 (four-axis space) + C3 (two certification targets) + C7 (observability ceiling)
→ classify each assessment by certification target first: unaided-floor vs. tool-inclusive-competence [Assumes: A16]
→ unaided-floor assessments move toward the supervised end of the axes (proctored exam, oral defense) rather than patching an unsupervised take-home with detectors (C4) [Assumes: A18, A19]
→ tool-inclusive-competence assessments keep the take-home but redesign the task to require and evaluate AI-assisted output explicitly, with disclosure
→ the large middle category uses a hybrid: staged take-home with process evidence (drafts/version history) plus a cheap spot-check oral audit (C5, C6)
→ the university should adopt a three-tier, per-course policy rather than a single campus-wide format switch
```
Confidence: MEDIUM overall (inherits C3/C5/C6/C8's MEDIUM ratings); HIGH on the structural spine (C1, C2, C7).

# 5. Abandoned Reasoning

- **"Just revert to timed exams everywhere."** Abandoned: ignores C3's construct differentiation and reintroduces GT-6?'s anxiety/equity harm for courses where an unaided-competence floor isn't actually required; C7 shows exam "safety" is about resource-conditions, not inherent superiority — using it where not needed sacrifices ecological validity and equity for no certification benefit.
- **"Fix it with better AI detectors."** Abandoned per C4: GT-2's read-at-source false-positive and evasion figures make detection-as-primary-control a dominated strategy.
- **"Surprise closed-book oral for every assignment, by default."** Abandoned per C5's Fermi estimate: stacking full oral on top of unchanged full written grading roughly doubles faculty hours (33–58h/100 students), infeasible as a blanket policy even though it is the highest-validity single option in principle.
- **Analogy to "how other universities do it."** Not used as evidence anywhere above, per the no-analogy-as-direct-evidence rule; every institutional-practice claim is grounded in an independent ground truth (GT-3?'s detector bans, GT-7?'s institutional-response study) rather than cited as "so we should too."
- **"Disclosure alone (self-report, no verification) is enough."** Abandoned per inversion (A13) and C6's deterrence math: without an audit/penalty mechanism, disclosure changes no rational actor's expected-cost calculus for undisclosed misuse.

# 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Adopt a three-tier, per-course/per-assignment policy rather than a single campus-wide replacement decision (chain C9). Tier 1 — licensure-adjacent, regulatory, or foundational-skill assessments retain or move to supervised/proctored/oral formats (with device-free protocols addressing the covert-real-time-assistance risk flagged at A20). Tier 2 — the general curriculum middle uses a hybrid take-home with required process evidence (drafts, version history) plus a cheap spot-check oral audit (chains C5, C6). Tier 3 — assessments of genuinely tool-inclusive professional skills keep an unsupervised take-home but redesign the task to require and evaluate AI-assisted output explicitly, with disclosure (chains C2, C3).

**Key insight:** The mechanism by which LLM availability breaks take-home validity is not "students might cheat" — it is that the format's implicit validity assumption (artifact quality implies competence) was always contingent on the pre-LLM economic fact that faking a quality artifact was costly, and the take-home's own operating conditions are exactly the conditions under which a now-cheap capable assistant is least constrained (chain C1). This reframes the decision from "exam vs. take-home" into a four-axis design problem (supervision, time-budget, connectivity, AI-permission) whose correct setting depends on which of two distinct things — a certified unaided-competence floor, or a certified tool-inclusive competence — a specific assessment is meant to certify (chains C2, C3, C7).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The three-tier policy costs more faculty design effort up front than either single-format extreme (`no chain — flagged assumption only`). The Tier 2 spot-check audit's deterrence effectiveness depends on institution-specific calibration of audit rate and penalty severity, bracketed but not fixed here (chain C6, explicit MEDIUM confidence, with A20's covert-real-time-assistance risk left unresolved). The credential-signaling harm from an unmitigated status quo (chain C8) is this analysis's weakest chain (MEDIUM/LOW) and should be treated as a risk to monitor via employer/grad-program feedback, not a proven present harm.
