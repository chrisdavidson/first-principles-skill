## 4. Derivation Chains

### Companion-technique outputs

**Five-Whys (real motivation, causal mode).** "We should move closer" → *why?* to help as they age → *why does that require relocating?* to reduce emergency-response risk and reduce the guilt/distance of not being present → *why does that require both adults changing jobs specifically?* it may not — this is the fork the analysis tests, because occasional visits don't lower response time or guilt, but neither does relocation necessarily, if a lighter-weight structure (paid local care, a monitored alert device, a nearby relative) delivers the same reduction. **Root cause:** the terminal values are (a) reduced tail-risk of an unattended crisis, (b) reduced anticipatory guilt, (c) preserved relationship quality — proximity is one instrument for these, not the goal itself, and families default to "the whole household moves" partly because it is the most *legible* signal of commitment, not because it is the most *effective* one.

**Inversion (what guarantees this move is wrong).** Sufficient conditions for failure: the caregiving need ends or changes character faster than expected (GT-1's short tail); neither adult secures comparable work; the parents don't actually want the involvement; the marriage/partnership can't absorb stacked stressors; an adolescent child's school transition goes badly; caregiving expands into unbounded "role captivity" while siblings disengage; the "closeness" that was supposed to cut response time doesn't materialize because the relocated adult is still at work all day; and no exit plan exists once a home is bought and both jobs are resigned. Each converts into an untested-belief row above (A4, A6, A8–A10, A14–A15, A17).

**Pre-mortem (2 years later, it failed).** Raw causes cluster into: (A) uncertain runway — the need was shorter, or already handled by someone else, or the parent's condition stabilized; (B) career/financial fragility — one job offer fell through, the trailing spouse never found comparable work, cost of living was underestimated; (C) unbounded caregiving/role captivity plus sibling disengagement; (D) marital/child overload from stacked stressors; (E) the parents didn't actually want it; (F) no exit plan — home purchased, both resigned "real" jobs, kids re-enrolled, so A–E become unrecoverable rather than correctable. The hardest-to-voice items in a group setting are typically E and the "the need ended sooner than expected" half of A — both are treated here as load-bearing planning scenarios, not disloyalty.

**Estimate — caregiving runway vs. switching-cost payback.** Central estimate of remaining caregiving need ≈ 4 years [bracket: well under 1 year in a rapid-decline-to-facility-or-death scenario, up to 10+ years for the 15% tail] (GT-1). Central estimate of the time needed to "earn back" two career resets, a home purchase, and a child's school transition ≈ 2–5 years. **Decision-resolution check:** the two brackets overlap heavily, and a meaningful share of caregiving spans falls *below* the 2–5-year payback floor — meaning a real, non-trivial probability exists that an irreversible relocation will not have "paid for itself" before the underlying need changes character. Since the family cannot know in advance which tail they're in, the correct response is a structure that performs acceptably across the whole bracket (staged/reversible), not one justified only in the long-tail scenario (full irreversible relocation).

**Theoretical-limit — distance vs. response time.** The governing constraint is travel time, not the emotional feeling of "closeness." GT-2 fixes the operative danger threshold at about one hour. Crossing from "hours-to-days away" (a flight, arranging coverage) to "tens of minutes away" crosses that threshold decisively. But crossing from, say, a 45-minute drive to a 10-minute drive does **not** cross it — both are already inside the golden-hour region if a responder is free to travel. The law-permitted ceiling on proximity's safety value is therefore a step function, not a smooth function of miles: once inside the region, further proximity buys little more, and the same region can be occupied by a monitored alert device plus a paid local aide without anyone relocating — while a relocated adult who is at work all day is not automatically "inside the region" in the sense that matters (available, not just nearby).

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Treat this as a staged, monitored process rather than a single irreversible commitment: verify the parents' actual wishes and current trajectory directly, preserve at least one adult's income or career anchor wherever the family's real job-portability allows, and negotiate an explicit, bounded caregiving-role agreement with the parents and any siblings — before treating any change in physical proximity as permanent (chains C1, C2, C4, C6).

**Key insight:** The variable that intuitively drives these decisions — physical distance — is a threshold effect that a monitored local support system can satisfy without relocation, while the variables people under-weight because they are less visible (career/financial reversibility, caregiving-role boundaries, the parents' own consent) are the ones that determine whether the move succeeds or becomes a multi-year regret (chains C1, C3, C5).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** Choosing a staged or local-support structure over full relocation defers some emotional/relational closeness that only physical co-presence provides, and may be perceived by the parents, siblings, or the couple themselves as under-committing even though it is structurally more robust; it also requires more upfront coordination work than simply deciding to move, and defers rather than eliminates the possibility that fuller relocation becomes necessary later (chain C6).

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — the core structural claim (verify-and-stay-reversible beats commit-and-hope under genuine duration uncertainty) rests on chains C1–C6; only C1 is HIGH, and C2 through C6 each inherit at least one `?`-marked ground truth (GT-3?, GT-4?, GT-5?, GT-6?, GT-7?). Verifying GT-4 (dual-career/relocation-failure statistics) and GT-6 (aging-trajectory stochasticity) at source — the two most load-bearing unverified inputs, feeding C4 and C2 respectively — would raise the overall confidence toward HIGH.
