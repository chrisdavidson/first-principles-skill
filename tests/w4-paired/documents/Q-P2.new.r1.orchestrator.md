## Bottom line: fund conditionally — not the full $310K blind, not zero

**Fund a scoped, cost-capped pilot** running alongside a 1–2 week churn diagnostic (voluntary vs. involuntary churn split, price/product/cohort review), with a **hard go/no-go gate at 90–180 days** measured against a control cohort's actual churn rate — not redemption/engagement metrics. That's a deliverable answer for the board in three weeks: "gated pilot with a defined checkpoint," not a yes/no.

**Why not a straight yes:** The core justification — "a competitor just launched a loyalty program, so this is why churn rose and this fixes it" — doesn't survive a timeline check. Churn started climbing **~6 months ago**; the competitor launched **~1 month ago**. The competitor can't explain the first five months of the spike. Separately, the loyalty-program efficacy stats I found (Paytronix's ~10% churn-reduction claim) trace back to an unverifiable secondary source inside vendor-promotional content selling loyalty software — weak evidence for the specific bet.

**Why not a straight no:** The dollars justify *some* action. At 48,000 members × $19/mo:
- Steady-state customer value has fallen from **~$452/customer** (at 4.2% churn) to **~$279/customer** (at 6.8%) — roughly **$8.3M in destroyed relationship value** already baked in.
- The $310K program's breakeven bar is low: on a conservative 12-month basis, it pays for itself if churn falls from 6.8% to just **~6.0%** — about a third of the way to the 5% target, not all of it.

**What would change the answer:**
1. **Voluntary vs. involuntary split** — if the diagnostic shows churn is mostly payment failures (common in subscriptions), redirect the $310K to payment-recovery tooling instead; rewards can't touch that.
2. **True all-in cost** — if "$310K to operate" excludes redemption-credit liability, real cost is higher and the breakeven bar gets worse. Get Finance to confirm scope before committing.
3. **A hidden single cause** (price change, outage, bad acquisition cohort) coinciding with the spike — if so, loyalty is a band-aid over the real problem.

**Pre-mortem flag:** the most likely failure mode a year out isn't the program failing outright — it's the diagnostic being rushed, the pilot cohort being non-random, or success being declared on engagement/redemption numbers instead of actual churn movement. Pin the numeric go/no-go threshold and the metric (churn, not redemptions) into the board approval itself, before launch.

Overall confidence on this recommendation is **LOW** — not because the logic is shaky, but because the underlying causal story (why churn actually rose) is still genuinely undiagnosed. That's exactly why "gated pilot" beats both "fund fully" and "don't fund" — it's the option built to be robust to that uncertainty rather than betting on it.
