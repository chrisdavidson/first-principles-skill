## The framing itself is the first thing to break

"Should timed exams be replaced by take-home assessments" presupposes one binary policy fits every case. It doesn't survive inversion: format-vulnerability to undisclosed AI use and what-an-assessment-certifies vary **independently**. A bar exam, a literature essay, and a coding assignment aren't testing the same thing, so collapsing them into one policy choice is a category error before any evidence is weighed.

## What's actually established

- **The premise is real and structurally worsening, not hypothetical**: reported AI use specifically for assessments among surveyed undergrads jumped 53%→88% in one year (HEPI 2025), and a proven theoretical result (Sadasivan et al.) shows any AI-text detector's accuracy is mathematically bounded by how distinguishable AI text is from human text — a bound that *falls toward chance* as models improve. Detection is not a durable fix, now or later.
- **Timed exams' problems and take-home's problems don't cancel out**: extended-time accommodation is a legally mandated floor (ADA), not a courtesy, and already runs at scale — so any redesign has to preserve it, not treat it as optional.

## Three purpose-classes, three different answers (scored via locked trade-off criteria, not vibes)

1. **Fluency-under-pressure** (licensure-adjacent, procedural competence): keep the timed proctored exam. Nothing else matches the construct, and it already dominates on every criterion.
2. **Independent original scholarly work** (essays, research): unmodified take-home is out (detection can't backstop it). Best option is **process-tracked take-home + a short live defense** — but this only wins clearly if institutions fund the added grading time. A Fermi estimate puts that at roughly break-even for small classes but **~100 extra hours per large (400-student) course** — meaning unfunded rollout will predictably get watered down exactly where oversight is thinnest.
3. **Tool-fluent professional practice** (much of software/data work): fighting AI use is both futile and beside the point — **permit it explicitly and grade the tool-use skill itself** (prompt quality, verification, integration). This wins, but only narrowly over a more AI-resistant hybrid — a real, disclosed near-tie, not a landslide.

## Bottom line

No single policy. A certification-purpose-indexed framework, with each track's own failure mode already flagged: bucket-ambiguity disputes, unfunded workload blowing up at scale, and a *new* equity gap in oral defenses (social anxiety, ESL, speech disability) replacing the one being closed.
