## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that a university's assessment exists to certify a specific capability to a third party, and that capable LLMs simultaneously (a) let an unmonitored take-home's traditional proxy for "unaided individual ability" be satisfied without that ability being present, and (b) leave automated detection of that fact too unreliable to police it — which assessment format, or combination of formats, delivers a defensible certification signal, decided per the capability each course actually needs to certify, rather than by one institution-wide policy?

**Success criteria:**
1. The answer names, for the major construct types a university certifies (unaided safety-critical fluency; tool-augmented judgment/synthesis; research ability), which format or combination is defensible for that construct — checkable by reading whether section 6 states a construct-to-format mapping rather than one universal verdict.
2. The answer states what capable LLMs actually change about the verification problem on *both* sides, not just "cheating got easier" — checkable by confirming section 6 cites a chain describing the take-home-side failure (the unaided-proxy assumption breaking) separately from the exam-side failure (the construct-relevance assumption breaking).
3. The answer avoids a flat yes/no and produces conditional branches — checkable by confirming section 6 contains at least one "yes, if…" and one "no, if…" claim.
4. The answer names at least one concrete, currently unresolved risk of its own recommendation rather than presenting it as costless — checkable by confirming Trade-offs acknowledged names a specific unresolved risk, not a generic disclaimer.

## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1** An assessment format can produce, as directly observable evidence, only a work product (or, for live/oral formats, real-time verbal/behavioral performance) — never the internal cognitive process that generated it — source: definitional necessity (an "assessment" is, by definition, an inference from observed behavior/output to an unobserved capability); read-at-source: N/A — verified by conceptual construction (passes the Phase 3 definitional-reduction branch, not a document citation)
- **GT-2?** As of 2026, general-purpose LLM writing assistants can, unsupervised and with ordinary internet/subscription access, produce fluent, structurally competent, subject-plausible prose for most undergraduate/graduate essay and short-answer prompts, often needing only light human editing to pass as unaided work — unverified: no controlled benchmark study was fetched this session; rests on general knowledge of the technology class
- **GT-3** Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu & Zou, "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers" (*Patterns*/arXiv:2304.02819, 2023): seven widely used GPT-text detectors produced an average false-positive rate of 61.22% on 91 TOEFL essays by non-native English speakers, versus ~5.19% average FPR on 88 US 8th-grade native-English-speaker essays (ASAP dataset), with all seven detectors unanimously misflagging 19.78% (18 of 91) of the TOEFL essays — source: arXiv:2304.02819; read-at-source: p.2, "Results → GPT detectors exhibit bias against non-native English authors" (figures quoted directly from the fetched PDF)
- **GT-4** Timed, proctored, in-person written exams are a long-established assessment format with mature university-scale infrastructure (lecture-hall seating, invigilation staffing, fixed-duration scheduling), predating and structurally independent of AI-assistant availability — source: institutional/observed practice; read-at-source: N/A — background fact about current university operations, not a document citation
- **GT-5?** Disability-accommodation frameworks in most OECD jurisdictions (e.g., ADA/Section 504 in the US) mandate extended time and alternative-format testing accommodations for documented disabilities, reflecting that fixed-duration timed testing disadvantages some test-takers independent of the construct assessed — unverified: statutory/case-law text was not opened this session
- **GT-6?** Reliable home internet, private uninterrupted study space, and paid-tier AI-assistant access are not uniformly distributed across a student population and correlate with socioeconomic status — unverified: no specific survey/study was opened this session
- **GT-7** Oral examination/live-defense formats (doctoral viva voce, medical-licensing OSCEs) let an examiner interactively probe understanding in real time and have long institutional precedent as a category distinct from both pure timed-written and pure take-home formats — source: institutional/observed practice; read-at-source: N/A — background fact, not a document citation
- **GT-8?** Under standard economic signaling theory (Spence, "Job Market Signaling," 1973), a credential's value to a third-party observer depends on being costly-to-fake relative to the capability certified; a credential obtainable without that capability, at equal or lower cost, ceases to function as a reliable signal — unverified: Spence's original paper was not opened this session
- **GT-9?** Cognitive-science research on retrieval practice/"desirable difficulty" (e.g., Roediger & Karpicke) documents that effortful, unassisted recall produces more durable long-term learning than passive or fully assisted review, for foundational-knowledge acquisition — unverified: not opened at source this session

### Conclusion C1: No single assessment format — timed or take-home — reaches the theoretical ceiling on what any format can verify; each is one noisy, single-occasion measurement below what triangulation would approach

GT-1 (assessment observes only output/behavior, never process)
→ every assessment format's inference from a finite observed sample to an unobserved capability carries irreducible measurement error *[Assumes: A12 — error shrinks only when multiple measurements are genuinely independent, treated as a working statistical premise]*
→ the ceiling that measurement theory permits is several independent, construct-valid measurements converging toward the true underlying ability, never a single one reaching it
→ a single timed exam and a single unmonitored take-home submission are each only one such measurement, so both sit below that ceiling for the identical structural reason

**Confidence:** MEDIUM
No GT-N? input feeds this chain, but its second hop rests on a general measurement-theory/psychometric principle not verified against a specific citable source this session (e.g., the AERA/APA/NCME *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*, or classical-test-theory literature). Reading such a source directly would raise this chain to HIGH. This is a self-imposed cap, not a D-07 requirement.

### Conclusion C2: Automated AI-text detectors cannot serve as a take-home format's integrity mechanism without exposing a vulnerable population to disproportionate wrongful accusation

GT-3 (detectors: 61.22% FPR on non-native writers vs 5.19% on native writers)
→ seven independently evaluated AI-text detectors misclassified non-native English writers' unaided work as AI-generated at roughly twelve times the rate they misclassified native English writers' unaided work
→ an integrity mechanism with that large and systematically population-skewed an error rate is not safe to use as disciplinary evidence without independent corroboration, however convenient it is to deploy at scale

**Confidence:** HIGH
No GT-N? input feeds this chain; GT-3 is read-at-source with an exact quoted location.

### Conclusion C3: A naive take-home swap — no redesign, integrity resting on the automated detection C2 already rules out — cannot reliably certify that submitted work reflects the individual student's own unassisted capability

GT-2? (capable LLMs can produce unaided-passing prose) + C2 (detectors unsafe as integrity mechanism)
→ a student seeking undisclosed AI assistance on an untimed, unsupervised take-home can obtain it at negligible cost and produce work a human grader cannot reliably distinguish from unaided work *[Assumes: A13 — human graders cannot reliably detect AI-assisted prose by ordinary reading, unverified]*
→ the institution's only mechanism that scales to large enrollments without major added staff time is the same automated detection this analysis has already ruled out as unsafe *[Assumes: A14 — no comparably scalable non-detector mechanism exists, challenged as overstated]*
→ the take-home format therefore loses its own verification purpose for whatever fraction of students uses undisclosed assistance, without a safe way to find out which fraction that is

**Confidence:** MEDIUM
GT-2? drives the downgrade. Raising this chain to HIGH would require a controlled benchmark study measuring how reliably graders detect undisclosed LLM assistance in take-home submissions, which was not fetched this session.

### Conclusion C4: Neither format is equity-neutral — each format's integrity or equity property is a direct consequence of the same structural feature that produces its own distinct penalty, so switching formats trades one inequity axis for another rather than removing inequity

GT-4 (timed exams institutionally mature) + GT-5? (timed conditions penalize disability/anxiety/language) + GT-6? (home resources/access unequal)
→ live invigilation substantially reduces real-time access to an external LLM assistant during the exam window, a structurally different threat model from an unmonitored take-home *[Assumes: A15 — invigilation is reliably effective, challenged: it reduces but does not eliminate device-based assistance]*
→ that same fixed-duration, live-monitored condition is the mechanism that produces the documented penalty against students with processing-speed disabilities, test anxiety, or non-native-language processing time
→ removing that condition in an unmonitored take-home does not simply remove a penalty, it substitutes a different one, because reliable home internet, private study space, and paid-tier AI-assistant access are not evenly distributed across a student population
→ each format's integrity or equity property traces to the same structural feature that produces its own distinct penalty, so switching formats trades one inequity axis for another rather than eliminating inequity

**Confidence:** MEDIUM
GT-5? and GT-6? drive the downgrade. Raising toward HIGH would require a direct reading of the governing accommodation statute/case law and of specific digital-divide survey data, neither done this session.

### Conclusion C5: Retaining timed exams unexamined is unsafe as an institution-wide default, but a bounded, course-justified unaided component remains independently defensible for foundational-fluency or safety-critical constructs

GT-8? (signaling theory: credential = costly-to-fake signal) + C1 (no format alone reaches the ceiling) + GT-9? (retrieval practice favors unassisted recall for foundational learning)
→ if the capability a program actually needs to certify is producing excellent work using the tools available in practice, a tools-banned timed exam does not measure that capability at all, however well it is administered
→ a credential that certifies the wrong capability is not a weaker signal of the right one, it is simply not a signal of it, which is a different and stronger failure than ordinary measurement noise
→ this failure is guaranteed whenever a program keeps its assessment format fixed without first naming, per course, which capability its stakeholders actually need certified, an exception being foundational-knowledge components where cognitive-science evidence favors unassisted effortful recall for durable learning in its own right
→ retaining timed exams unexamined is unsafe as an institution-wide default, but a bounded, course-justified unaided component remains independently defensible wherever the target is foundational fluency or safety-critical unaided judgment

**Confidence:** MEDIUM
GT-8? and GT-9? drive the downgrade. Raising toward HIGH would require direct readings of Spence (1973) and of Roediger & Karpicke rather than reliance on their status as widely-taught theory.

### Conclusion C6: An unmanaged naive take-home swap harms the credential's market value for every graduate of a program, not only for the students who use undisclosed assistance

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Use AI-text detectors as the take-home integrity mechanism

**What was tried:** Considered recommending automated AI-detector screening (Turnitin/GPTZero-style tools) as the practical integrity backbone for a take-home format.

**Why abandoned:** Contradicts GT-3 directly — the read-at-source figures (61.22% mean FPR on non-native-speaker essays vs 5.19% on native-speaker essays) show this actively produces biased wrongful accusations against exactly the population a university most needs to protect from that harm.

**What it ruled out:** Any recommendation branch that treats detector output as sufficient disciplinary evidence; this pushed the design toward live human verification (chain C7) instead.

### Dead End: A flat institution-wide "yes, switch everyone to take-home" or "no, keep exams everywhere" policy

**What was tried:** Attempted to answer the prompt's binary framing directly, as a single policy recommendation.

**Why abandoned:** Assumption classification (A9) showed this rests on a false premise — different courses/programs legitimately certify different constructs (safety-critical unaided recall vs. tool-augmented judgment vs. research synthesis) — so a blanket policy is a category error regardless of which format "wins" on average.

**What it ruled out:** Any single-format universal verdict; this is why §6 below is conditional rather than a flat answer.

### Dead End: Remote webcam/lockdown-browser proctoring as the take-home integrity fix

**What was tried:** Considered recommending remote-surveillance proctoring tools as a substitute for in-person invigilation on take-home work.

**Why abandoned:** This substitutes one equity/privacy problem (surveillance access requirements, false-flagging of legitimate behavior, conflicts with disability accommodation) for another rather than resolving the underlying verification problem; pursuing it to the same depth as the oral-defense alternative was out of scope for reaching a validated conclusion at reasonable depth, and the oral-defense route (GT-7, chain C7) already had long institutional precedent to build on instead.

**What it ruled out:** Treating "add more surveillance" as a costless technical fix; this narrowed the redesign recommendation to live human verification specifically.

### Dead End: Weighting student wellbeing much more heavily in the trade-off analysis

**What was tried:** Considered re-running chain C7's weighted trade-off with a much higher wellbeing weight, which would favor take-home options more strongly.

**Why abandoned:** The sensitivity check in chain C7 showed the ranking of the top two options (both already take-home-positive designs) does not hinge on this weight — reweighting would only change the already-decided ranking of the two losing options, not the substantive recommendation.

**What it ruled out:** Further manual tuning of the trade-off weights as a way to manufacture a cleaner-looking result; the near-tie between the top two options was accepted as a genuine finding rather than smoothed away.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Reject the flat "timed exams vs. take-home" binary and choose format per course-level construct rather than by one institution-wide policy (chain C1); replace an unchanged timed-exam-only default with a redesigned, disclosed-AI-use assessment carrying a live verification component, or a bounded hybrid, rather than a naive take-home swap (chain C7). Concretely:

- Yes, replace timed exams with a redesigned take-home — disclosed/permitted AI use, graded on judgment and verification of AI output, plus a live oral-defense or spot-check — wherever the certified construct is synthesis, research, or judgment rather than unaided recall, and the institution can sustain oral-assessment capacity at that course's enrollment scale (chain C7).
- No, do not adopt a naive take-home swap that keeps essay-style grading unchanged and relies on AI-text detectors for integrity, because those detectors carry a documented, population-skewed error rate that makes them unsafe as disciplinary evidence (chains C2, C3).
- No, do not keep a timed, tools-banned exam unexamined for a course whose real target capability is tool-augmented work product, because that measures a construct the program no longer needs certified (chain C5).
- Yes, retain a bounded timed component, inside a hybrid design, specifically for courses whose target construct is safety-critical unaided judgment or foundational fluency where cognitive-science evidence favors unassisted recall in its own right — and pair it with the accommodation framework such formats already require (chains C5, C4).

**Key insight:** The arrival of capable LLMs doesn't make take-home assessments unsafe and timed exams safe, or the reverse, in the abstract — it removes a quiet shortcut each format was already relying on. Take-home format assumed unmonitored prose was a safe proxy for individual ability, an assumption chains C2 and C3 show is now false and not safely repairable by automated detection. Timed-exam format assumed unaided time-pressured recall was still the construct worth certifying, an assumption chain C5 shows is true only for a narrower set of courses than institutions currently apply it to. Both hidden assumptions break under the identical pressure, and the fix in both directions is the same: verify via live human judgment rather than either an unaided-writing proxy or an automated-detection proxy, matched to what each course actually needs to certify (chain C7).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This recommendation costs real faculty time and redesign investment that the unchanged status quo does not (chain C7), and carries a genuine, disclosed risk of decaying back toward either replaced failure mode within a few years absent sustained institutional commitment (chain C8). It also leaves one residual risk this analysis did not resolve: live oral-defense verification may reintroduce its own bias — for example against accented speech or interview-anxious students — at a severity not measured here against GT-3's documented detector-bias figure (chain C7). The choice between a fully redesigned take-home and a hybrid retaining a bounded timed slice is itself a near-tie this analysis cannot resolve in the abstract; it turns on an institution-specific oral-assessment-capacity fact this analysis was not given (chain C7).

**Confidence:** MEDIUM — driven by GT-2?, GT-5?, GT-6?, GT-8?, and GT-9?, the unverified inputs feeding chains C1, C3, C4, C5, and C6; each chain's own confidence line names the specific verification that would raise it to HIGH. Chain C2 (detector unreliability) is the one component of this analysis that already stands at HIGH, resting on a figure read directly at source.
