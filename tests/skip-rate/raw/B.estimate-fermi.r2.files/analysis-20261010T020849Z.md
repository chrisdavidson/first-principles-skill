## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Does a bottom-up, constituent-unit-cost build-up of the installed and
cycle-life-amortized cost of a 5 MWh molten-salt thermal energy storage (TES) system show it to
be cost-competitive with lithium-ion battery storage at that scale, and — since scale is known to
dominate TES economics — under what duration, cycle-life, round-trip-efficiency, and deployment-scale
conditions does each technology actually win?

**Success criteria:**
1. An installed $/kWh (thermal storage capacity) figure for the 5 MWh system is derived from named
   constituent unit costs (salt mass from specific heat/ΔT, tank $/kg or $/m³, BOP, EPC/contingency)
   rather than quoted from a single industry benchmark, and is shown as an explicit build-up with ranges.
2. That figure is converted to a $/kWh-delivered levelized cost under stated cycle life, calendar life,
   round-trip efficiency, and O&M assumptions, on an explicitly stated thermal-only-vs-electricity-delivered
   basis (both bases are shown where they differ materially).
3. The levelized figure is compared numerically, at the same scale and on the same electricity-in/
   electricity-out basis, against a comparably-built lithium-ion levelized figure, yielding an explicit
   competitiveness verdict plus the conditions (duration, cycle count, scale, charging-energy source)
   under which each technology wins.
4. The analysis explicitly tests whether a standalone 5 MWh molten-salt system is representative of how
   the technology is actually deployed, and surfaces any scale/application mismatch as a named caveat
   rather than silently assuming the premise is realistic.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Using the USGS "solar salt" commodity price as the TES media price

**What was tried:** An early search surfaced USGS Mineral Commodity Summary figures for "solar salt" at
roughly $128–153/metric ton (2021–2025) and this was briefly considered as the salt-media unit cost.

**Why abandoned:** "Solar salt" in the USGS mineral-commodity sense is evaporated sodium-chloride
(sea/rock salt used for water softening, food, and de-icing) — a completely different material from the
NaNO3/KNO3 nitrate eutectic ("solar salt" in the CSP/TES sense) this analysis is costing. The two share a
name and nothing else; using the sodium-chloride price would have understated the TES media cost by
roughly 5–10×.

**What it ruled out:** Treating any commodity-salt price index as a proxy for nitrate-eutectic TES media
pricing without checking which "salt" the source actually means. GT-4 (the Reuters Events nitrate-salt
range) and the NREL/SAM report's own caution about the ammonia-PPI surrogate (read in the same source as
GT-5/GT-6) were used instead.

### Dead End: Using the global $125/kWh Li-ion figure as the US comparison baseline

**What was tried:** GT-9's $125/kWh (Ember, October 2025) was initially considered as the headline
lithium-ion installed-cost figure for chain C5.

**Why abandoned:** The source explicitly scopes this figure to "markets outside China and the U.S."; US
installed costs are reliably higher due to tariffs, labor rates, and interconnection costs. Using the
single cheapest global data point as the US baseline would have manufactured an unrealistically wide
competitiveness gap in lithium-ion's favor.

**What it ruled out:** Cherry-picking the most favorable available benchmark for one side of a
comparison. GT-9 is retained in the Ground Truths list as a documented low-end global anchor, but chain
C5 uses the wider, US-relevant GT-8? range instead.

### Dead End: Applying the formal weighted Trade-off procedure to the TES-vs-Li-ion choice

**What was tried:** Considered opening the Trade-off procedure (locked criteria weights, anchored 1–5
scoring) to formalize the TES-vs-Li-ion comparison.

**Why abandoned:** The comparison already resolves on quantitative levelized-cost derivation chains
across duration, scale, and efficiency regimes (chains C1–C8); it is not a qualitative multi-criteria
option choice that benefits from locked weights and anchored scores. Forcing a weighted-score matrix onto
an already-quantified cost comparison would add notation without adding information.

**What it ruled out:** A second, parallel scoring exercise that would have restated chain C6's
conclusion in a different, less transparent format. (Recorded again in the Techniques-not-applied
process-output block.)

### Dead End: Applying formal Pre-Mortem instead of Inversion as the Phase 5 adversarial technique

**What was tried:** The user's request explicitly asked for "inversion/pre-mortem thinking." Both
techniques were considered for the Phase 5 adversarial pass.

**Why abandoned:** This analysis's conclusion is a claim (a cost-competitiveness verdict), not a specific
deployment plan or recommendation to build a named system; the methodology's decision rule routes a claim
to Inversion and a plan to Pre-Mortem. Pre-Mortem was therefore not applicable as the *formal* Phase 5

**What it ruled out:** Running a plan-stress-test procedure against an artifact (a plan to build the

### Dead End: Treating the NREL/SAM $27–35.5/kWh-t utility figure as the final answer

**What was tried:** Early in the research, the NREL/SAM reference-plant figure (GT-5) was the single

**Why abandoned:** The user explicitly requested a bottom-up build from constituent unit costs rather

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Use the bottom-up build as the basis for decision-making, not a quoted
benchmark: installed cost for the 5 MWh storage-only system comes to roughly $281/kWh_t central (range
$146–454/kWh_t) (chain C1). Costed as a grid-charged electric battery, this levelizes to roughly
$758/MWh delivered central (range $308–1,840/MWh) (chain C4), against roughly $162/MWh delivered central
(range $78–302/MWh) for a comparable 5 MWh lithium-ion system (chain C5) — so for a short-duration,
grid-electricity-charged, megawatt-hour-scale deployment, lithium-ion is the cost-competitive choice, not
molten-salt TES (chain C6).

**Key insight:** The uncompetitiveness is not a materials or chemistry problem — the salt itself is cheap,
roughly $48,000 of the ~$1.40M installed cost (chain C1) — it is two compounding scale penalties: a
fixed-cost-dominated tank/balance-of-plant floor that does not shrink proportionally with capacity (chains
C1, C2), and a small-turbine thermodynamic penalty that leaves a tiny power block capturing only about a
third of the Carnot ceiling versus two-thirds at utility scale (chain C3). Together these are why the same
technology that costs ~$27–36/kWh-t at utility scale costs roughly 9× more per kWh-t at 5 MWh (chain C2),
and roughly 4.7× more per MWh delivered than lithium-ion at this scale (chain C6) — a naive comparison of
raw media cost alone would have missed both penalties entirely.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This conclusion accepts LOW confidence on the exact dollar figures — the
dominant cost drivers (tank fabrication, balance-of-plant, small power-block cost) are this analysis's own
unverified synthesis rather than vendor quotes (chains C1, C4) — in exchange for a transparent,
re-derivable bottom-up build instead of a single quoted benchmark, per the user's explicit request. It
also deprioritizes the more favorable thermal-only/process-heat use case (chain C8), which answers a
different question than "vs. lithium-ion battery storage," and sets aside the finding that no standalone
grid-charged product of this type appears to exist in the market (chain C7): the verdict is necessarily
a synthetic estimate illustrating why the industry does not build systems this way, not a
market-benchmarked quote.

**Confidence:** LOW — matches the weakest contributing chains (C1, C2, C4, C5, C6 and C8, all LOW; C3
