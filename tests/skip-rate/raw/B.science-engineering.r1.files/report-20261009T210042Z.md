## 1. Problem Essence

**Essence Statement:** What minimum PV array capacity (W, STC-rated) and LiFePO4 battery bank capacity (kWh, nameplate) will reliably supply a year-round, off-grid, 2-adult cabin at 35°N high-desert New Mexico through its worst solar month (December), including a 3-day no-sun autonomy reserve — once realistic cumulative system losses and a *correctly verified* daily load (not just the as-given 1.5 kWh/day placeholder) are accounted for?

**Success criteria — a correct answer must:**

1. State a specific recommended panel array size (W, STC) and battery bank size (kWh, nameplate), each with a stated uncertainty bracket — not a false-precision point value.
2. Derive sizing from the worst-month (December) solar resource, not the annual average.
3. Build up a combined, itemized system-loss (derate) factor from named physical/engineering components — not assume near-100% efficiency.
4. Account explicitly, in the battery sizing, for LiFePO4 usable depth-of-discharge, discharge-path efficiency, and cold-temperature capacity effects.
5. Sanity-check the recommendation against at least one independent cross-check (panel area, weight, cost order of magnitude, or comparison to real-world off-grid cabin systems) capable of catching a 10× error.
6. Explicitly evaluate whether the given 1.5 kWh/day load figure is plausible for 2 full-time occupants against a bottom-up appliance estimate, and state whether the final sizing trusts, adjusts, or brackets that figure — without being required to land on exactly 1.5 kWh/day, exactly one named option, or any other pre-specified shape.

This is a sizing *derivation*, not a choice among qualitatively different off-grid system architectures (chemistry, grid-tie, generator-hybrid) — those are fixed by the prompt (LiFePO4, no grid) or out of scope. The one place a genuine multi-option decision surfaces (battery thermal placement) is treated as a second-order design finding in §4, not a separate trade-off deliverable, because the user's question is "how big," not "which architecture."

## 4. Derivation Chains

### C1 — Corrected design daily load (Estimate / Fermi rebuild)

Target quantity: daily electrical energy consumption (kWh/day) for 2 full-time adult occupants, winter-inclusive. Unit-factors: appliance power (W) × daily duty (h), summed, converted to kWh.

```text
GT-9? (cabin appliance load benchmarks)
→ lighting (≈240 Wh, winter duty), well/pressure pump (≈530 Wh), an efficient compressor fridge (≈700 Wh central), and electronics for 2 adults (≈400 Wh) sum with a winter-duty margin (≈150 Wh) to a no-internet central load near 2.0 kWh/day
→ adding a satellite-internet allowance (≈300 Wh/day, common at remote cabins) raises the central estimate to about 2.2 kWh/day, bracketed [1.8, 3.0] kWh/day pending confirmation of internet and fridge size
→ [Assumes: A-2] this bottom-up total assumes no electric-resistance space or water heating is ever drawn from the system; if that assumption is wrong the figure above is invalid regardless of the appliance list
→ the design daily load should be corrected upward from the given 1.5 kWh/day to a central estimate of ≈2.2 kWh/day (bracket 1.8–3.0), with the given 1.5 kWh/day figure sitting below even the no-internet, modest-fridge low end of this bottom-up build
```

**Decision-resolution check (estimate procedure):** the bracket's low end (1.8 kWh/day) and high end (3.0 kWh/day) do **not** drive the same sizing decision — they scale the downstream array/battery recommendation by roughly 2×. Per the stop criterion, this uncertainty is escalated explicitly rather than papered over with false precision: the 2.2 kWh/day figure is a working central estimate pending an on-site appliance audit, not a settled number.

**Confidence:** LOW — two axes short. *Inputs:* GT-9? is reported-by-delegate, not read at a specific appliance datasheet for this cabin; closed by an on-site 1–2 week kWh audit or confirmed appliance model numbers. *Rivals:* the rival "1.5 kWh/day as given is correct" stays live under a narrow surviving condition — no fridge at all and no internet — which this chain cannot rule out without site confirmation (see §5 Rival). *Inference:* the one named dependency (A-2, no electric heating) is explicitly priced as the highest-blast-radius assumption in §2, so this axis is not counted short.

### C5 — Theoretical-limit and order-of-magnitude sanity check

```text

→ the recommended array (≈1,000 W) and battery (≈9–10 kWh) pass the order-of-magnitude sanity check on area, weight, and cost, and sit within the range of real-world off-grid cabin systems reported for comparable or larger daily loads (GT-9?); no 10× sizing error is evident in either direction
```

**Theoretical-limit bracket (direction: higher module efficiency is better → ideal ceiling):**
- **Ideal ceiling:** ≈29–33% (Shockley–Queisser single-junction limit, GT-12, physical law)
- **Best demonstrated:** low-to-mid 20s % (commercial record modules, GT-11?, observed)
- **Conventional:** ≈20% (bulk commercial product, GT-11?, used in C3/C5)
- **Gap 1 (conventional → best demonstrated):** ≈20%→~23–24%, modest, mostly manufacturing-cost convention, not physics

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

*What was tried:* an initial pass considered sizing the array against New Mexico's annual-average peak sun hours (≈6.4 h/day, GT-1?) for simplicity, since it is the headline figure most solar calculators surface first.
*Why abandoned:* Phase 1 success criterion (2) explicitly requires worst-month sizing, and using the annual average would leave the system short exactly in the season this prompt flagged as the risk — lower sun hours coinciding with potentially higher load.

*What it ruled out:* the substantive finding was preserved instead as chain C4's third-order extension (§4) and as a named Disposition item in the Phase 5 adversarial-pass record (§ Appendix), rather than as a separate weighted-criteria section — see the Techniques-not-applied disclosure for the formal trigger decision.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Size the system to a corrected central daily load of ≈2.2 kWh/day rather than the given 1.5 kWh/day placeholder: a ≈1,000 W STC PV array (bracket 720–1,480 W) on a winter-favoring fixed tilt, paired with a ≈9–10 kWh nameplate LiFePO4 battery bank (≈8–9 kWh usable at 90% DoD, bracket 6.7–17.9 kWh), with the battery bank installed in a thermally protected enclosure as a hard precondition rather than an optional upgrade (chain C3, chain C4).

**Key insight:** The daily-load estimate — not the solar resource, the derate chain, or the battery chemistry mechanics — is the single input that moves every downstream number by roughly 2×; the given 1.5 kWh/day figure sits below even the most load-light, no-internet end of a bottom-up appliance rebuild once any refrigeration is included (chain C1, chain C3).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** Sizing to the corrected ≈2.2 kWh/day load roughly doubles upfront array/battery cost and weight versus sizing to the given 1.5 kWh/day figure, but undersizing risks a winter brownout precisely when recovery is hardest — short days, cold nights, and a possible battery charge lockout below 0 °C (chain C4). Thermally protecting the battery adds cost and complexity but removes the single most severe failure mode identified in this analysis (chain C4).

**Confidence:** LOW overall, resting on chains C1, C2, C3, C4, and C5. The band is dominated by the unresolved daily-load estimate (C1) rather than by the engineering derates or battery-chemistry mechanics, which converge reasonably well across independent sources (C2, cross-validated by GT-8?). This would rise toward MEDIUM or HIGH with: (1) an on-site appliance audit confirming the cabin's actual daily load, (2) a direct NREL PVWatts run for the exact cabin coordinates and tilt rather than an Albuquerque proxy, and (3) manufacturer datasheets for the specific charge controller, inverter, and battery product selected, read at source rather than reported by a search/fetch delegate.
