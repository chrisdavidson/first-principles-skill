## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Sizing against the annual-average solar resource
*What was tried:* an initial pass considered sizing the array against New Mexico's annual-average peak sun hours (≈6.4 h/day, GT-1?) for simplicity, since it is the headline figure most solar calculators surface first.
*Why abandoned:* Phase 1 success criterion (2) explicitly requires worst-month sizing, and using the annual average would leave the system short exactly in the season this prompt flagged as the risk — lower sun hours coinciding with potentially higher load.
*What it ruled out:* ruled out by GT-1?'s December-specific figure (4.4 h) being adopted directly in chain C3's first hop instead of the annual figure.

### Dead End: Anchoring battery depth-of-discharge to the lead-acid 50% convention
*What was tried:* an initial anchor on the familiar 50% DoD rule of thumb common in off-grid lead-acid sizing guides.
*Why abandoned:* the prompt specifies LiFePO4 explicitly, and GT-3? establishes that LiFePO4 safely supports 80–95% DoD; continuing with a 50% assumption would have overstated the required nameplate battery capacity by roughly 1.8×. Wrong-direction (oversizing) but still a wrong number.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Size the system to a corrected central daily load of ≈2.2 kWh/day rather than the given 1.5 kWh/day placeholder: a ≈1,000 W STC PV array (bracket 720–1,480 W) on a winter-favoring fixed tilt, paired with a ≈9–10 kWh nameplate LiFePO4 battery bank (≈8–9 kWh usable at 90% DoD, bracket 6.7–17.9 kWh), with the battery bank installed in a thermally protected enclosure as a hard precondition rather than an optional upgrade (chain C3, chain C4).
