## 1. Problem Essence

**Essence Statement:** Should this company — $2.4M ARR, 240 paying teams, ~$10,000 average ACV, acquired entirely through outbound sales and referrals, with no self-serve inbound channel — add a free tier, given that (a) free-tier infrastructure/support cost scales with *active* users regardless of whether they ever pay, and (b) no historical free-to-paid conversion data exists for this product?

**Success criteria — a correct answer must:**
- Name the specific job a free tier would do for *this* company's actual go-to-market motion (distribution mechanism, competitive-parity signal, PLG motion, or something else), not a generic "freemium is good practice" claim.
- Produce an explicit cost/NPV model showing what conversion rate, cost-per-active-free-user, and funnel volume would need to be true for a free tier to pay for itself, anchored to the $10K ACV figure.
- Address the named second-order risks (sales cannibalization, support load, competitive-parity reasoning) rather than ignoring them.
- Identify the cheapest way to obtain real conversion signal before committing capital to a permanent free tier.
- Give one recommendation — which may be conditional — with explicit, falsifiable conditions that would flip it in either direction. No success criterion requires the answer to be exactly "add" or "don't add"; a conditional path is an admissible and, in this case, likely answer.

A skeptic test: the triggering event was presumably "competitors all have one, should we get one too" — but the real question is not "do we need the feature" but "does this company's demand-generation and sales motion give a free tier anything to do." That is the question this analysis answers.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Skip the pilot, launch full freemium now

**What was tried:** Treat "skip the pilot and go straight to a full self-serve freemium launch, since every named competitor already validated the model" as the leading option.
**Why abandoned:** This conflates two distinct claims — "freemium works for category X" and "freemium works for *this company's* GTM motion." C1 shows the structural precondition (an inbound funnel to convert) is absent regardless of what competitors proved elsewhere; C3 shows the competitor-parity signal itself is weak (mimicry/survivorship, no win/loss evidence); C5's trade-off scoring ranks this option lowest of the four on signal quality, cost exposure, and reversibility (score 34 vs. 102 for the trial option).
**What it ruled out:** Full freemium launch (option C) as the near-term move; it remains a theoretically available later step only after the inbound-funnel precondition is separately solved and the pilot (C6) clears its thresholds.

### Dead End: Narrow-ICP perpetual free tier as the first move

**What was tried:** Treat the narrow-ICP perpetual free tier (option D — e.g., free for a non-core segment such as single-seat or hobbyist users) as a way to get real freemium signal without touching the core sales motion.
**Why abandoned:** It still requires building meaningful self-serve infrastructure (A-8 — this is not "just a pricing page"), carries GT-3?'s open-ended cost exposure pattern (no time box), and the resulting conversion signal would come from a segment that is not the $10K-ACV buyer GT-1? is anchored to — so it answers a narrower, less decision-relevant question than C5's chosen option. Trade-off scoring (C5) ranked it third of four (61 vs. 102).
**What it ruled out:** A narrow-ICP permanent free tier as the *first* move; it remains a plausible *second* experiment if the core-motion trial (C6) succeeds and a genuinely distinct underserved segment is later identified.

### Dead End: Permanent inaction as the final answer

**What was tried:** Treat "do nothing, indefinitely" (option A, status quo) as an acceptable permanent answer rather than a temporary default.

**Why abandoned:** Status quo scores well on every axis except the two that matter for resolving GT-4?'s actual gap — Signal quality and Speed to data (score 1 of 5 on each) — meaning it preserves optionality but never retires the uncertainty the user explicitly asked this analysis to address. C5's trade-off result (B beats A by 19 points, robust under the flip test) shows a bounded trial strictly dominates indefinite inaction once cost-exposure is controlled for.
**What it ruled out:** Permanent inaction as the final answer; it remains correct only as the fallback if C2's pessimistic-cost bracket is confirmed (see Section 6's flip condition for an outright "no").

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Do not launch a permanent or full self-serve free tier now. Instead, run a time-boxed (≈30-day) full-feature trial attached to sales-qualified outbound and referral leads — reusing the existing funnel rather than building a new one — with pre-committed go/no-go conversion and cost thresholds and four named, owned tripwires (decision-date discipline, sales-cycle-length delta, cost-per-user overrun, extension-request rate) (chain C6). Use the pilot's measured results to replace this analysis's estimated and unverified cost-model inputs (chain C2) with real, sourced numbers before deciding on anything broader.

**Key insight:** A free tier is a *conversion* mechanism, not a *demand-generation* mechanism — its revenue impact is the product of funnel volume, conversion rate, and converted LTV, and if funnel volume is zero the other two terms are irrelevant (chain C1). This company's GTM motion (outbound + referral) currently supplies that volume at or near zero for a public free tier, which is why "add a free tier" is the wrong frame; "get a company-specific conversion-rate measurement cheaply, inside the motion that already works" (chain C5/C6) is the right one.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The recommended trial is not free of risk — it carries real, if bounded, sales-cycle-lengthening and cost-overrun exposure (chain C4), and it deliberately defers the two biggest upside scenarios (a real inbound/PLG motion, or full competitive parity) rather than capturing them immediately. It also does not resolve the "competitors all have one" perception question (chain C3) — a prospect who wants to self-serve evaluate today still cannot, during the trial design and rollout window.
