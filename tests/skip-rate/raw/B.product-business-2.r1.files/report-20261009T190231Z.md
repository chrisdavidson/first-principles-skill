## 1. Problem Essence

**Essence Statement:** Given that this quarter's build capacity (3.2 engineer-quarters, net of on-call/maintenance) funds exactly one of two mutually exclusive investments, which investment — the Slack integration or the reporting rewrite — should the team fund, and under what conditions would that choice reverse?

**Success criteria (a correct answer must achieve):**
- Names which option(s) to fund this quarter — a single option, a sequenced pair, or a descoped composite — not merely restating the two named choices without testing whether a split/sequence beats either alone.
- Separates verified/stipulated facts from inferred or assumed claims, and shows the classification work rather than asserting it.
- Quantifies, or explicitly states the limits of quantifying, the hard-dollar risk on one side against the soft/unmeasured pipeline opportunity cost on the other.
- States a confidence band for the recommendation and names the specific evidence or event that would flip it.
- Does not require the answer to be exactly "Option A" or exactly "Option B" to the exclusion of a viable combination — a composite or sequenced path is tested as a candidate, not foreclosed by the framing.

Capacity fit is scored as a weighted criterion instead (Schedule feasibility, below).

**Criteria & weights, locked before scoring:**

| Quantified near-term $ downside avoided | 5 | no quantified $ figure attached | a specific, contractually-dated $ figure directly addressed |

**Flip test:** the largest single-criterion drivers of D's 13-point margin over B are Schedule feasibility (contributes +10 to the gap) and Reversibility (+6), partly offset by Demand signal (−6 in B's favor). Lowering the Schedule-feasibility weight to its floor (1) only closes the gap to 5 — D still wins. No single weight change within the 1–5 scale flips the result. This says the ranking is not sensitive to how the criteria are weighted; it says nothing about whether the descope-fits-in-budget estimate behind D's Schedule score, or the LOI figure behind both D's and B's $-downside score, are actually correct — the flip test varies weights, not scores, and does not repair an unverified input.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: A fabricated expected-value model for Option A

**What was tried:** Building a quantified revenue estimate for the Slack integration by assuming a conversion rate (requests → closed deals) and an average deal size, so Option A's upside could be compared dollar-for-dollar against Option B's $180k LOI figure.

**Why abandoned:** No conversion rate, deal-stage attribution, or average-deal-size figure was supplied anywhere in the inputs (GT-2?, GT-3?). Any conversion rate used would have been invented by this analysis, not drawn from a ground truth — exactly the reasoning-by-fabricated-analogy the methodology prohibits. Manufacturing a number to force a clean expected-value comparison would misrepresent confidence that does not exist.

**What it ruled out:** It rules out presenting a specific dollar figure for Option A's upside anywhere in this analysis. Chain C1 and C3 instead report the asymmetry in *what evidence exists* (one side has a dated contractual figure, the other does not) rather than a fabricated magnitude comparison.

### Dead End: Status quo (Option C) as the sole quarter commitment

**What was tried:** Treating "commit no engineering capacity this quarter, pursue an LOI grace-period conversation only" as a complete, standalone recommendation rather than a partial one.

**Why abandoned:** Scored in the trade-off matrix (chain C4), Option C totals 54 — behind both Option D (83) and Option B (70) — because it leaves the two non-LOI top-10 accounts' independently-cited renewal risk (GT-5?) and the long-run shared-infrastructure risk entirely unaddressed, even in the best case where the LOI's exit clause turns out to be soft.

**What it ruled out:** It rules out "wait and see" as a sufficient response on its own. Its cheapest component — confirming whether the LOI's exit clause is actually firm (testing assumption A-6) — survives as a zero-cost parallel action inside the recommended composite (Option D), rather than as the entire response.

### Dead End: Recommend Option B outright, full rewrite, no scoping spike

**What was tried:** Recommending the full reporting rewrite as scoped in the brief, committing the entire 3.2 EQ budget immediately without first validating that the scope fits.

**Why abandoned:** Chain C2 (LOW confidence) estimates a full rewrite at roughly 2.5–6 engineer-quarters using generic SaaS project patterns ([Assumes: A-14]) — the upper half of that range exceeds the entire quarterly budget. Committing blind against an estimate that already straddles the budget risks spending the whole quarter and still missing the LOI deadline, which would forfeit both the $180k ARR and the Slack opportunity cost simultaneously.

**What it ruled out:** It rules out treating "ship B" and "ship a full rewrite" as the same commitment. This is the rival chain C4 names and rules out directly — the 2-week scoping spike (Option D) dominates committing to the full rewrite blind, because it is a near-zero-cost way to learn whether the schedule risk in C2 is actually survivable before spending the capacity.

### Dead End: Recommend Option A outright

**What was tried:** Recommending the Slack integration as the sole quarter commitment, on the theory that broader top-of-funnel pipeline growth outweighs single-account retention risk.

**Why abandoned:** Chain C1 shows Option A's revenue case cannot be quantified from the given inputs, and chain C3 shows Option A carries no dollar figure at all against Option B's dated, contractual $180k figure. In the trade-off matrix (chain C4), Option A scores lowest of all four options (41), driven by zero coverage of the quantified $-downside and structural-risk criteria.

**What it ruled out:** It rules out treating the 41 raw Slack requests as revenue-equivalent to a signed LOI. The two signals are not comparable in kind without additional data this analysis does not have (see the first Dead End above).

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Fund a descoped reporting fix this quarter — not a ground-up rewrite, and not the Slack integration — preceded by a 2-week engineering scoping spike to confirm it fits the remaining budget, with any freed capacity applied to a minimal Slack MVP; run two zero-engineering-cost actions in parallel starting now: confirm with the LOI account whether the exit clause is actually firm, and have sales begin logging deduplicated, deal-linked Slack objections (chain C4, chain C5).

**Key insight:** The decisive asymmetry is not "41 requests vs. 3 tickets" — it is that one side of this decision has a specific, contractually-dated dollar figure attached and the other has none (chain C3); and the real threat to the reporting investment is not the choice to deprioritize Slack, it is a scope-vs-capacity mismatch that could miss the LOI deadline regardless of what else is chosen (chain C2).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This recommendation defers a full Slack-integration decision to next quarter without new data beyond what sales begins logging now, and accepts a moral-hazard risk that visibly rewarding "renewal risk" framing in support tickets may increase future ticket volume framed that way (chain C5). It also accepts that if the 2-week scoping spike shows even a descoped fix cannot meet the deadline, the $180k is likely lost regardless of what the team builds (chain C2) — no chain, flagged assumption only, for the residual possibility that the LOI account would have churned for unrelated reasons regardless of shipping.

**Confidence:** LOW — C2 is rated LOW because the full-rewrite effort estimate behind it rests on an industry-pattern benchmark ([Assumes: A-14]) rather than a real engineering scoping estimate, and that exact uncertainty is why this recommendation is "spike first, then descope" rather than "commit to the full rewrite now." C3 and C4 are each independently LOW: C3 because its `?`-marked ground-truth inputs (GT-2?, GT-4?, GT-5?) combine with a live, unsettled rival (the unmeasured Slack pipeline could in principle outweigh $180k/yr — raised in §5 but not ruled out); C4 because the same `?`-marked inputs combine with two unpriced load-bearing assumptions ([Assumes: A-9], [Assumes: A-15]). C5 is capped at C4's band. The verification that would lift C2 is the 2-week scoping spike itself: if it returns a descoped-fix estimate comfortably inside the 3.2 EQ budget, the successor estimate (not C2, which is specifically about the full rewrite) supports a higher-confidence version of this same recommendation; the verification that would lift C3 is a deduplicated, deal-linked Slack pipeline figure (chain C1); the verification that would lift C4 is the same scoping spike plus written confirmation that the descoped fix's scope covers the two non-LOI accounts' complaints (A-9). **This recommendation flips to Option A outright if** the spike shows even a descoped fix exceeds budget with no reduction path AND the LOI account confirms the exit clause is firm with no flexibility (chain C2, chain C4's ruled-out rival in §5) — in that joint case, engineering capacity cannot buy the outcome it would be spent to buy. **It flips toward a reconsidered Option A** if sales produces even a rough deduplicated request count plus one concrete example of a deal lost to the missing Slack integration, with attributable pipeline value approaching or exceeding $180k/yr (chain C1, chain C3) — that would remove the asymmetry chain C3 rests on.
