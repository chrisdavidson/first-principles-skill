## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** State the ceiling as a finite-rate-corrected Carnot bound, not naive Carnot and not Curzon-Ahlborn (chain C1, C2, C3): using the achievable steam turbine-inlet temperature (≈540°C / 813 K, after the unavoidable salt-to-steam approach loss) and a realistic, cooling-mode-dependent condenser temperature (≈333-340 K dry-cooled, ≈311-316 K wet-cooled), the operative law-permitted ceiling is **≈58% (dry-cooled desert siting) to ≈62% (wet-cooled)**, against an absolute, zero-power, unconditional bound of ≈64.4% and a Curzon-Ahlborn finite-power benchmark of only ≈35.5-38% (chain C2, C3).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The recoverable share requires capital-intensive upgrades (supercritical boilers/turbines, more feedwater-heater stages) that no molten-salt tower has yet built at the relevant scale (all cited plants are ≤150 MWe); the locked-in share is "locked" only relative to the water constraint — wet cooling would recover roughly 1-3 of those points at the cost of water consumption these sites were specifically sited to avoid; a hybrid wet/dry scheme (already deployed at Crescent Dunes, GT-4?) can capture part of that recovery at a fraction of full wet-cooling's water draw, so the dry-cooling share of the "locked-in" category is better read as locked in only for a pure-ACC design, not as an absolute floor (adversarial-pass finding, Cluster 3).
