## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Treating the five leadership-named drivers as mutually exclusive and scoring for a single winner

**What was tried:** An early framing scored offer gaps, pricing, onboarding, service quality, and competitive displacement against each other as if exactly one would be selected as "the" answer, matching the mandate's "find the root cause" phrasing.

**Why abandoned:** The inversion analysis in section 2 and chain C5 show the two CS-flagged signals are equally consistent with a shared cause or with independent compounding causes; nothing in the given facts rules out compounding, and chain C2's Fermi bracket shows the swing is small enough in absolute terms that a concentrated, non-company-wide cause is at least as plausible. **What it ruled out:** This saves a future analyst from re-running a single-winner framing on the same thin evidence (GT-7: no driver data yet exists) and getting a different, equally unfounded single answer.

### Dead End: Applying the theoretical-limit procedure to churn rate

**What was tried:** Considered naming a governing hard constraint and deriving a law-permitted "floor" churn rate, the way the companion theoretical-limit technique derives a Carnot-style ceiling.

**Why abandoned:** No governing physical, mathematical, or protocol-level constraint analogous to thermodynamics bounds a B2B SaaS churn rate. The closest substitute — an industry benchmark churn rate — is an observed-only comparator, not a derivable ceiling; using it as a "floor" would have been a model-dependent bound dressed as a physical one, exactly the failure mode the technique's own documentation warns against.

**What it ruled out:** This saves a future analyst from manufacturing a spurious "theoretical floor" for churn and treating industry benchmarks as more rigorous than they are.

### Dead End: Recommending an immediate, broad support-headcount increase as the first priority action

**What was tried:** The CS-flagged "rising ticket volume" signal makes hiring more support staff the most intuitively obvious first move, and an early draft of this analysis treated it as priority #1.

**What it ruled out:** This saves leadership from pre-committing a resource-intensive fix that both poisons its own future measurement and may be solving the wrong problem.
