The conceptual claims (Conway's Law, Fowler's "microservices premium") are grounded in named frameworks rather than raw assertion, but the quantitative backbone of the argument is presented as fact with no visible basis. This is **Hand-wavy**.

## Assumption surfacing

The single most consequential assumption — whether the 6 engineers already operate as informal sub-teams — is explicitly named as unstated in the prompt, and the document engages with it directly: it says precisely how the recommendation would flip if this assumption is false, and flags it as the one thing that could reverse the verdict. This is exactly the kind of engagement the top band asks for, applied to the assumption that matters most. **Rigorous**.

## Calibration

The document states an overall confidence level (Medium) and gives a reason tied to the one open variable, which is good practice. But this sits awkwardly against the false precision of the trade-off scores (101/93/88/56) and the specific-looking overhead percentages, which are presented with more numeric confidence than their (unshown) basis would support. The self-awareness is real but doesn't fully extend to the numeric claims that carry the argument. This is **Sound** rather than rigorous.

## Falsifiability

This is not a generic hedge; it names the actual conditions under which the recommendation reverses. **Rigorous**.
