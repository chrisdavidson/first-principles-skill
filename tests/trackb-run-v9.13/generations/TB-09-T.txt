The core question isn't "is joining a startup a good idea" in the abstract — it's whether *this* trade survives contact with two things: your downside tolerance and the specific startup's fundamentals.

**Runway math first.** An 11-month cushion at your *current* spending covers roughly 15-16 months at the new, lower income (30% cut stretches the buffer). That's real slack — enough to absorb the startup failing in year one without financial catastrophe — but you should treat the equity as worth $0 in this calculation. Illiquid, unpriced, easily diluted to nothing; if the decision only works when the equity pays off, it's not a decision, it's a bet.

**Then diligence the specific bet**, not the category: revenue/runway of the startup itself (how many months *they* have before they need to raise or die), why these four people, what you'd actually own (vested over what, cliff, strike price, what happens on a down-round), and whether your role is core to what makes the company survive or a nice-to-have that gets cut first.

**The tradeoff that actually matters at 34** isn't money, it's optionality cost: a year at a startup is reversible (you can return to stable employment, and "startup experience" reads well), but it's not free — it's a year of compounding lost to the lower salary and to whatever the stable job's growth path would have paid. The real question to answer before deciding: if this exact startup fails in 12 months and you're back on the job market with a thinner cushion, do you still think taking the swing was correct? If yes, the math supports it. If you'd regret the risk itself regardless of outcome, no equity upside fixes that.
