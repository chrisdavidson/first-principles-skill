## Pre-Mortem: Payments-Rewrite EU Launch

The premise: it's two weeks out, the launch failed, and we work backward to find why. The 15 individual failure causes collapse into **four structural weaknesses** — and the danger is that all four stack at once:

| Pattern | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A. No reversibility** | Payment writes (mandates, charges, tokens) can't be unwound with a feature flag. A hard cutover with no rollback is a **one-way door over irreversible financial state**. |
| **B. Maximum blast radius** | 100%-at-once removes the only cheap way to catch the unknown-unknowns every rewrite has. |
| **C. Invisible-until-later correctness** | The scariest bugs (reconciliation drift, missed webhooks, SCA edge cases, disputes) surface *days* later — so passing launch-day testing proves almost nothing. |
| **D. Worst recovery window** | Friday + skeleton weekend on-call = maximum time-to-recovery for an incident over irreversible state. |

### Recommended plan changes

**Must-fix (reversibility):** rehearsed rollback runbook accounting for divergent state; prove idempotency keys survive the cutover; verify token/SEPA-mandate migration against *real* data; build continuous new-vs-Stripe reconciliation with per-customer discrepancy reporting.

**Strongly recommended (blast radius):** replace hard cutover with a staged rollout (internal → 1% → 10% → 50% → 100%); load-test at EU-peak volume; stand up per-payment success-rate dashboards + alerting.

**Nearly free (recovery window):** **don't ship Friday** — ship Tue/Wed with full team and bank support available for the days the long-tail failures actually appear.

**Compliance gate:** confirm SCA/PSD2 3-D Secure against real EU bank flows, not Stripe sandbox.

**The headline:** the cheapest two changes (don't ship Friday, do a staged rollout) defuse two of the four patterns for almost no cost. But the one to resolve *before committing* is **A — can you actually roll back?** If the honest answer is no, that single finding should reshape the entire plan.
