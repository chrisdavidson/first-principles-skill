## Pre-Mortem: Authentication Rollout Failure Modes

The agent ran a **pre-mortem** — imagining the rollout has already failed and working backward — and flagged upfront that it was working *without your actual plan document*, so several findings rest on assumptions you should verify.

### The two structural weaknesses (the real findings)

1. **Synchronization is the dominant risk.** Authentication is a *total-failure* dependency (when it breaks, everything downstream is inaccessible), and in a synchronized cutover the slowest team gates the whole rollout. Combine those with big-bang sequencing and one team's problem becomes everyone's outage. **Recommendation: restructure away from big-bang — stagger by team, canary first, per-team go/no-go gates.**

2. **Adoption is the silently-suppressed killer.** Each team has its own roadmap; the auth migration is *your* priority, not theirs. Without executive air cover and named owners, integration work slips quietly. This feels "too obvious to plan for," which is exactly why pre-mortems catch it.

### Failure clusters identified
- **Coordination/sequencing** — hidden inter-team dependencies, no canary, big-bang blast radius
- **Adoption/people** — deprioritization, docs gaps, skills gap on the new auth model
- **Migration/data** — corrupted identity data, mass session logout, *silent* permission-mapping errors
- **Rollback/recovery** — no per-team rollback, or one that's untested when you need it
- **Schedule/quality** — fixed date + fixed scope means testing is the only variable left to sacrifice
- **Security/environment** — production misconfig (token expiry, CORS, redirect URIs), IdP becoming a new single point of failure

### Priority actions before locking the timeline
1. Stagger + canary instead of big-bang (HIGH confidence)
2. Treat rollback as a **tested, named-owner capability**, not an assumption — verify your plan actually has per-team rollback
3. Plan adoption like a project (owners, executive mandate, staffed support)
4. Protect the testing/security gate from deadline compression
5. Dry-run the identity/permission migration with reconciliation (permission errors fail silently)
