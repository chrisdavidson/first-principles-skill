*Derived from §6 and the structured summary of analysis-20261002T015836Z.md; the six sections remain the source of truth.*

In brief: The analysis tests whether a two-tank nitrate salt store cycled between 290 °C and 565 °C returns above 85% of the electricity used to charge it, and whether that would make it cost-competitive with lithium-ion (§1).

It examined the efficiency claim and the cost claim separately, because the claim joins them with "making it" (§1). The distinction matters because a 5 MWh thermal tank delivers about 1.98 MWh of electricity, not 5 MWh (C5).

The electric round trip is capped at 56.3% under resistive charging and at 70.3% when an ideal heat pump charges the store, both below 85% (C2, C3).

Overall confidence is MEDIUM, while every efficiency chain is HIGH (§6). Whether the store is cheaper per delivered kWh stays undetermined, resting on unverified salt and power-block costs (C7, GT-10?, GT-11?).

The recommendation is stated in the Answer block and §6, and rests on the efficiency chains (§6, C2, C3).
