# First-Principles Trade-Off Analysis: PostgreSQL vs MongoDB vs DynamoDB

**Recommendation: PostgreSQL (managed — AWS RDS Multi-AZ or Aurora)** — weighted score **88/110**, ahead of MongoDB (76) and DynamoDB (60).

## The reframe
The agent's key insight: **budget is not the binding constraint.** At 500 writes/sec (~1.3B writes/month), all three sit well under $3k — so the decision is about *fit*, not affordability:

| Option | Est. managed cost/month | Headroom vs $3k |
|---|---|---|
| PostgreSQL (RDS m5.large Multi-AZ) | ~$425 | Deep |
| MongoDB Atlas (M40) | ~$759 | Comfortable |
| DynamoDB (on-demand writes alone) | ~$800–1,600 (can breach $3k if items >1KB or GSIs replicate writes) | Tight — the only option where budget is in play |

Also, 500 w/s is a *low* write rate — it doesn't discriminate between any of the three engines, so raw throughput is a non-factor.

PostgreSQL wins on the two highest-weighted criteria (ACID transactional fit + query flexibility) while comfortably clearing cost and throughput. DynamoDB's genuine strengths (ops simplicity, scalability) are over-served at this load.

## Two caveats that could flip it (confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH)
The conclusion rests on two unverified assumptions about your workload:
1. **Do writes need cross-entity atomic transactions?** (cart → order → inventory → payment) — near-certain for e-commerce.
2. **Will you need ad-hoc / relational / reporting queries?** — this is the real pivot. If your access pattern is actually known-ahead single-key *and* you expect 50–100x scale growth, DynamoDB becomes competitive.

If yes to both → PostgreSQL is decisive. MongoDB is the reasonable runner-up if you expect a document-shaped, evolving schema with less relational reporting.
