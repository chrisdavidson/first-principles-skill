## When "faster ships → better retention" breaks down

The claim is a causal chain with **four hidden preconditions**. It breaks whenever any one fails:

1. **You're shipping at the binding constraint** — not at price, market fit, or wrong-fit churn. If retention is gated by something shipping can't touch, faster cadence moves a lever that isn't binding.

2. **Cadence stays below the quality/fatigue threshold** — past a point, defect rate and change-annoyance per ship overtake the value per ship, and the relationship *inverts* from positive to negative.

3. **Users discover and adopt what you ship** — improvements that land without onboarding/changelog/in-app surfacing don't move perception.

4. **Cadence is coupled to a measurement loop** — shipping every two weeks while learning what worked every two quarters decouples speed from outcome.

### The two flags worth acting on

- **Highest-stakes unknown:** Is your churn even *feature-addressable*? If a meaningful slice is price- or fit-driven, faster shipping is provably the wrong lever. **Verify first** by segmenting churn by cause.

- **Most dangerous mode for a healthy team (Regime 3b):** shipping every two weeks but learning every two quarters. You'd *feel* productive, the assumption would *feel* true, and it would be untestable. Fix: treat ship cadence and learning rate as separate things — only the learning rate drives retention.
