### The dominant failure pattern
**Correlated, all-at-once cutover with an untested rollback and unverified team capacity.** Because authentication is on the critical path for every app it fronts, failures are *total, not partial* — and a simultaneous all-teams cutover means one shared defect hits everyone at once, with no unaffected team to learn from.

### Five failure clusters
- **A — Big-bang correlated failure:** A single shared-layer defect locks out all teams simultaneously; no canary, so you find out in production. The slowest team also becomes the long pole.
- **B — Theoretical rollback:** A blocking bug hits, but the rollback was never actually tested and doesn't work → extended outage.
- **C — Dirty identity data:** Stale/mis-mapped users and roles cause lockouts or over-permissioned access (a security finding), and cleanup blows the schedule.
- **D — Team capacity / long pole:** The timeline assumes slack teams don't have; auth integration competes with their own roadmaps and slips.
- **E — Cutover-day operational gaps:** No clear incident owner when login breaks; help-desk unbriefed and overwhelmed by lockout/reset volume.

### Three gating preconditions (before locking the timeline)
