## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1:** Second Law of Thermodynamics → for any heat engine operating between a hot reservoir at Th and cold reservoir at Tc (both in Kelvin), maximum possible efficiency is the Carnot limit: η_Carnot = 1 − Tc/Th. (Physical law, irreducible.)
- **GT-2:** Solar salt (60% NaNO3 / 40% KNO3), the standard molten-salt storage medium, has a practical upper operating temperature of ~565°C (838 K) before accelerated corrosion/decomposition. Next-generation chloride salts push this to ~700–720°C (~973–993 K). (Current constraint, materials-science-bounded, cited to CSP/salt storage literature.)
- **GT-3:** Ambient heat rejection (condenser/cooling tower or dry cooling) sets the cold reservoir at roughly 288–313 K (15–40°C) depending on climate and cooling method; 300 K is a reasonable nominal midpoint. (Current constraint, environment-bounded.)
- **GT-4:** Curzon-Ahlborn endoreversible efficiency η_CA = 1 − √(Tc/Th) gives the efficiency at which a heat engine delivers *maximum power output* (not zero power, as Carnot requires) — this is the more physically realistic "attainable ceiling" for an actual working plant. (Physical law corollary, well-established in finite-time thermodynamics.)

## 6. Conclusion

The governing law is the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**, and the absolute physical ceiling on thermal-to-electric conversion is the **Carnot efficiency**, η = 1 − Tc/Th. For today's solar-salt molten storage (max ~565°C / 838 K) rejecting heat to ambient (~300 K), that ceiling is **≈64%**. For emerging higher-temperature chloride-salt systems (~700°C / 973 K), the ceiling rises to **≈69%**, and for advanced high-temperature particle/ceramic storage approaching 1000°C, it approaches **≈76%**.

However, Carnot efficiency requires infinitely slow (reversible) heat transfer — i.e., zero power output — so no working plant can approach it. The more physically meaningful "what can actually be built" bound is the **Curzon-Ahlborn finite-power-output efficiency**, η = 1 − √(Tc/Th), which comes out to **≈40%** for current solar-salt temperatures — strikingly close to what today's best CSP molten-salt plants (~38–42%) already achieve.

**Bottom line:** current engineering practice is not far below the realistic thermodynamic bound at today's salt temperatures — most of the apparent 24-point gap to the ~64% Carnot figure is an irreducible consequence of extracting nonzero power, not an engineering deficiency. The real, closeable lever for raising the ceiling is **hot-side temperature** (better salt/storage-medium chemistry), which shifts both the Carnot ceiling and the attainable Curzon-Ahlborn bound upward together.
