**Why abandoned:** This is analogy used as direct evidence, which D-07 disallows unless grounded in a verified ground truth about that specific other situation. **What was tried:** Initially retrieving Bureau of Labor Statistics establishment-survival data (all private-sector businesses, ~50% surviving to year 5) as a base rate for "will the startup still exist."

# 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Do not decide yet. Resolve three load-bearing unknowns, in this order, before applying any decision rule: (1) whether 70% of current pay still covers essential monthly burn — this determines whether the runway clock is even running during employment; (2) the startup's actual cash-in-bank, monthly burn, and realistic runway relative to the 12-month vesting cliff; and (3) the actual equity grant size, strike price, and vesting/acceleration terms in the real offer letter, not industry medians. If burn exceeds reduced income (the exposed case) and the resulting 5-to-8-month safe window does not comfortably clear both the startup's own runway and the cliff, or if the real equity/valuation numbers sit near the low end of the cited ranges, decline or renegotiate (a higher cash floor, a shorter cliff, or an explicit acceleration clause) rather than accept as offered (chain C6).

**Key insight:** The naive framing compares "30% pay cut" against "possible equity upside" as though they run on the same clock. They don't. Whether the runway is even at risk during employment depends on an unstated number (burn vs. reduced pay) that has nothing to do with the startup's odds of success (chain C1), and the vesting cliff can zero out the equity side of the bet entirely before the runway side is ever threatened (chain C4) — meaning the two halves of this trade can fail independently, on different clocks, for different reasons.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This framework cannot produce a single dollar-denominated verdict without inventing the equity percentage, valuation, and burn-rate figures this person did not supply (chain C3); it deliberately trades that false precision for an honest bracket. It also does not model household or psychological variance-tolerance, which the pre-mortem below found to be a real, separate failure mode: financial and structural clearance on this checklist is necessary but not sufficient — no chain — flagged assumption only (chain C6).
