## PART 2 — Plain-language summary for the family

**The five things that should actually drive this decision, in order:**

1. **How much help do your parents actually need right now — and is it likely to stay that way?** "A little help sometimes" and "needs someone most hours of the day" are completely different problems that call for completely different answers. Get an honest read on this (ideally from a doctor or professional care assessor, not just your own worried guess), and separately ask: is this a passing rough patch, or a new normal?

2. **Can paid help close the gap, even if you don't move?** Living five minutes away typically buys a working adult somewhere in the ballpark of 5–10 extra hours a week of hands-on help. That's genuinely useful for light support, but it does not come close to covering the hours needed once a parent requires real daily assistance. At that level of need, you're going to need paid care either way — the question is really "family nearby *plus* paid care" vs. "paid care alone, coordinated from a distance," not "moving vs. doing nothing."

3. **Can you actually afford the change — in money and in career risk — without it costing you your safety net?** Two job changes and a move happening at once is a much bigger risk than either alone. Be honest about what happens to your savings, your ability to find comparable work, and your stress levels if the job search takes longer than hoped.

4. **How would each of your specific kids handle this, given their actual age — not "kids are resilient" in general?** A move that's a non-event for a 6-year-old can be genuinely damaging for a 15-year-old in the middle of their social world. Look at each kid individually, not at "the children" as one unit.

5. **Can you undo this if you're wrong?** Before you sell the house, quit both jobs, and pull the kids out of school, ask: if Mom's health stabilizes, or improves, or — bluntly — if she passes away within the first year, will you look back and think this was still the right move for reasons beyond hers? Big, all-at-once, hard-to-reverse decisions are much riskier than smaller ones you can build on gradually.

**The actual test — a concrete yes/no you can apply:**

**Don't do anything drastic yet if any of these is true:**
- Your parents' need is currently mild (occasional help with errands, bills, appointments) rather than daily hands-on care, **and**
- Nobody has actually asked your parents whether *they* want you to move closer — you're assuming it, not confirming it, **and**
- You haven't priced out what local paid care or a care-coordination service would actually cost in their area.

**In that case, the right move right now is: stay put, and invest in local support instead** — hire help or a care manager near your parents, set up ways to check in on them remotely, and increase visit frequency. This is fully reversible, doesn't touch your jobs or your kids' schools, and directly targets the actual problem (not knowing what's happening day-to-day), which is usually the bigger driver than people realize.

**Escalate toward an actual move only when *all* of the following are true at the same time:**
- A professional assessment (or an unmistakable event — a fall, a hospitalization, a serious diagnosis) puts your parent at a "needs regular daily help" level or beyond, **and**
- You've priced local paid care and it either isn't available or isn't affordable at that level of need, **and**
- You can make the change while keeping your finances intact — and importantly, you sequence it so the truly irreversible steps (like selling your current home) happen *last*, only after a trial period has proven out, **and**
- You've checked in on each child individually, and none of them is at an age/stage where the move would do serious, specific harm without a correspondingly serious care need to justify it, **and**
- Your parents have actually said, out loud, that they want this — and if you have siblings, you've agreed in writing on who does what, so it doesn't quietly become "whoever lives closest does everything forever."

**And when it's time to escalate, ask in this order, not "family moves" by default:** first, could your parents move near *you* instead? (This avoids both job changes and both school changes entirely, and often wins on every practical measure — the only real obstacle is whether your parents are willing, which is worth asking directly before ruling it out.) Only if that's not workable does a full family relocation become the answer of last resort, not first resort.

**One honest caveat:** this test is built to minimize risk and regret. It will not tell you what to do if what you actually want — independent of the numbers — is more years of ordinary, everyday time with your parents while they're still well, even if the logistics don't "need" it yet. That's a legitimate thing to want. This framework can't make that choice for you; it can only make sure you're choosing it on purpose, with your eyes open to the cost, rather than backing into it by assumption.
