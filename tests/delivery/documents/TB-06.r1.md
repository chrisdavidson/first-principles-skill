## 3. Ground Truths

Step (1)–(2) are directly verifiable; step (3) bottoms out at a formal definition from classical test theory — it cannot be reduced further, only formalized — which is recorded below as GT-10. ### Conclusion C1: The binary framing in the question is a false dichotomy

GT-8 (employer credibility at stake) + GT-10 (X=T+E, validity ceiling <1 for any instrument)
→ the single word "assessment" is asked to do certification, feedback, motivation, ranking and accreditation work at once, and GT-10's validity ceiling applies separately to each of those five uses

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: "Wait for AI-detection technology to improve, then keep unmodified take-homes"

**What was tried:** Treating GT-1's detector unreliability as a temporary technical problem, and recommending that universities simply wait for third-party AI-detection tools to mature before trusting take-home submissions again.

**Why abandoned:** GT-1 shows no closing timeline for detector accuracy, and the fairness problem (61.3% false-positive rate for non-native English speakers, racial disparities of 20%/10%/7%) is a harm in its own right that a "wait and see" policy actively extends rather than avoids — a punitive detection regime built on a currently-biased tool would compound the equity problem C3 is trying to solve, not fix it.

**What it ruled out:** Any recommendation resting on "improved detection" as the primary fix for full take-home replacement (this is the rival named on C6's confidence line, ruled out here).

### Dead End 2: "The self-reported AI-misuse rate is low (9%), so credential devaluation is overblown"

**What was tried:** Using GT-7's finding that only 9% of AI-using students admit knowingly submitting AI-generated work in violation of policy as evidence that C2's certification-degradation concern is exaggerated.

**Why abandoned:** Signaling theory discounts a signal based on the third party's *inability to cheaply distinguish* honest from dishonest submissions, not on the true population rate of dishonesty; even a "small" violation rate can fully unravel a pooling signal once the evaluator cannot tell which submissions are which, which is exactly the mechanism GT-8's "credibility... at stake" language reports as already underway.

**What it ruled out:** The rival on C2's confidence line — this dead end is what "the true cheating rate is low, so this is overblown" resolves to once traced through.

### Dead End 3: "Just enforce proctoring harder (lockdown browsers, device bans) and the exam-side problem is solved"

**What was tried:** Recommending stricter in-exam device controls as a sufficient response to AI availability, leaving the rest of the course (homework, practice, take-home components) untouched.

**Why abandoned:** This only addresses the exam moment itself, not the disconnect GT-5 identifies between AI-assisted practice and unaided performance; a student who offloads cognitive work throughout the term still arrives at a tightly-proctored final exam under-prepared, so hardening proctoring alone does not touch the actual skill-development mechanism C3 depends on.

**What it ruled out:** The rival on C3's confidence line, and any recommendation that treats "keep timed exams, just enforce them harder" as a complete answer rather than one component of a broader redesign.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** For any course outside a licensure-controlled pipeline, replace the single format entirely with a three-part hybrid — a short closed-book conceptual check, an AI-permitted take-home graded on critique rather than raw prose, and a live oral defense (full-cohort in small classes, stochastically sampled at large scale, per the bounded instructor-hour cost established) — because this hybrid beats both "keep timed exams unchanged" and "replace them outright" on a locked, weighted comparison that survives every single-criterion re-weighting tested (chain C6). For a licensure-controlled course, keep whatever proctored component the accreditor requires as a floor and add the same oral/applied layer on top rather than substituting for it (chain C6).

**Key insight:** "Replace vs. keep" is not one decision but at least five, because certification, feedback, motivation, ranking and accreditation do not move together under AI availability (chain C1); and no format — including the pre-AI timed exam — was ever fully robust, since any instrument's validity is capped below 1.0 by definition, so the real fix is closing a measurement gap that predates AI, not choosing a side in an AI-era binary (chain C4).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** Adopting the hybrid costs bounded but real instructor time (chain C5), risks a slow decay of the oral component's fakeability-resistance as coaching markets emerge over successive terms (chain C7), trades one equity axis (timed-exam anxiety) for a different, not-yet-fully-characterized one (oral-specific anxiety/bias) (chain C7), and will not immediately restore employer trust in the credential even if it genuinely raises validity, because perception updates on a lag independent of actual format quality (chain C7).
