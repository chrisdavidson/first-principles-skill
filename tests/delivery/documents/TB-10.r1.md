*Causes (unfiltered, from three stakeholder viewpoints):*
Trailing-career-spouse viewpoint: (1) job search took far longer than budgeted, depleting savings; (2) licensure/credentials didn't transfer, forcing a lower-tier role; (3) they never really agreed, just acquiesced to avoid conflict; (4) new role pays materially less with no path back to prior seniority; (5) lost their own professional/social network with nothing rebuilt.

Structural: (13) nobody ever defined what "helping" concretely meant operationally; (14) cost-of-living in the destination was mispriced, straining finances; (15) the decision was made reactively during a health scare with no contingency planning.

# 3. Ground Truths

**Read-at-source:** GT-1 — [PMC11692659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11692659/), quoted regression coefficients; GT-2 — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0070601), quoted odds ratios and percentages; GT-7 — [simplypsychology.org/srrs.html](https://www.simplypsychology.org/srrs.html), quoted risk thresholds.

- **GT-1** Long-distance caregivers (>1 hr away) show statistically higher physical strain (b=-0.236, p<.05) and financial strain (b=-0.228, p<.05) than proximate/co-resident caregivers, but perceived *choice* in caregiving is a stronger, more significant predictor of physical (b=.508), financial (b=.446), and emotional (b=.810) strain than proximity itself — source: 2020 NAC/AARP National Survey analysis; read-at-source: [PMC11692659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11692659/), abstract/results section, quoted directly via WebFetch.
- **GT-2** In a Welsh cohort of 121,422 children (assessed ages 6–7), two or more school moves between ages 4–6 carried an adjusted odds ratio of 2.33 for not achieving expected Key Stage 1 attainment (78% vs 87% achievement); residential-only moves carried a much smaller adjusted OR of 1.16; moves before age 1 showed no significant effect; school moves showed a substantially larger effect than residential moves, moderated by deprivation/free-school-meal eligibility — source: PLOS ONE cohort study; read-at-source: [journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0070601](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0070601), results section, quoted directly via WebFetch.
- **GT-3?** Instrumental activities of daily living (meal prep, home maintenance, shopping-type support) for elderly parents require more physical proximity than other care types; the farther an adult child lives, the less likely they are expected to, or actually do, provide this category of care — cited to LTSS Center/ScienceDirect sources; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis; cited sources not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-4?** Long-distance caregivers of people with dementia report burden broadly similar to proximate caregivers; roughly half of long-distance caregivers of care-recipients with significant cognitive impairment living in the community report burden/depression/anxiety higher than long-distance caregivers whose relative is in a nursing home — cited to NCBI/PMC sources; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis; cited sources not opened.
- **GT-5?** Married women disproportionately become the "trailing spouse" in dual-career relocations, more often bearing earnings loss and reduced labor-force participation than male partners; career-interruption risk is a leading reason dual-earner couples decline or struggle with relocation — cited to Equitable Growth/IMPACT Group/academic sources; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis; cited sources not opened.
- **GT-6?** Average 40hr/week in-home professional elder care costs ≈$65,000/yr; nursing-home care costs ≈$95,000 (shared room) to $108,000 (single room)/yr; family caregivers nationally report large aggregate lost earnings (≈$107B/yr) and individually spend an average ≈26% of income (≈$7,242/yr) directly on caregiving — cited to HealthWell Foundation/AARP/academic sources; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis; cited sources not opened.
- **GT-7** The Holmes-Rahe Social Readjustment Rating Scale (a validated, widely used instrument) scores cumulative "life change units" over a rolling year; a score of 150–299 corresponds to an estimated 50% chance, and a score over 300 to an estimated 80% chance, of a stress-related health breakdown in the following two years — source: simplypsychology.org SRRS summary; read-at-source: [simplypsychology.org/srrs.html](https://www.simplypsychology.org/srrs.html), quoted directly via WebFetch.

# 4. Derivation Chains

### Conclusion C1: Proximity does not reliably "produce better care" as a general claim

GT-1 (choice beats distance as a strain predictor) + GT-3? (IADL support needs proximity) + GT-9 (travel-time floor on emergency response) + GT-12 (need-type sets the minimum-sufficient intervention)
→ perceived choice in caregiving is a stronger predictor of strain than raw distance, so "closer" does not uniformly reduce strain for caregiver or care-recipient

# 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Computing a numeric trade-off winner for a generic family

**What was tried:** Attempted to build weighted-criteria scores (Cr1–Cr5) that would yield a single recommended option (move vs. stay) applicable to families in general.
**Why abandoned:** The user's brief explicitly specifies this must be treated as a generic instance, not a fabricated specific backstory. The trade-off procedure's own scores must be cited to ground truths (per its step 6); no ground truth exists for a specific family's need-severity, portability, or savings, so any numeric score would be invented and dressed up as this analysis's evidence-based conclusion — exactly the false precision the trade-off procedure's own guidance warns against.
**What it ruled out:** Any conclusion of the form "families in this situation should generally choose X" as an evidence-backed universal recommendation. It redirected the deliverable to the criteria/weights/must-haves structure itself (chain C4), which is the honest output for a genuinely underdetermined generic case.

### Dead End: Treating guilt/obligation as a pure decision error to be purged

**What was tried:** An initial framing that treated obligation-driven motivation as simply a bias to eliminate from the decision entirely.
**Why abandoned:** This conflates two different things — guilt as a proxy standing in for un-evaluated factual claims (which should be replaced by the GT-checked variables in C1–C5), and guilt as a label attached to a freely held terminal value ("we want to be near family for its own sake"), which is a legitimate values input no first-principles analysis is entitled to invalidate, because values aren't factual claims to be verified or falsified the way "proximity improves care" is.
**What it ruled out:** Framing this analysis's job as proving families should never move for connection's own sake. The job instead became separating the factual claims that need evidence from the values claim that doesn't — which is why the values-based rival survives, unresolved, in §6's caveat rather than being argued away.

### Dead End: Pursuing a dedicated literature search on marital-satisfaction outcomes after joint relocation decisions

**What was tried:** Began framing a targeted search for peer-reviewed studies specifically on marital outcomes following joint career-sacrifice relocation decisions.
**Why abandoned:** That would require a literature dive scoped to a clinical/academic project rather than a general-audience decision-framework analysis. The decision-relevant point — that simultaneous major life-stressor stacking elevates relationship/health strain risk — is already adequately supported at this deliverable's appropriate rigor level by the well-established, cross-validated SRRS threshold data (GT-7), with the item-level detail honestly flagged unverified (GT-8?) rather than pursued further.

# 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Replace "closer = more caring" with a four-gate, five-criterion decision procedure the family runs on its own facts before either job is given notice (chains C1, C2, C3, C4). The gates and criteria:
- Confirm the parents' actual need-type on the spectrum from relational-only to 24/7 custodial
- Confirm each partner's career portability separately, not as one shared number
- Confirm the destination can match any specific school program a child currently depends on
- Confirm 6–12 months of financial runway exists before any resignation
- Confirm both partners' independently-elicited consent, not passive agreement
- Confirm the parents' own consent to increased involvement
- Write the caregiving operating plan (who, what, how often, escalation) before moving, not after
- Tripwire: no concrete recurring caregiving routine exists six months post-move
- Tripwire: the less-portable partner has no comparable job by the pre-agreed runway date
- Tripwire: either partner privately says they wouldn't choose this again within year one

**Key insight:** The variable that actually separates good outcomes from bad ones is not distance but whether the family can name a concrete, jointly-owned care plan matched to the parents' real need-type, priced against portability-adjusted career cost and school-timing risk — the failure mode observed across the pre-mortem's causes is not "moving" or "not moving," it is mistaking proximity itself for the plan (chains C1, C5).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This analysis explicitly declines to produce a universal "move" or "stay" verdict, deprioritizing false precision in favor of naming the scoring procedure itself as the deliverable (chain C4); it also accepts a LOW-confidence extension (chain C5) on the marriage/long-run-acuity effects because the underlying empirical inputs there are unverified or reported-by-delegate. Wanting to be near ageing family for its own sake, independent of any instrumental caregiving benefit, is a legitimate value this analysis has no authority to weigh for the family — no chain — flagged assumption only.
