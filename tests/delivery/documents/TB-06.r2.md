# 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: "This isn't a new problem — students could always plagiarize or hire ghostwriters"

**What was tried:** Treating AI-assisted take-home submission as functionally equivalent to pre-existing cheating channels (plagiarism, paid ghostwriting), implying no policy update is needed.

**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-3/GT-4 combined with GT-1/GT-2 — AI assistance is free, instant, and precisely keyed to the specific prompt, and per GT-3/GT-4 is essentially undetectable (and systematically biased against non-native speakers when detection is attempted), whereas ghostwriting required money and access and left detectable stylometric inconsistencies across a student's body of work. Equating the two understates the magnitude of the shift.

**What it ruled out:** The "no real change, no policy update needed" framing as a basis for leaving assessment design untouched — ruled out by chains C1 and C2.

### Dead End: "Just ban AI use and enforce with detection software"

**What was tried:** Retaining take-home assessment universally, with an honor-code ban on AI backed by AI-detector screening as the integrity mechanism.

**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-3/GT-4 (high false-positive rate concentrated on non-native English speakers; easy evasion via paraphrasing) and fails must-have M3 in the trade-off (equitable enforcement) — this is O1's natural implementation, eliminated at the knockout stage in chain C6.

**What it ruled out:** Reliance on AI-detection products as the primary integrity mechanism for any high-stakes assessment.

### Dead End: "Return unconditionally to 100% proctored exams for everything"

**What was tried:** Treating AI availability as grounds to revert every assessment, including upper-level seminars and research-heavy courses, to timed proctored exams.

**Why abandoned:** This pushes O0 beyond what it represents in the trade-off scoring; it ignores C3's finding that timed exams structurally cannot test extended synthesis, iterative revision, or research integration, and ignores that many disciplines' terminal skills (thesis writing, extended argument) have no timed-format expression at all. C6's scoring shows this option optimizes only a subset of criteria (Cr1, Cr3, Cr4, Cr5) while collapsing others (Cr2, Cr6) — a net loss relative to O2.

**What it ruled out:** "Just go back to pure exams everywhere," as if AI availability were the only relevant fact, ignoring the pre-AI pedagogical case for take-home formats.

# 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach — stratified by stakes and discipline, not a blanket switch either direction:**
- Retain or add a synchronous, supervised, individually-attributable component for whichever part of a course actually certifies competence to outside stakeholders — a proctored exam where speed/recall genuinely matters to the discipline, an oral defense of submitted work, a live coding/problem-solving session, or an in-person practicum (chain C1, chain C5).
- Move only the non-certifying, formative work (problem sets, drafts, discussion posts) to unsupervised take-home format, with AI use explicitly permitted, scaffolded, and taught rather than banned (chain C3).
- Redesign the certifying component itself to test judgment, synthesis, and application under realistic conditions rather than pure rote recall, wherever the discipline allows it — this is not "keep 1990s blue-book exams," it is "keep synchronous verification, modernize what it verifies" (chain C3).
- Do not rely on AI-text detectors as an integrity backstop for anything high-stakes (chain C2).

**Key insight:** The debate as usually framed — "timed exams versus take-home assessments" — is a false binary that the AI-capability shift exposes rather than creates. GT-9's definitional point (an artifact a tool produced cannot itself evidence the producing individual's own unaided cognition) means no purely asynchronous, purely written format is or can become AI-proof, no matter how sophisticated detection or prompt design gets (chain C5). Format is a proxy for a deeper design variable — synchronous-and-observed versus asynchronous-and-unobserved — and AI has simply made the proxy stop working as a proxy. The real, narrower question every assessment decision should answer is: what is this specific assessment certifying, to whom, and does verifying that require watching the performance happen?

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This recommendation costs faculty and TA time relative to either pure extreme — pure take-home is cheapest to grade at scale, pure timed exams are already running — while the hybrid model requires investment in oral-defense or live-check infrastructure most institutions haven't budgeted for (chain C6). It also does not fully solve equity concerns around AI-tool access on the low-stakes, AI-permitted portion of coursework: students with better tools, prompting skill, or private tutoring retain an advantage there, even though that advantage no longer corrupts the certifying signal. And it gives up, permanently, on ever again treating a pure take-home written artifact by itself as proof of individual unaided competence — for any program whose entire value proposition rested on that assumption with no synchronous check anywhere, this is a real institutional retreat, not a cosmetic one (chain C1, chain C5) — the magnitude of that retreat's transition cost is not itself traced to a chain here — no chain — flagged assumption only. Separately, an undifferentiated rollout risks the second-order market-discounting dynamic identified in chain C4: once it becomes known that a program's assessments are unsupervised take-home throughout, employers and graduate admissions committees may begin discounting that credential's signal value across the board, not just for the dishonest minority — or, per GT-5?, the now-dishonest majority.
