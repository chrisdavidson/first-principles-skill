The first-principles agent completed its analysis. Here's its conclusion:

## Bottom line: ~$15–30 per usable kWh (thermal), central estimate ≈ $20/kWh_th

For a 200 MWh molten-salt TES with pre-commissioned tanks excluded, that works out to roughly **$3–6M** for the priced scope.

### Cost breakdown
| Component | $/kWh_th |
|---|---|
| Salt inventory (~8–9 kg solar salt per kWh @ ~$0.7–1.0/kg) | $7–10 |
| Balance of system (heat exchangers, pumps, salt-handling — tanks excluded) | $8–18 |
| **Total** | **$15–30** |

Excluding the tank shells matters a lot — an **all-in figure including tanks** is typically **$20–30/kWh_th**, with the tanks/civil works often the single largest line item.

### Two caveats that move the number most
1. **Operating ΔT is load-bearing.** The estimate assumes a CSP-grade ~275 K temperature swing (~0.115 kWh_th/kg). A lower-temperature industrial config (ΔT ~100–150 K) roughly doubles the salt mass and pushes toward or past the upper bound.
2. **Thermal vs. electrical.** This is cost per *thermal* kWh — appropriate since molten-salt TES stores heat. For cost per kWh of *electricity*, divide by power-block efficiency (~38–42%), giving very roughly **$40–75/kWh_e** — but that cost belongs to the power block, not the storage.

The agent noted it can tighten the bracket considerably if you specify the operating temperature range and whether you want a thermal or electrical basis.
