### Ground truths it anchored on
- Auth is on the critical path for **everything** — if it fails, access fails.
- An all-teams simultaneous cutover makes failures **correlated, not isolated** — one shared-component defect hits everyone at once.
- Migration effort scales with **integration points, not team count**.
- Rollback cost rises the moment new tokens are issued and old ones invalidated.

### Six failure modes
- **A. Capacity collision** — timeline set top-down rather than from a per-team integration inventory; the most critical teams (most integrations) slip.
- **B. Coverage gap mid-flight** — new system doesn't support some in-use auth pattern (service accounts, legacy OAuth, SAML quirks), blocking a team with no clean path forward or back.
- **C. Correlated big-bang failure** — a shared-component defect takes everyone down at once, with rollback already expensive. *The catastrophic case.*
- **D. Identity-data corruption** — permission mappings migrate subtly wrong; users authenticate but get wrong access. *A silent security incident disguised as success.*
- **E. Rollback trap** — no per-team rollback window, forcing an all-or-nothing decision under pressure.
- **F. External dependency not ready** — IdP/vendor isn't ready on your dates.

**Highest-signal finding (second-order):** schedule pressure is the *common root* — capacity failure (A) feeds the catastrophic failures (C, D) by forcing corner-cut validation and load testing. These risks are not independent.

### Priority actions before locking the timeline
1. **Verify coverage now** — confirm the new system handles every in-use auth pattern (the single most load-bearing unknown).
2. **Rebuild the timeline bottom-up** from an integration-point inventory, not team count.
3. **Reject big-bang** — phase per-team cutover with a rollback window to decorrelate failure.
4. **Dry-run the identity-data migration** and validate permission mappings.
5. **Confirm external dependency readiness** against your dates.
6. **Track schedule pressure itself** as the root risk.
