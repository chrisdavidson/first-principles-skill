## Pre-Mortem: Authentication Rollout — How It Could Go Badly

The agent reframed your request as a **pre-mortem** (imagine the rollout has already failed, then work backward to the causes) and surfaced the load-bearing assumptions in the plan before mapping failure modes.

### The two failure modes most likely to turn a delay into a crisis
- **No tested rollback → org-wide outage.** Because auth is a hard dependency for nearly everything, if the cutover makes the new system authoritative without a *rehearsed* rollback, a failure isn't a slip — it locks everyone out of everything at once. (Including, potentially, the recovery team locking itself out.)
- **Forgotten non-human accounts → silent failures.** Service accounts, batch jobs, and break-glass admin paths get missed in identity migration and fail quietly (e.g., at 2am), or worse, get silently over-permissioned.

### The full set of failure clusters
1. **Calendar-driven, not readiness-driven timeline** — the most common root cause. The slowest/most-complex team either breaks or blocks the whole "all teams" goal.
2. **Blast radius & rollback** — the catastrophic tail (above).
3. **Dirty identity data** — stale accounts, broken group mappings, orphaned service accounts.
4. **Coordination & human readiness** — N× support load at cutover, no single accountable owner, users adopting workarounds.
5. **Functional non-equivalence** — a team relies on auth behavior the new system doesn't replicate.

A notable **second-order effect**: a security-tightening migration that goes badly trains users to seek bypasses, leaving you *less* secure than before — defeating the rollout's own purpose.

### Recommended preparations before locking the timeline
1. Re-derive the timeline from per-team readiness; pilot with one representative team first.
2. Make a **tested** rollback the gating requirement — keep the old system authoritative and reversible.
3. Audit identity data, including service and break-glass accounts.
4. Name a single accountable rollout owner; over-resource cutover-day support.
5. Validate functional equivalence per team, especially atypical ones.
