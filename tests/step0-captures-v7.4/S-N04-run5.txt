### The five failure modes (with the plan change each implies)

1. **Bottleneck team blows the schedule (HIGH).** A cross-team rollout finishes at the pace of the *slowest, most-coupled* team — not the average. If the timeline is planned as uniform, the laggard breaks it. → Size each team's integration effort individually; pace the timeline by the slowest team plus buffer, and name the bottleneck out loud.

2. **Capacity was assumed, not committed (HIGH).** The single most common silent cause of cross-team slippage: teams' bandwidth next quarter is currently a *belief*, not a fact. → Get explicit written capacity commitment from each team lead before locking. Cheapest high-impact fix.

3. **Forgotten edge clients break silently (HIGH).** Service accounts, cron jobs, mobile apps, partner integrations, and API keys often use old auth and break without warning. → Build a complete inventory of every auth consumer and assign each a migration path before launch.

4. **Critical-path production breakage with no rollback (MEDIUM-HIGH).** Auth is on every dependent service's critical path; load tested only at average, plus clock-skew/unreachable-endpoint failures, plus no tested rollback = emergency freeze. → Load-test at peak-plus-headroom; document and rehearse staged rollback; verify clock-skew tolerance.

5. **Big-bang cutover with mass re-login shock (MEDIUM).** Simultaneous cutover removes any healthy cohort to fall back to; session invalidation forces a mass re-login that looks like an outage. → Stage the rollout (pilot → cohorts), keep a fallback cohort, stagger re-login, pre-stage support.

### The agent's bottom line
> The two changes most likely to prevent the slip are **#2 (written capacity commitments)** and **#1 (timeline paced by the bottleneck)** — do those before locking the timeline.
