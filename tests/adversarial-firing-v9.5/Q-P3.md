## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Treating the 35% as a single actionable number

**What was tried:** Computing each option's saving as 35% × heating cost × the fraction of the two measures undertaken — the arithmetic the figure invites.

**Why abandoned:** The figure sums two assemblies with very different U-values and areas. Applying it undivided credits the attic with loss that is actually leaving through the glass; the arithmetic would have made the attic look roughly 2.5× better than C4 supports, because it implicitly assigns the attic half of a share the UA inventory puts at under a third.

**What it ruled out:** Any payback number quoted on the premise that a measure "fixes 35% of your loss." The split is the load-bearing quantity, and neither quote supplies it.

### Dead End: Using the ENERGY STAR 15% figure as the attic job's return

**What was tried:** 0.15 × $1,750 = $263/yr, giving $4,200 a 16-year payback that nearly clears the horizon at the optimistic end — the most favourable case available for the attic quote.

**Why abandoned:** GT-2's own wording scopes the 15% to air sealing *together with* insulation in attics, floors over crawl spaces and basements. Using it for attic insulation alone double-counts the savings earned by the other measures in the bundle, and the contractor's scope contains none of them.

**What it ruled out:** The single most commonly cited efficiency statistic as a justification for this specific quote. This became C2 rather than being discarded, because the observation is more useful than the path.

### Dead End: A theoretical-limit pass on the envelope

**What was tried:** Deriving the law-permitted floor for this house's heat loss — with R → ∞ the conductive term goes to zero, bounded below only by the air exchange required for occupant health.

**Why abandoned:** The intermediate could not be established as decision-relevant. The binding constraint here is capital cost per unit of R, not what physics permits; the derivation yields a ceiling (zero conductive loss) that discriminates between none of the five options.

**What it ruled out:** Reframing the question as "how efficient could this house be." It is a purchasing question operating far from the physical bound, and the constraint that binds is the price of the next R-value, not the last one.

### Dead End: Resale-value recovery as the justification for the window quote

**What was tried:** Treating part of the $18,500 as recovered at sale, closing the payback gap that C7 leaves open.

**Why abandoned:** No ground truth about cost recovery for this market, this house or this window type was obtained, and the 10-year horizon means any remodeling-survey percentage — which measures recovery at near-immediate resale — would need a discount the survey does not support. It is an untested belief with no verification path available in this analysis, so it is not cited on any chain's head.

**What it ruled out:** The "new windows pay for themselves at resale" argument as anything stronger than an untested belief here. It survives only as criterion 7 in C10's matrix, where it is scored and weighted rather than asserted.
