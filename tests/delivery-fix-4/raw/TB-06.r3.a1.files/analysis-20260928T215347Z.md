## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that capable AI writing assistants can perform much of the cognitive work an assessment is meant to elicit, undetectably in most current implementations, which assessment format(s) — not necessarily a single one, and not necessarily either of the two named — best support the specific credentialing and learning-feedback inferences each course actually needs to certify, at a resourcing and equity cost the institution can sustain?

**Success criteria:**
1. Section 6's "Recommended approach" names the actual construct(s) each recommended format is meant to certify (not merely "prevent cheating" or "test knowledge") for each course category it distinguishes.
2. Section 6's "Recommended approach" lists more than two named formats or conditions rather than presenting a binary timed-exam-versus-take-home choice.
3. Section 6's "Key insight" states, as its finding, whether the pre-AI timed exam was ever a clean measure of the target construct.
4. Section 6's "Recommended approach" names at least two distinct conditions (construct type, field, resourcing) governing which format applies, rather than a context-free flat yes/no.
5. Section 6's "Trade-offs acknowledged" and "Confidence" lines name at least one second-order effect each on student behavior, equity, faculty workload, or institutional/credential reputation, traceable to a chain carrying `[2nd]`/`[3rd]` order marks in section 4.

## 3. Ground Truths

**Five-Whys reduce-to-primitives (Phase 3 technique) for GT-8:** Claim tested: "A submitted take-home text artifact demonstrates that the named student possesses the capability the course claims to certify." Constituent 1: the work product was produced predominantly by the named student's own cognition — irreducibility check: is this verified by anything intrinsic to the artifact? No; in the absence of a process record (drafts, timestamps, oral defense) this is *assumed*, not observed — Assumed, unverified branch, feeding GT-9?/GT-12(A-12). Constituent 2: the surface form of a well-prepared student's output and a well-prompted AI system's output can converge for open-ended writing prompts — irreducibility check: is this a further-reducible claim or a bottom fact? It bottoms out at a definitional/informational fact about text as a medium — a finished natural-language artifact does not carry its own generation-process metadata unless something external attaches it. This bottom fact is verified by definition (not by citation) and is promoted below as GT-8, unsuffixed. Parent claim verdict: NOT verified as stated (Constituent 1 fails), which is exactly why an unverified take-home artifact cannot alone support the credentialing inference — this finding feeds chain C3.

- **GT-1** Construct-irrelevant variance is a named, recognized threat to assessment validity: systematic score variance from factors unrelated to the intended construct (e.g., language barriers, test speededness, contextual stress that "alienate and confuse" some examinees and "distort task performance" for reasons unrelated to what is being measured) — source: Frontiers in Education (2021), "A Framework of Construct-Irrelevant Variance for Contextualized Constructed Response Assessment," quoting Messick (1989, p.19); read-at-source: quote ("contextual features that engage and motivate one [examinee]...may alienate and confuse another...and bias or distort task performance") located via direct fetch of the Frontiers article. (Note: this reads the Frontiers 2021 secondary presentation of Messick's framework directly; the primary 1989 monograph itself was not independently opened in this run.)
- **GT-2** Commercial AI-text detectors tested on non-native-English TOEFL essays (n=91) versus native-English 8th-grade essays (n=88), across 7 detectors (Originality.AI, Quil.org, Sapling, OpenAI, Crossplag, GPTZero, ZeroGPT), produced an average false-positive rate of 61.22% on non-native essays versus 5.19% on native essays, with 97.80% of non-native essays flagged by at least one detector — source: Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu, Zou, "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers," arXiv:2304.02819 (published in *Patterns*, 2023); read-at-source: figures located and quoted directly from arxiv.org/html/2304.02819 via fetch.
- **GT-3?** General-purpose LLMs (the class stipulated in the prompt — Claude, GPT-class systems) can generate fluent, structurally competent, subject-plausible prose in response to most standard undergraduate open-ended writing prompts within seconds, without the requester having independently performed the underlying synthesis — reported-by-delegate: stipulated as a background premise in the user's own prompt/context ("capable AI writing assistants... widely available"), not independently benchmarked against a specific evaluation source in this analysis.
- **GT-4?** A credential (grade or degree) functions as an information signal to third parties (employers, licensing bodies, graduate programs) who did not observe the work being produced and who rely on the institution's certification process to infer the credentialed individual's capability — unverified: general signaling-theory framing (Spence-lineage), no specific source opened in this run.
- **GT-5?** For most post-graduation knowledge-work tasks a degree is meant to prepare a graduate for, the operative real-world performance constraint is "produce a correct/insightful output within a reasonable deadline, with reference tools available," not "produce it unaided within a short, fixed, proctored window"; the reverse (unaided, strict real-time, tool-free) is the binding constraint mainly in an identifiable minority of safety-critical or licensure-gated professions (e.g., emergency medicine triage, courtroom advocacy, licensure board exams, live interpretation) — unverified: professional-practice domain knowledge, no external source opened in this run.
- **GT-6?** Oral examination / viva voce defense and in-person process-tracking (draft-history review, live follow-up questioning on submitted work) are long-standing, pre-AI-era methods institutions have used to verify that a submitted work product reflects the named individual's own understanding — unverified: historical/procedural domain knowledge (e.g., doctoral viva tradition), no external source opened in this run.
- **GT-7?** Aggregate market trust in a credential is a function of perceived reliability of the institution's certification process; a widely-held belief that an institution's assessments cannot verify individual authorship discounts the credential's signaling value for all of that institution's graduates, not only for individuals who obtained undisclosed help — unverified: inference from signaling theory, no external source opened in this run.
- **GT-8** A finished natural-language text artifact, evaluated without any accompanying process record (drafts, timestamps, an oral defense, or in-person work-checks), does not by itself reveal whether it was produced predominantly by the named submitter's own cognition or predominantly by a generative AI system the submitter prompted, because both processes can converge on similar or indistinguishable surface-level output for many open-ended writing tasks — verified by the reduce-to-primitives decomposition above (definitional/informational entailment about text as a medium, not an empirical citation; non-vacuous given GT-3?'s qualitative capability claim).
- **GT-9?** Take-home formats that are "authentic" (requiring inputs — in-class-generated data, personally collected artifacts, student-specific case material — not available to a generic external AI session) and/or paired with a verification step (oral defense, process-tracking) substantially narrow the GT-8 authorship-ambiguity gap, because they either require inputs a generic AI session lacks or add an independent evidentiary channel a purely textual artifact does not have — unverified: inference from GT-6? and general evidence-channel logic; no institutional efficacy data available in this run. This is the single most decision-critical unverified input in the analysis.
- **GT-10?** Redesigning assessments to be authentic and/or verification-paired at scale requires additional faculty/TA time (personalized task design, rubric rework, per-student oral checks) that is a real, current resource constraint at most institutions, particularly at high student-to-faculty ratios — unverified: institutional/practical domain knowledge, no external source opened in this run; current constraint — expires if institutions invest in TA support, AI-assisted grading tools, or restructure section sizes.

## 4. Derivation Chains

### Trade-off analysis (Phase 4 technique) — full matrix supporting Conclusion C1

**Options named, including status quo:** (A) Status quo — timed, proctored, closed-book/closed-AI written exam. (B) Naive full switch — unverified take-home, no redesign, no check. (C) Authentic take-home + standardized verification step (oral spot-check or brief follow-up). (D) AI-inclusive timed in-class judgment task (critique/correct/apply an AI-generated draft under time pressure). (E) Portfolio-with-periodic-checks across the term.

**Must-have knockouts (applied before scoring):** (1) must support the credentialing inference the course claims to certify; (2) must be deliverable within real faculty/TA resourcing; (3) must not rely on AI-detector verdicts as the primary integrity mechanism (per GT-2's 61.22%-vs-5.19% disparate false-positive rate). Option B fails knockout (1) for any course making an individual-capability claim (per GT-8/chain C3) and survives only in the narrow carve-out of courses with no such claim (e.g., team deliverables explicitly scored as AI-collaborative output). "Status quo + AI-detector enforcement" (a commonly proposed patch, not one of the five named options) fails knockout (3) outright and is not scored. A, C, D, E survive to scoring for the general case.

**Criteria, weights (1–5, locked before scoring), and anchors:**
1. Construct validity for the claimed target (weight 5): 1 = score mostly explained by factors unrelated to the construct; 5 = score variance overwhelmingly explained by the construct.
2. Resistance to undetectable AI substitution of the credentialed inference (weight 4): 1 = top score achievable with near-zero personal cognitive contribution and no realistic chance of being asked to explain; 5 = substantial AI-only reliance would very likely surface.
3. Equity across student circumstances (weight 4): 1 = strongly disadvantages a group; 5 = roughly equal footing.
4. Faculty/institutional resource feasibility at scale (weight 3): 1 = requires unstaffable per-student time; 5 = deliverable within current typical staffing.
5. Authenticity to post-graduation task conditions, field-relative (weight 3): 1 = performance conditions bear little resemblance to how the relevant work is actually done; 5 = closely mirrors real post-graduation conditions for the specific field.
6. Student stress/wellbeing distortion (weight 2): 1 = high artificial single-sitting stress; 5 = minimal artificial distortion.

**Scores (cited to ground truths) and weighted totals (weight sum = 21):**

| Criterion (weight) | A: status quo | C: authentic+verified | D: AI-inclusive timed | E: portfolio+checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Construct validity (5) | 3 (GT-1: some CIV present) | 4 (GT-9?) | 4 (targets the AI-era construct directly) | 4 (similar logic to C) |
| AI-substitution resistance (4) | 5 (proctoring blocks live AI use) | 4 (GT-9?: not perfect) | 5 (proctored + task design resists disguise) | 3 (spread across many artifacts, harder to spot-check all) |
| Equity (3→4 col; weight 4) | 2 (GT-1's CIV: speededness/stress) | 4 (avoids GT-2's detector-bias harm; new live-defense stress risk) | 3 (retains GT-1's stress cost; avoids detector-bias risk) | 4 (spreads stakes; avoids detector-bias risk) |

**Ranking:** D (78) > C (76) > E (74) > A (66). **Flip test:** the smallest single-weight change that changes the winner is raising the wellbeing weight (criterion 6) from 2 to 3 — a move of 1 — which flips the D/C ranking in favor of C; no single-weight move flips A into the lead within the 1–5 range. The D–C gap is therefore a near-tie sensitive to how heavily an institution values student wellbeing relative to construct validity/integrity, while A's last-place standing (for non-real-time-construct courses) is robust to any single-weight change.

### Conclusion C4: The pre-AI timed exam was never a clean measure of the target construct — AI's arrival removes a compensating advantage rather than introducing a new validity problem

GT-1 (construct-irrelevant variance framework) + GT-5? (real-world tasks rarely require unaided real-time performance)
→ timed proctored exams have carried documented construct-irrelevant variance from speededness and induced stress since long before AI assistants existed

→ it instead removes the one advantage — resistance to undetectable outside assistance — that had been propping up continued use of an already construct-compromised format
→ the honest framing of the policy question is not protecting a valid measure from a new threat but choosing which of two already-imperfect measurement problems is more tractable to redesign around for a given construct

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Blanket full switch to unverified take-home assessment (literal option B)

**What was tried:** Treated the policy question at face value — replace timed exams with take-home assessments everywhere, with no redesign or verification step.

**Why abandoned:** GT-8 (a text artifact alone cannot reveal its authorship process) combined with GT-3? (capable AI can produce competent prose fast) shows this configuration cannot support the credentialing inference for any course claiming to certify individual capability — the assumption A-12 (no verification structure) that this option silently relies on was explicitly discarded in Phase 2.

**What it ruled out:** Saves the reader from re-treating "just switch formats" as a serious policy option; it is retained only as a narrow carve-out for assessments making no individual-capability claim (chain C3).

### Dead End: Blanket "keep timed-proctored exams unchanged everywhere"

**What was tried:** Considered retaining option A as the universal default on the grounds that its validity costs are smaller and better-understood than the unquantified risks of redesign.

**Why abandoned:** Scores lowest in the trade-off matrix (66 vs. 74-78) for any course whose target construct is not itself unaided real-time performance, and this ranking is robust to any single-weight change (chain C1's flip test). It is retained, not fully abandoned, for the identifiable minority of courses where A-9 holds (licensure/safety-critical real-time construct) — this carve-out is preserved in the Conclusion rather than a blanket win for A.

**What it ruled out:** Rules out "no change is needed" as a general-purpose answer; confines the case for no change to a named, checkable category of courses.

### Dead End: "Solve it with better AI-detection software"

**What was tried:** Considered recommending improved or mandated AI-detection tools as the primary integrity mechanism alongside either assessment format.

**Why abandoned:** GT-2 shows current detector technology carries a severe, differentially-biased false-positive rate (61.22% vs. 5.19%) that would systematically harm non-native English speakers if used as a primary or sole integrity mechanism; this is a structural problem with how such detectors work (scoring linguistic predictability, which is confounded with second-language writing style), not merely a today's-implementation gap expected to close with better software.

**What it ruled out:** Rules out "detection-as-a-service" adoption as a reflexive fix; this finding is carried forward as pre-mortem Cluster 4 (enforcement-drift) and as knockout criterion 3 in the trade-off screening.

### Dead End: Portfolio-with-checks (option E) as the sole universal recommendation

**What was tried:** Considered recommending option E as the single answer for all non-real-time-construct courses, since it scored competitively (74) and had the best wellbeing profile.

**Why abandoned:** Not fully abandoned — E scored below both D and C in the default weighting and carries the heaviest ongoing faculty grading load (tied-lowest resource-feasibility score), but the flip test shows the D/C-vs-E ranking is sensitive to how heavily an institution weights student wellbeing. **What it ruled out:** This is a partial, not a full, ruling-out — E remains a live rival explicitly carried into chain C1's confidence line and the Conclusion's trade-offs, rather than a closed dead end. ### Dead End: Treating "authenticity" as a blanket preference for take-home formats

**What was tried:** Initially scored the authenticity criterion as favoring take-home formats categorically, on the framing (embedded in the prompt itself) that take-home assessment is generally more valid for real-world tasks.

**Why abandoned:** Too coarse once field-dependence (A-9, GT-5?) was recognized — for licensure/safety-critical fields, the real post-graduation task genuinely is timed and unaided, so a take-home format would score worse, not better, on authenticity. The criterion was redefined as field-relative rather than format-relative.

**What it ruled out:** Rules out a one-size-fits-all "take-home is more authentic" claim; this is what makes the final recommendation conditional rather than a blanket endorsement of take-home assessment.

## 6. Conclusion

Given the essence, ground truths, and derivation chains above, the analysis concludes:

**Recommended approach:** Replace the binary policy question with a construct-differentiated, resourced, and externally-communicated redesign: for courses whose target construct is applied synthesis without a hard real-time-performance requirement, move to authentic take-home assessment paired with a standardized, accommodation-built-in verification step, or a portfolio-with-checks model where resourcing favors it; for courses in AI-saturated professional domains, move to timed, proctored, AI-inclusive judgment assessments that test critique, correction, and application of AI output rather than unaided recall; retain timed, proctored, closed assessment only where the downstream task genuinely requires unaided real-time performance (licensure-gated or foundational-skill courses); and do not adopt AI-detection software as a primary or sole integrity mechanism under any configuration (chain C1).

**Key insight:** The pre-AI timed exam was never a clean measure of the target construct in the first place — construct-irrelevant variance from speededness and stress predates AI by decades — so the arrival of capable AI assistants does not create a validity problem out of a previously-solved one; it removes the one property (resistance to undetectable outside help) that had been compensating for a format whose validity was already compromised, forcing a trade-off that was always latent into the open (chain C4).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** Authentic-take-home-plus-verification requires faculty/TA resourcing most institutions do not currently budget for, and its integrity benefit rests on an assumption (GT-9?) untested against real institutional data (chain C1, chain C2); the AI-inclusive timed format requires genuine task-redesign effort and does not reduce exam-hall stress (chain C1); portfolio assessment is a live, near-tied rival that some institutions should prefer if they weight student wellbeing more heavily than this analysis's default weighting does (chain C1's flip test); and any conditional, per-construct policy is administratively harder to sustain than a blanket rule, creating a real risk of drift back to the status quo for reasons of implementation convenience rather than merit (chain C2).
