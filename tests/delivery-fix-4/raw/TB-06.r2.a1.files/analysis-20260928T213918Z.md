# First-Principles Analysis: Assessment Design in the Presence of Capable AI Writing Assistants

## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** Given that capable, freely-available generative-AI writing assistants have collapsed the effort and skill required to produce a passing written artifact, what assessment architecture lets a university keep verifying that a certified competency is genuinely the individual student's own — without silently trading that validity gain for a different, undisclosed validity or equity loss?

**Success criteria:**
1. The Conclusion section names a concrete, adoptable decision-procedure a university's teaching-and-learning committee could act on directly — not a values statement.
2. The Conclusion section differentiates its recommendation by competency/discipline type rather than prescribing one uniform format for every course.
3. The Conclusion section's mechanism directly targets the production-cost collapse caused by AI availability, rather than treating "cheating" as unchanged by that collapse.
4. The Conclusion section states an explicit expected durability for its recommendation rather than presenting it as a permanent fix.
5. The Conclusion section discloses at least one validity or equity cost the recommendation itself introduces, rather than presenting it as cost-free.

A skeptic can check all five directly against §6 without asking this analysis anything further.

## 3. Ground Truths

**Five-whys, reduce-to-primitives mode (technique applied).** Claim decomposed: "This assessment format validly measures the target competency." Branch (d) reduces to a staffing-hours arithmetic identity, verified directly in chain C5 below. This drill supplies GT-10.

### Conclusion C3 (theoretical-limit): The ceiling on achievable AI-text-detection accuracy is not fixed — it falls toward chance as generative models improve, so better detection tooling cannot be a durable solution.

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Invest further in AI-text detection as the primary integrity mechanism

**What was tried:** considered recommending continued or heavier investment in commercial AI-text detectors (e.g., mandating Turnitin AI scores as evidence in misconduct proceedings) as the university's primary response.

**Why abandoned:** an absent-fails derivation — this path requires the assumption "detectors can become reliable enough with more investment"; chain C3 shows that assumption is false, since detector accuracy is bounded by a quantity (total-variation distance between human/AI text) that shrinks as generative models improve, so the ceiling itself degrades over time rather than reflecting an engineering gap; chains C2's disparate-impact evidence (GT-3, GT-4?) independently reinforces the same conclusion.

**What it ruled out:** saves a future reviewer from re-proposing "buy a better detector" or "wait for detection to improve" as a durable fix — per C2/C3 that path is foreclosed mathematically, not by current technological immaturity.

### Dead End: Harden timed/proctored exams as the sole universal answer (device-screening or remote AI-proctoring software)

**What was tried:** considered recommending a full reversion to timed, in-person exams as a blanket, single-format solution, optionally hardened with device-screening or third-party remote-proctoring software.

**Why abandoned:** chain C6's trade-off scored a full return to timed exams at 59 against the recommended architecture's 87, driven by weak scores on ecological validity, equity, and wellbeing; chain C4/GT-8 additionally shows incomplete attribution-resistance even after hardening (GT-8 documents circumvention in jurisdictions that already screen for devices). Remote AI-proctoring software inherits GT-8's physical-circumvention weakness while adding its own harms (bandwidth/webcam-access inequity, algorithmic false-flagging of disabled and neurodivergent test-takers) consistent with, though not separately quantified beyond, the Cr-C equity criterion's low score for this option-family.

**What it ruled out:** saves a future reviewer from treating "go back to blue books" or "buy remote-proctoring software" as a low-effort universal fix; it remains a legitimate component for specific competency types (chain C6's timed/closed-condition internal branch) but fails as a universal default.

### Dead End: Increase misconduct penalties without changing the verification mechanism

**What was tried:** considered recommending a deterrence-only response — raising the severity of penalties for AI-assisted misconduct, enforced through the existing detection-based process, without redesigning assessments.

**Why abandoned:** an absent-fails derivation — this requires the assumption that the enforcement mechanism identifying misconduct is itself reliable; chains C2 and C3 show that assumption is false (documented, disparate misclassification rates, and a mathematically falling reliability ceiling), so harsher penalties enforced through the same mechanism raise the cost of a false accusation without raising the true-positive catch rate — the conclusion ("stronger deterrence solves this") does not follow from the false premise.

**What it ruled out:** saves a future reviewer from treating penalty severity as a substitute for a validity fix; the two are orthogonal, and pursuing the former alone increases harm (wrongful high-stakes penalties) without addressing the construct-validity gap chain C1 identifies.

## 6. Conclusion

The chains above converge on a single architecture, with the AI-availability shock's mechanism, competency-dependence, and durability limits made explicit rather than assumed.

**Recommended approach:** Adopt a stakes- and competency-proportional, verification-embedded hybrid assessment architecture: keep distributed, take-home work as the default production mode, but attach a brief, live, unpredictable oral defense whose depth scales with the artifact's stakes (spot-check for low-stakes work, full defense for capstone/high-stakes work), reserve purely timed/closed-book conditions for competencies genuinely well-measured under time pressure (foundational recall, bounded problem-solving), and reserve AI-inclusive redesign — grading judgment and verification of AI output — for courses whose actual target competency is AI-augmented practice itself (chain C6).

**Key insight:** Timed-proctored and take-home are not opposite ends of an AI-resistance spectrum — both are arms races between an enforcement mechanism and a concealment/evasion technology, one fought in the text domain and one fought in the physical/device domain; neither is a stable anchor, so the real design problem is which verification mechanism degrades slowest and is cheapest to keep refreshed (chains C2, C3, C4, C7).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The recommended architecture accepts a real, unresolved equity cost from live verbal performance under pressure (chain C6). It accepts a real feasibility/resourcing cost at large-enrollment scale that requires new staffing or an explicit sampling rate rather than a free lunch (chain C5). And it accepts that it is a durable improvement rather than a permanent fix, requiring an ongoing institutional maintenance cadence — question-bank refresh, adaptive questioning, disaggregated-outcome monitoring — as a standing operational requirement, not an afterthought (chain C7).
