# Wind Turbine HSS Gearbox Bearing Failure — First-Principles Root Cause Analysis

## 1. Problem Essence

**Essence Statement:** What is the physical root cause of the premature failure (~11% of L10) of the generator-end HSS bearing, and is the operator's "low-viscosity boundary-lubrication wear-through" hypothesis consistent with the fractographic/metallurgical evidence — and if not, what mechanism is — such that a defensible replacement-bearing specification can be authorized?

**Success criteria (each a checkable property of section 6, the Conclusion):**
1. Section 6 states, for the origin depth, crescent microcracks, WECs, butterflies, and axial spall geometry taken together, which damage-mechanism class they are diagnostic of, tied to a named chain.
2. Section 6 contains an explicit verdict — supported / partially supported / refuted — on the operator's literal hypothesis, tied to a named chain.
3. Section 6's recommendation names at least one specific literature-documented WEC driver category as the leading candidate trigger (not merely "WEC is present").
4. Section 6 or a chain it cites names at least four concrete, independently obtainable diagnostic tests that would discriminate among the remaining candidate causes.
5. Section 6 carries an explicit confidence band (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW) attached to a specific, ranked root-cause statement.
6. Section 6's recommended approach states a concrete action on the replacement-bearing-spec decision (authorize / withhold / authorize conditionally) and names what about the spec should or should not change as a result.

No success criterion requires the answer to be exactly "the operator is right" or exactly "the operator is wrong" — a composite verdict (literal hypothesis refuted, a related mechanism partially supported) is explicitly in scope and is in fact where the evidence leads.

## 3. Ground Truths

Each candidate ground truth was pushed toward the irreducibility test (reduce-to-primitives mode): is it a physical/geometric law, a direct measurement, or a definition — or does it bottom out as an unverified literature claim? Several load-bearing literature claims were checked this session by opening the cited source directly (Phase 3 verification step); read-at-source quotes and locations are given where that succeeded, and failed/unreached reads are recorded.

- **GT-1** — In a Hertzian line contact (cylindrical roller on race), the maximum orthogonal shear stress occurs subsurface at depth ≈0.5×(semi-contact-width b); for cylindrical roller bearings in the 1.5 MW HSS load/size class, b is typically such that this depth falls in the ~0.2–0.5 mm band. *Physical law (Hertzian contact mechanics / Lundberg-Palmgren theory).* No external citation needed beyond the mechanics itself; recomputed for order-of-magnitude plausibility in §5 Recompute. No `?`.
- **GT-2** — Abrasive/adhesive wear produced by boundary-lubrication (metal-to-metal asperity contact) is, by definition, a surface-initiated mechanism: material removal, plastic flow, and micro-welding occur at the contact surface, not as a primary subsurface event. *Definition (tribology).* No `?`.
- **GT-3** — Crack initiation in WEC-affected bearing steel has been found to occur at subsurface material defects, which may then develop into white etching cracks. Read-at-source: Bruce, Long, Slatter et al., *"Formation of white etching cracks at manganese sulfide (MnS) inclusions in bearing steel due to hammering impact loading,"* Wind Energy 19(10), pp. 1903–1915 — abstract, fetched directly (`https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/98592`): *"Crack initiation has been found to occur at subsurface material defects in bearing steel, which may develop into WECs."* No `?`.
- **GT-4** — White structure flaking (WSF)/WEC is explicitly treated in the literature as *mechanistically distinct* from ordinary subsurface- or surface-originated fatigue flaking, not a variant of abrasive wear. Read-at-source: Wind Systems Magazine WSF article (fetched `https://www.windsystemsmag.com/?p=4103`): the article "indicates it is a different type of flaking from the subsurface and surface originated flaking." No `?`.
- **GT-5** — The WEC/WSF literature documents (at least) four distinct driver categories, each independently confirmed at source this session: (a) **hydrogen** diffusion into the steel — Evans et al., STLE abstract *"Investigation of the Role of Hydrogen in White Structure Flaking of Wind Turbine Gearbox Bearings"* (fetched): *"Hydrogen diffusion into the bearing steel during operation is thought to be one of the driving factors of WSF,"* occurring in part *"through wear induced nascent surfaces"*; (b) **electrical current** — Wind Systems Mag article (fetched), discussion of static/electric-discharge contribution; corroborated by Chalmers University research program on wind-turbine main-shaft/HSS bearing currents from converter common-mode voltage (search-synthesized, source not opened this session — carries `?` for this sub-point only); (c) **micro-slip/skidding** — Wind Systems Mag article (fetched): *"large differential slip occurred"* near raceway shoulders and *"slip between rings and rollers"* during *"rapid acceleration and deceleration"*; (d) **transient impact/hammering loading** at MnS inclusions — Bruce et al. ## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Literal wear-through as the sole cause

**What was tried:** The operator's hypothesis, taken literally — classical abrasive/adhesive wear-through driven by low viscosity during the cold snap, as the primary and sole cause.

**Why abandoned:** Tested directly against the origin-depth evidence (chain C1): a surface-initiated mechanism (GT-2) cannot be the primary initiator of a crack network whose origin is 0.4 mm subsurface, inside the classical Hertzian shear zone (GT-1). This is a definitional/geometric argument, not a probabilistic one.

**What it ruled out:** Simple wear-through as the sole/primary cause. It did **not** rule out a modified, cold-snap-correlated mechanism (impact-loading or skidding-induced hydrogen generation, chain C3) — that pathway remains live and is carried into the ranked conclusion in §6.

### Dead End: Pure stray-current/EDM fluting as the complete explanation

**What was tried:** Pure stray-current/EDM fluting as the primary and complete explanation, motivated by the generator-end location's documented exposure to converter common-mode voltage (GT-10).

**Why abandoned (as the *sole* explanation — downgraded, not eliminated):** The macroscopic damage pattern is one contiguous 18 mm axial spall, not the washboard pattern of closely spaced parallel grooves (GT-8) that defines classic EDM fluting, and it matches the axial-cracking morphology documented for impact/hammering-driven WEC formation (GT-5d, GT-3) at least as well.

**What it ruled out:** The strongest, textbook form of the electrical-damage hypothesis (full fluting) as the dominant driver. It did **not** rule out a subtler current-assisted contribution (a point-source arc seeding one dominant crack) — that remains a secondary candidate pending direct bearing-current measurement (chain C3).

### Dead End: Steel-inclusion batch defect inferred from butterfly nucleation sites

**What was tried:** Material/steel-inclusion batch defect as a primary candidate, surfaced via the fishbone Material branch, on the reasoning that butterflies nucleate at inclusions.

**Why abandoned:** Butterflies and WECs nucleate at non-metallic inclusions (MnS, oxides) in *both* normal and defective bearing steel — inclusions are a necessary nucleation site, not evidence of abnormal cleanliness (A-13, discarded in §2). Treating "butterflies nucleated at an inclusion" as proof of a material defect conflates a necessary condition with a sufficient one — this specific inference is circular, not merely unverified.

**What it ruled out:** That specific (invalid) inference. It did **not** rule out an actual material contribution, which stays testable (and cheap to test) via the inclusion-cleanliness analysis named in chain C3.
