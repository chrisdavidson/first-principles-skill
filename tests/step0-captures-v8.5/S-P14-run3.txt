The agent's analysis:

**The real ceiling is ~56.6%, not the commonly quoted 64%.**

The key move: a molten-salt store is *sensible* heat storage, so it discharges across a temperature glide (838 K → 563 K), not isothermally. The exergy-weighted source temperature is the log-mean, 691 K — not the hot-tank temperature. Against a 300 K ambient sink:

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Current practice | ~41% |
| Endoreversible (Curzon–Ahlborn) bound | 40.2% |
| **Law-permitted ceiling, nitrate salt** | **56.6%** |
| Naive Carnot at T_hot | 64.2% (overstates by ~8 pts) |
| Chloride salt @ 720 °C | 64.7% |
| Latent/refractory store @ 2000 K | 85% |

Two findings worth flagging:

1. **Practice already sits at the finite-rate bound.** That ~41% plants achieve lands almost exactly on Curzon–Ahlborn's 40.2%. So the remaining headroom isn't bought with better turbines — it's bought by slowing heat transfer, i.e. larger and costlier exchangers.

2. **The glide penalty (64.2 → 56.6) is irreducible for sensible storage.** It's entropy generated by the temperature spread itself. No working fluid or cycle recovers it.

The lever that actually moves the ceiling is materials, not thermodynamics: a higher-temperature medium (chloride salt → ~65%) or eliminating the glide via latent/phase-change storage (→ ~85%).

Scope note: this covers stored heat → work only. The solar-to-heat optical chain is a separate upstream multiplier (~55–60%). The agent also flagged one unverified premise (tank standing loss <1%/day), but noted no conclusion depends on it.
