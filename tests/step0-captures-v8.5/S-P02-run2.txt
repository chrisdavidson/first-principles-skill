# Inversion: "faster ships → better retention"

The agent's core reframe: the claim isn't false, it's **unfalsifiable as you hold it** — it names no conditions under which it would fail. Here's what it derived.

## Where it breaks — three distinct modes

**Irrelevantly** — churn is exogenous (price, champion left, budget) or structural (accounts died). Cadence can't touch either. Fix: stop using cadence as a retention lever.

**Silently** — ships land on surfaces retained users don't touch, or land imperceptibly (behind flags, no changelog). Fix is placement and perception, not more speed.

**Harmfully** — change cost exceeds change value. Each ship forces relearning, breaks a saved workflow or an integration. In workflow-embedded tools this is the dominant inversion: cadence itself becomes the churn driver. Fix: slow down or batch.

## The one that's broken by arithmetic, not hypothesis

Retention is measured over 30/60/90-day windows. At two weeks you ship 3–6 times before the first ship's effect is legible. So cadence is increasing your rate of *unvalidated* change, not validated improvement — **faster shipping without a faster feedback loop is faster guessing.** Unless you have a leading proxy you didn't mention, you're shipping open-loop by construction.

## Two conventions it challenged

- *"Two weeks is fast"* — descends from Scrum sprint convention, chosen for team-coordination reasons, never derived from user absorption rate.
- *"Change is net-positive to a user"* — holds for consumer/exploratory products, frequently inverts for daily-workflow tools.

## Second-order effect worth sitting with

Optimizing for cadence selects for smaller, safer, more shippable work. Retention-moving work is usually the opposite — larger, riskier, doesn't fit in two weeks. Third order: you improve continuously on measures nobody churns over, while delivery metrics look excellent throughout.

## Cheapest first move

Establish one retention proxy readable inside a single two-week cycle. Without it every cadence decision stays unmeasurable.

Two things would sharpen this a lot: **what kind of product** (consumer vs. workflow-embedded — they invert oppositely on change cost) and **what your retention curve looks like** (early-drop vs. long-tail).
