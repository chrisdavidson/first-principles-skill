### Inversion procedure (Phase 2 companion technique)

**Claim inverted:** "A well-designed assessment policy will maintain both learning integrity and real-world relevance as AI capability grows" → inverted to "the policy fails to maintain both."

**Failure-guaranteeing conditions enumerated (7):** (1) the university adopts one uniform blanket format regardless of course objective; (2) the verification backstop is applied inconsistently, or targets students by suspicion rather than by documented risk/random criteria, creating discrimination exposure; (3) retrieval-practice checks carry zero grade weight so students ignore them; (4) faculty receive no added grading/TA resources for higher-touch components, producing burnout or quiet reversion to easier-to-grade formats; (5) the policy is set once and never revisited as AI capability improves; (6) students correctly perceive spot-check selection as low-probability and rationally outsource the artifact wholesale, since expected cost of being caught is lower than the effort saved; (7) external accreditation/licensure requirements change faster than university policy adapts.

**Necessary preconditions derived, each tagged load-bearing or not:** P1 — policy must be objective-specific, not blanket (**load-bearing**; already established independently by chain C1, so no new table row). P2 — verification-backstop selection must be risk/random-based with documented, bias-audited criteria (**load-bearing**; → A-9's scalability finding plus a new implementation requirement folded into §6's Cluster-2 disposition). P3 — retrieval checks must carry nonzero grade weight (**load-bearing** → A-16). P4 — faculty resourcing must scale with the new architecture (**load-bearing** → A-14). P5 — the policy needs a scheduled review/sunset clause (**load-bearing** → A-15). P6 — deterrence economics (probability × penalty > effort saved) must hold for the spot-check to function (**load-bearing** → A-17). ## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End 1: Ban AI outright and rely on AI-text-detection software as the enforcement mechanism

**What was tried:** Treating detector-based enforcement as the primary integrity mechanism for any take-home component, on the assumption (A-1) that detection is reliable enough to backstop authorship.

**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-1? — independent evaluations show 12-26% false-positive rates on human-written text, over 20% false-positive rates for non-native English writers, and near-0% accuracy on hybrid human-AI text. A mechanism with this error profile cannot be the basis of a high-stakes integrity determination.

**What it ruled out:** Any policy design (including several early real-world university responses documented in GT-4) that treats a detector score as authoritative evidence of misconduct; this is why chain C2 and the trade-off's Misuse-resistance criterion define "robust" as "not dependent on detector accuracy."

### Dead End 2: Full return to 100% timed, in-person, closed-book exams for everything

**What was tried:** Treating the pre-AI status quo (option A, universally applied) as the default-safe answer, motivated by GT-2's grade-inflation data point.

**Why abandoned:** Ruled out by chain C3 and GT-7? — most modern knowledge work is now tool-augmented, so a closed-book format under-measures the "produce quality work with available tools" competence entirely, failing the real-world-preparation function named in the original scope.

**What it ruled out:** Treating C4's retention-preservation finding as license to override C3 rather than supplement it — the hybrid (C6) retains a checkpoint-exam component specifically because of C4, without reverting to 100% closed-book coverage.

### Dead End 3: Full replacement with unrestricted, unsupervised take-home assessment for everything

**What was tried:** Treating AI-tool availability (GT-7?, GT-8) as sufficient justification for eliminating all directly-observed assessment.

**Why abandoned:** Ruled out by chain C2 — GT-12's authorship-unverifiability problem, combined with GT-1?'s detector unreliability, means the "certify individual unaided competence" function becomes undeliverable under this design wherever that function is actually required (e.g., licensure-adjacent coursework, GT-13?).

**What it ruled out:** Treating "AI tools exist and are useful" as a complete argument for format choice on its own — GT-8 alone does not resolve which function a given assessment component needs to serve.

### Dead End 4: Universal, mandatory oral defense of every submitted artifact

**What was tried:** Considered as the strongest single mechanism for closing the authorship-verification gap without relying on detectors, applied to 100% of take-home submissions.

**Why abandoned:** Assumption A-6/A-14 (faculty grading capacity) makes this infeasible at scale — the trade-off's must-have knockout rules out option C as a *universal* requirement above roughly 150-200 enrolled students, absent new resourcing that this analysis found no evidence is typically provided.

**What it ruled out:** A "universal oral defense" policy as the whole solution; retained only in bounded, risk-based, funded form inside the hybrid (C6), which is what avoids the knockout that felled the universal version.

### Dead End 5: Treating GT-2's Birkbeck 45%/13% grade jump as proof that take-home grades were AI-inflated and therefore invalid

**What was tried:** Using the Birkbeck first-class-grading swing directly as evidence that take-home assessment specifically produces inflated, unreliable grades under AI availability.

**Why abandoned:** This is one plausible reading but not the only one — grading-calibration differences between in-person and take-home rubrics, or a cohort effect, are equally consistent with the same single data point, and no controlled comparison was found this session to distinguish them. Treating it as proof would over-claim beyond what GT-2 (read at source, but a single institutional anecdote) can support.

**What it ruled out:** Using GT-2 as a load-bearing input in any derivation chain; it is retained in §3 only as illustrative, motivating context for why universities are reconsidering take-home formats, not as evidence inside C1-C6.

### Dead End 6: Treating "AI-authorship-verification technology will mature soon, making the hybrid unnecessary" as already-resolved (either accepted or dismissed)

**What was tried:** Considering whether to build the recommendation around an assumption that non-detector verification technology (behavioral biometrics, process-replay, cryptographic provenance) will mature within the policy's planning horizon.

**Why abandoned:** No evidence of a mature, production-scale, independently-audited technology of this kind was found this session — but its future arrival cannot be ruled out either. Treating it as resolved in either direction would overstate this analysis's evidence.

**What it ruled out:** A permanent, unconditional recommendation; instead, this exact condition is carried forward as this analysis's explicit Falsification condition (§5 Adversarial pass, above) and as trigger condition 1 in §6.

### Dead End 7: Deriving the oral/process spot-check sampling-rate floor (≥20%) from a formal Fermi/deterrence-economics estimate

**What was tried:** Attempting to compute a specific sampling-rate floor from first-principles unit-factors (probability of AI misuse × penalty severity × effort saved), per the estimate (Fermi) companion technique.

**Why abandoned:** No reliable unit-factor data was available in this analysis for any of the three factors (no measured distribution of AI-use-for-cheating probability, no standard penalty-severity scale, no measured effort-hours saved per assignment type) — fabricating such factors would produce false precision rather than a defensible estimate. See the Techniques-not-applied block above.

**What it ruled out:** Presenting ≥20% as an empirically-derived figure; it is instead presented in §6 explicitly as a design parameter requiring local calibration, carrying the `no chain — flagged assumption only` marker.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Do not "replace" timed in-person exams with take-home assessments as a blanket university policy. Adopt a hybrid architecture instead: in-room, timed, AI-inclusive checkpoint exams for the subset of competence a course must individually certify, take-home/project work (with disclosed AI use) for the subset whose function is producing high-quality work with available tools, and a risk-based — not universal — oral or process spot-check as an authorship backstop on high-stakes take-home components (chain C6).

**Key insight:** "Should take-home replace timed exams" is a category error, not a genuinely contingent question with one right global answer — the five assessment functions named in the original scope (certification, motivation, feedback, ranking, real-world prep) systematically favor opposite formats, so treating format choice as a single university-wide switch guarantees under-serving at least one function no matter which way the switch is thrown (chain C1).

**Trade-offs acknowledged:**
- Retrieval-practice checks must carry nonzero grade weight to preserve durable learning under this policy; this is the analysis's lowest-confidence finding and should be piloted, not assumed (chain C4).
- Equity requires explicit, funded mitigations regardless of which format a course uses (chain C5).
- Which format is more equitable in aggregate for a given institution is not determined by this analysis, for lack of comparative-magnitude data on home-environment/paid-tool disparity versus accommodation burden — no chain — flagged assumption only.
- The proposed oral/process spot-check sampling floor (≥20%) is a design parameter set by judgment, not a validated deterrence-economics figure — no chain — flagged assumption only.

**Additional required design features:**
- Publish and fund a minimum oral/process spot-check sampling rate as an audited policy floor (proposed default ≥20% of take-home submissions, plus 100% of grade-boundary and anomaly-flagged cases), reported annually to the department chair or provost — not left to individual-instructor discretion (chain C6).
- Structure spot-checks as short, rubric-anchored, binary pass/fail ("authorship confirmed" / "not confirmed") rather than graded oral performance, to limit any coaching-access advantage (chain C5).
- Label transcript and course-grade components explicitly as individually-certified versus tool-assisted, rather than merging them into one opaque grade, so the credential stays legible to employers and accreditors (chain C2).
- Adopt one university-level AI-use disclosure tier system (e.g., Tier 0 — no AI; Tier 1 — AI-assisted, cited; Tier 2 — AI-primary, verified), published before each term starts, rather than leaving norms to individual-instructor discretion (chain C6).

1. If an independently-audited AI-authorship-verification method reaches sub-1% false-positive and false-negative error, robust to hybrid human-AI text, and is deployed at production scale, shift weight toward unsupervised take-home (option B) and reduce reliance on in-room checkpoints (chain C2).

If a controlled study finds no significant outcome difference between hybrid-D graduates and pure-timed-exam graduates on licensure or professional outcomes, the hybrid's added complexity is not justified for non-licensure-bound programs, and a simpler architecture is preferable there (chain C6).
3. If the oral/process spot-check sampling floor cannot be funded, do not deploy an underfunded hybrid that manufactures false confidence — revert the certifying components to pure timed/in-person assessment (option A) instead (chain C6).
4. Wherever an external accreditation or licensure body mandates a specific proctored format for a given program, that mandate is a floor regardless of this analysis's general recommendation, independent of local pedagogical preference (chain C2).
