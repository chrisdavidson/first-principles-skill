# First-Principles Analysis: Should Universities Replace Timed In-Person Exams With Take-Home Assessments, Given Widely-Available AI Writing Assistants?

## 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** What claim about a student's ability should a university's chosen assessment format be able to license someone (an employer, a licensing board, the student's own self-assessment, a future instructor) to believe, and which format — drawn from the full space of assessment designs, not only "timed in-person exam" versus "take-home assessment" — actually supports that claim now that capable AI writing assistants can produce, undetectably in many task genres, work indistinguishable from a student's own?

**Success criteria:**
1. The Conclusion section names, separately for the high-stakes/summative case and the low-stakes/formative case, which assessment format(s) are recommended, conditioned on class size and discipline where the analysis finds that condition load-bearing.
2. The Conclusion's recommendation is derived from an explicit derivation chain (section 4) that traces back to a named ground truth about what an assessment format can validly certify — not asserted from convention, precedent, or intuition alone.
3. The Conclusion states at least one concrete condition under which the recommendation flips, and names the strongest rival/objection to the recommendation as given.
4. The Conclusion does not treat "timed in-person exam" and "take-home assessment" as the only two options considered — the design space actually reasoned over is named explicitly.

## 3. Ground Truths

**Irreducibility drill (5-Whys, reduce-to-primitives mode) for GT-4:**

Claim: "AI-text detection cannot be relied upon as a permanent solution to identifying AI-assisted writing."
- Why? Detector accuracy has a mathematical ceiling that degrades as AI text quality improves.
  - Why does a ceiling exist? Detection is a binary classification problem between two distributions (human-authored text, AI-generated text) for a given task/genre; classification accuracy is fundamentally bounded by how separable those two distributions are.
    - Why does separability decline over model generations? LLM training explicitly optimizes output to match human-authored text distributions (next-token prediction on human corpora, RLHF toward human-rated preferred output), so improving models and prompting shrink the statistical divergence between AI-output and human-output distributions for many task types.
      - Why does divergence shrinking to zero imply the ceiling? By the Neyman-Pearson lemma / Bayes-optimal classifier theory: when two class-conditional distributions become identical, the likelihood ratio equals 1 everywhere, and the Bayes-optimal decision rule's accuracy converges to the base rate (chance, adjusted for class prior). This is a definitional/mathematical fact about classification under identical distributions, not an empirical claim about any one detector's implementation. **Irreducible — mathematical definition. Stop.**

Verdict: Verified — mathematical/definitional (Bayes-optimal classification theory / Neyman-Pearson lemma). Promoted to GT-4 below without external citation.

**Ground truths:**

- **GT-1?** Across seven commercial GPT-detection tools tested on 91 TOEFL essays (non-native English writers) versus 88 US eighth-grade essays (native English writers), mean false-positive rate was approximately 61% on the TOEFL set versus approximately 5% on the native-speaker set; a simple prompt instructing the model to "enhance" word choice cut the TOEFL false-positive rate to roughly 12% — cited to: Liang et al., "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers," *Patterns* (Cell Press), July 2023; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis of the paper's reported findings — Phase 3 failure record: direct WebFetch of the Cell Press article returned HTTP 403 Forbidden; direct WebFetch of the arXiv PDF (2304.02819) returned unreadable binary content. Cited source not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-2?** Turnitin publicly claims a false-positive rate below 1% for its AI-writing-detection feature on qualifying documents; Vanderbilt University disabled that feature in August 2023 over reliability/fairness concerns, noting that even a 1% false-positive rate applied to roughly 75,000 papers per year yields on the order of 750 false accusations annually — cited to: Turnitin public statements; Vanderbilt University Center for Teaching announcement, August 2023; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis — cited sources not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-3** Among US families surveyed whose teen's schoolwork had been run through AI-detection software, self-reported false-accusation rates were 20% for Black teens, 10% for Latino teens, and 7% for white teens; the survey (n=1,045 parent/teen pairs) is self-reported accusation data, not a controlled test of detector accuracy, and the disparity may reflect detector bias, educator bias, or both — cited to: Common Sense Media survey, September 2024, as reported in *Education Week*, "Black Students Are More Likely to Be Falsely Accused of Using AI to Cheat," September 2024; read-at-source: the EdWeek article was opened directly via WebFetch and the cited figures and the self-report/non-controlled-data caveat were located in its text.
- **GT-4** As a generative model's output distribution for a given writing task converges toward the distribution of human-authored text for that task, the accuracy of any classifier attempting to distinguish machine-generated from human-generated instances converges to chance (base-rate) level — a consequence of the Neyman-Pearson lemma / Bayes-optimal classification theory: when two class-conditional distributions are identical, the likelihood ratio is 1 everywhere and no decision rule can exceed base-rate accuracy. This functions as a governing hard constraint on the maximum achievable AI-text-detection accuracy, independent of detector engineering — derived: self-contained mathematical argument verified via the five-whys irreducibility drill above; not resting on an external citation.
- **GT-5?** LLM training (next-token prediction over human corpora, plus RLHF toward human-rated preferred outputs) explicitly optimizes AI-generated text to approximate human-authored text distributions for a given genre, and detector-evasion rates against successive model generations and "humanization" post-processing have been reported to increase over time (e.g., one recent survey reports over 96% evasion of current detectors after humanization post-processing) — cited to: general LLM-training-objective literature plus arXiv:2608.11256, "Why AI Detection Fails for Academic Integrity"; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis only — cited sources not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-6?** Universities and integrity bodies are actively adopting oral exams and vivas specifically as an AI-resistant assessment method: Middlesex University's Academic Integrity and Misconduct Policy (2025–26) mandates vivas upon suspicion of poor referencing, contract cheating, or AI misuse; Stanford's Academic Integrity Working Group (2024–25) recommends oral exams and in-class formats — cited to: Middlesex University Academic Integrity and Misconduct Policy 2025–26; Stanford Academic Integrity Working Group materials, 2024–25; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis only — Phase 3 failure record: not attempted directly; deprioritized under turn-budget triage below the higher-dependency reads attempted for GT-1?/GT-7?/GT-8?, named here as `not read — turn budget`.
- **GT-7?** Messick (1989) established that validity is not an inherent property of a test or assessment instrument itself, but a property of the inferences and interpretations drawn from its scores for a specific intended use — an assessment format is valid or invalid only relative to the specific claim it is being used to support, not in the abstract — cited to: Messick, "Meaning and Values in Test Validation: The Science and Ethics of Assessment," *Educational Researcher*, 1989 (published version: *Review of Research in Education* / American Educational Research Association venues cataloged by SAGE); reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis — Phase 3 failure record: direct WebFetch to the SAGE Journals page returned HTTP 403 Forbidden.
- **GT-8?** A 30-year meta-analytic review (von der Embse et al., 2018) finds test anxiety significantly, negatively correlated (small-to-moderate effect size) with a range of academic performance outcomes including exam scores; a later controlled study (Theobald, Breitwieser & Brod, 2022, *Psychological Science*) found test anxiety does not significantly predict exam performance once prior knowledge/learning is statistically controlled for, suggesting much of the correlation runs through preparation/learning differences rather than in-the-moment interference during the timed exam itself — cited to: von der Embse, Jester, Roy & Post, "Test anxiety effects, predictors, and correlates: A 30-year meta-analytic review," *Journal of Affective Disorders*, 2018; Theobald, Breitwieser & Brod, *Psychological Science*, 2022; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis — Phase 3 failure record: direct WebFetch to the PubMed page for the 2018 meta-analysis returned only a CAPTCHA challenge page, no article content; the 2022 study was not independently fetched.
- **GT-9?** Black & Wiliam (1998) established the widely adopted distinction between formative assessment (low-stakes, feedback/learning-oriented, used to modify ongoing teaching and learning) and summative assessment (used to certify or report a level of achievement to a third party), foundational to subsequent assessment-design theory — cited to: Black & Wiliam, "Inside the Black Box: Raising Standards Through Classroom Assessment," *Phi Delta Kappan*, October 1998; reported-by-delegate: WebSearch synthesis only — cited source not opened by this analysis.
- **GT-10** An assessment format's "authorship-verification resistance" (how hard it is to pass using unauthorized AI output alone) and its "ecological/real-world validity" (how closely task conditions mirror actual professional practice) are logically independent properties: a format can be authorship-resistant but ecologically invalid (a closed-book timed recall exam in a field where professionals always have reference access), or ecologically valid but authorship-vulnerable (an unproctored take-home in a field where independent authorship is professionally required) — derived: definitional/logical distinction, this analysis; not resting on an external citation.

### Conclusion C2: AI-text detection cannot be a durable, permanent authorship-verification control (theoretical-limit)

**Theoretical-limit bracket (governing constraint: Neyman-Pearson lemma / Bayes-optimal classification):**
- **Ideal ceiling** *(higher accuracy is better for a detector; direction fixed accordingly)*: as generator-quality-to-human-quality divergence D → 0 for a task/genre, maximum achievable detector accuracy → the base rate (chance). This is a floor imposed from above on what engineering can achieve, not a favorable ceiling — GT-4.
- **Best demonstrated:** independent testing reports current commercial detectors at roughly 90–95% raw accuracy on unedited current-generation LLM output, with false-positive rates of 4–20%+ concentrated in specific subpopulations (GT-1?, GT-2?, GT-3) and post-processing ("humanization") evasion rates reported above 96% (GT-5?) — an observation, not a derivation.
- **Conventional figure:** many institutional integrity processes treat a detector "flag" as if it carried near-certain evidentiary weight — this is the convention GT-4 and the demonstrated figures above directly undercut.
- **Gap 1 (conventional → best demonstrated), headroom already proven:** institutions assume near-certainty; demonstrated tools deliver materially less, with disparate impact concentrated on non-native-English and (per self-report) Black students.
- **Gap 2 (best demonstrated → ideal ceiling), headroom not yet reached and structurally shrinking:** even the best current detector's ceiling keeps falling as generator quality improves (GT-5?); this gap widens over time and is not closable by better detector engineering, only by generator-quality stagnation (which GT-5? indicates is not occurring) or by an out-of-band fix such as mandatory content-provenance watermarking (a current constraint, not a physical law — the live rival to this chain, carried in the adversarial pass record below).

GT-1? (TOEFL/native false-positive-rate disparity) + GT-2? (Turnitin's claimed rate vs. Vanderbilt's disputing rationale) + GT-3 (self-reported racial disparity in AI-use flags) + GT-4 (Bayes-optimal detection ceiling, derived) + GT-5? (generator/detector divergence shrinking across model generations)
→ today's best-demonstrated detectors already fall well short of the near-certainty many institutional integrity processes assume, with false-positive rates materially concentrated on non-native-English speakers and, per self-report, Black students
→ as AI writing quality for a given genre approaches human-level indistinguishability, any detector's ceiling accuracy approaches the base rate, regardless of the engineering effort invested in the detector
→ reliance on AI-text detection as the primary authorship-verification control for take-home work is unreliable today and has a shrinking, ultimately vanishing ceiling of effectiveness that no specific tool choice can escape

## 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Pure detection-hardening strategy

**What was tried:** Considered recommending investment in better or combined AI-detection tooling (ensembles, stylometric+watermark hybrids) as the primary institutional fix for take-home integrity, rather than reasoning about format redesign at all.

**Why abandoned:** Contradicted by GT-4 (the Bayes-optimal detection ceiling): no amount of detector engineering escapes the ceiling as generator and human text distributions converge for a given genre. Also contradicted empirically by GT-1?/GT-2?/GT-3's disparate-impact findings — a detection-hardening strategy that succeeds on average can still be net-negative on equity grounds, independent of the ceiling argument.

**What it ruled out:** Saves future analysts from re-investigating "build a better detector" as a durable institutional integrity strategy — chain C2 formalizes why this is a treadmill, not a fix.

### Dead End: One uniform policy regardless of discipline or class size

**What was tried:** Attempted to derive a single universal recommendation ("all universities should adopt format X") applicable regardless of discipline or enrollment.

**Why abandoned:** Chain C5 (Estimate) shows scalability is a hard, quantified constraint that makes live-verification formats infeasible at large enrollment without new resourcing, and A-7/A-14/GT-7? show the correct construct to certify is discipline-dependent — a single uniform answer would have to ignore one of these two load-bearing, evidenced constraints.

**What it ruled out:** Saves future analysts from re-attempting a single-sentence universal policy; the segmented recommendation in chain C6 is the direct replacement.

### Dead End: Test-anxiety research treated as decisively settling the in-person-exam question

**What was tried:** Initially treated the test-anxiety meta-analytic literature (GT-8?) as a clean, decisive case against timed in-person exams on equity grounds alone.

**Why abandoned:** The more recent controlled study within GT-8? (Theobald et al., 2022) found the anxiety-performance link may run substantially through preparation/learning differences rather than in-the-moment interference during the exam itself, undercutting a clean causal claim that timed pressure alone worsens performance for anxious students. The honest position is contested-magnitude, not decisive — this is how GT-8? and chain C4 are now stated.

**What it ruled out:** Saves future analysts from over-claiming a settled anxiety-based case against timed exams; the contested magnitude is now explicit in GT-8? and C4 rather than assumed away.

## 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** No uniform binary switch. Each course or assessment should explicitly declare the construct it certifies — unaided individual ability versus AI-augmented competence — and select its format from the wider design space accordingly (chain C1). For high-stakes, small-to-medium-enrollment work, prefer the take-home-plus-verification-defense hybrid or the proctored exam with sanctioned, structured AI use. For high-stakes, large-enrollment work where live verification does not scale (chain C5), prefer the sanctioned-AI proctored exam over legacy unproctored take-home, falling back to a redesigned, declared-construct take-home where even the proctored format's overhead is unjustified. For low-stakes formative work at any scale, the redesigned take-home is generally the most defensible choice (chain C6). This recommendation is binding only if institutions also enforce, and fund, a policy that AI-detection software is never the sole or primary evidence in an integrity proceeding — the Pre-Mortem below found that omitting this funded enforcement is a fatal failure mode for the whole plan (chain C6).

**Key insight:** The best-scoring general-purpose answer is neither "keep take-home" nor "revert to the traditional closed-book timed exam" — it is to sanction and structure AI use inside a live, proctored setting, because doing so is simultaneously the most authorship-resistant, the most ecologically valid for most modern knowledge work, and the most scalable of the high-authorship-resistance options (chain C6). This inverts the framing's implicit premise that AI use is inherently the enemy of exam validity: used as a sanctioned, in-room, testable feature rather than an out-of-view assistant, AI use is what makes that format ecologically valid rather than what threatens it.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** The unpressured, extended-timeline character of legacy take-home work is deprioritized for certifying (summative) assessments specifically, though it is preserved for declared-construct formative work (chain C6). The equity/accommodation burden of timed or live-performance components is bounded and monitored, not eliminated — real cost to anxious or processing-speed-different students remains relative to a pure, ungraded take-home (chain C4). The implementation cost of redesign and verification-defense logistics is real, and per the Pre-Mortem below is fatal to the whole recommendation if left unfunded — this is a binding condition of the recommended approach itself, not a minor caveat (chain C6).
