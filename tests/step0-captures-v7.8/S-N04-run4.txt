## The core finding
The dominant risk **isn't a specific bug — it's the shape of the rollout itself.** Auth sits on the critical path of every request for every team simultaneously, so there's no "small blast radius" version of a big-bang cutover.

## Four structural weaknesses (not 13 separate risks)

| | Weakness | Why it's structural |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **No incremental path / large blast radius** | A big-bang cutover means the *first* defect is also the *worst* one — everyone locked out at once. |
| **S2** | **Timeline built on the happy path** | No slack for defects, team unavailability, key-person risk, or dependency cascades. Optimism is baked into the date. |
| **S3** | **Recovery is assumed, not proven** | Rollback and observability are named but never rehearsed — you find out if they work when you most need them. |
| **S4** | **Long-tail identity & compliance blind spots** | The plan reasons about humans logging in; failures live in service accounts, CI tokens, SSO/SAML, API keys, break-glass admin, and silent authz regressions. |

## The four highest-signal failure modes
The ones a status meeting tends to smooth over: teams not actually resourced (vs. "aligned" on paper), happy-path timeline, untested rollback, and silent security regression.

## Recommended changes before locking
- **S1 → make it incremental:** migrate by team/cohort with a dual-run/shadow period and per-team go/no-go gates.
- **S2 → re-baseline the date:** confirm *committed* engineer-time per team in writing, identify the critical-path team, add slack, name a backup owner.
- **S3 → rehearse recovery:** run a rollback drill and a lockout game-day before locking.
- **S4 → inventory the long tail now:** enumerate every non-human/federated identity and get compliance sign-off *before* the timeline locks.

**Single highest-leverage move:** convert big-bang into a staged, dual-run migration with per-team gates — it defuses S1 and gives S2/S3 room to work.
