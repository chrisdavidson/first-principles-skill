## The two things that turn a bug into a disaster

The whole risk picture hinges on two weaknesses that don't *cause* failure but make every other failure **silent and irreversible**:

- **The validation gap.** Most migration plans verify the job *ran* (exit code, "no errors"), not that the data is *correct*. A clean exit tells you nothing about dropped rows, corrupted encodings, or coerced types. The migration passes its own check while silently broken — gets declared a success — and the rollback window closes over a corrupt state.
- **Untested rollback.** A rollback path never executed has unknown correctness *and* unknown duration. "We can roll back" is an assumption, not a capability. By the time users find the corruption, the path back may be gone.

Combined, ordinary recoverable bugs become a late-discovered, irreversible incident.

## The other six failure clusters

- **Correctness/integrity** — silent corruption, encoding/collation mojibake, truncation, type coercion, constraints disabled-for-load and never re-validated
- **Completeness** — dropped/duplicated rows from non-idempotent loads
- **Performance/window overrun** — no timed full-volume rehearsal
- **In-flight write drift** — source not frozen, snapshot diverges during cutover
- **Downstream consumers** — reports/APIs/ETL breaking on the new schema
- **Operational gaps** — first prod-scale run *is* the real cutover; big-bang flip = 100% blast radius

## What to do before locking it in (highest leverage first)

1. **Define "go fine" as a falsifiable gate** — sign-off requires row counts match, content checksums/aggregates match, key-set diff empty, sampled values equal, constraints re-validated, critical queries smoke-tested, all green with a named owner.
2. **Rehearse rollback end-to-end and time it** — source read-only through a bake period, restore-tested pre-cutover backup, explicit rollback triggers and a decision owner.
3. **Timed dry run at full volume on a production clone** — real volume, real dirtiness; confirm window ≥ load + verify + rollback.
4. **Decide the write-during-cutover strategy** — freeze, or CDC-replay the delta with verified zero lag.
5. Field-level mapping + encoding/constraint audit; per-table reconciliation with idempotent loads; inventory + contract-test downstream consumers; rehearse the full runbook with on-call and stage the rollout if feasible.

**The single most important shift:** stop treating "the job ran" as success. Items 1 and 2 close the gap between "we're confident it'll go fine" and "we can prove it did, and undo it if it didn't."
