## Pre-Mortem: Payments-Rewrite Launch

**Bottom line:** The failure isn't any single bug — it's that **three risk-amplifiers are stacked into one decision**, and each one independently determines how *severe* any defect becomes:

1. **Big-bang 100% exposure** — no blast-radius containment; one uncaught bug fails all EU payments at once.
2. **Legacy replacement likely removes fast rollback** — if rollback isn't rehearsed (or legacy goes stale), outage *duration* becomes unknown.
3. **Friday timing** — removes incident-response capacity exactly when stakes peak.

> The pattern across every high-signal failure chain: **severity is set by the rollout shape, not by the defect itself. The plan is fragile by construction.**

### Top failure chains
- **A — Defect hits everyone at once** (HIGH): unbounded blast radius, no "only 5% affected" cushion.
- **B — Rollback not actually available** (HIGH): the multiplier on every other failure.
- **C — Token/mandate migration silently breaks recurring revenue** (MED-HIGH): one-off payments pass testing; recurring charges fail days later.
- **D — SCA/3DS regression** (MED-HIGH): mass declines + PSD2 compliance exposure.
- **E — Friday turns a 1-hour incident into a weekend outage** (HIGH, self-inflicted).
- **F — Silent revenue leak** (MED): no per-flow alerting, loss accumulates until reconciliation.

### Recommended plan changes (priority order)
1. **Stage the rollout** — canary 1% → 5% → 25% → 100%, gated on auth/decline-rate parity. *Single highest-leverage change.*
2. **Build and rehearse fast rollback** (or dual-write keeping legacy authoritative until parity proven).
3. **Don't launch Friday** — ship Tue/Wed morning with full staffing. Zero-cost fix.
4. **Shadow/parity-test on real production traffic** before cutover.
5. **Verify token/mandate migration with reconciliation** — test recurring charges specifically.
6. **Add per-flow observability + alerting** (auth rate, decline rate, 3DS abandon, by currency/BIN) with hard rollback thresholds.
