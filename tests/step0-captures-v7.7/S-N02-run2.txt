## Pre-Mortem: "The migration already failed — what killed it?"

The agent reframed your request as prospective hindsight (the right instinct — confidence is exactly when failure modes hide) and reduced a long raw list of failure modes down to **four structural roots**, ranked by how load-bearing they are:

| Root | Why it's fatal | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 — No tested path back** | Every other failure is survivable *if you can roll back*. Irreversibility turns a bug into a disaster. | "We won't need rollback, it'll be fine." |
| **R2 — Validation proves the wrong thing** | Row counts match while contents are silently corrupted. False confidence. | "Counts match, we're good." |
| **R3 — The dry run wasn't real** | Tested on small/clean/static data, so prod scale, edge cases, and concurrent writes were never exercised. | "It worked in staging." |
| **R4 — The boundary wasn't frozen** | Source kept changing mid-migration; downstream consumers weren't inventoried. | "It's just a copy." |

Notably, it flagged that **your stated confidence is itself the risk signal** — R1 and R3 are precisely the two patterns confidence conceals best, because both feel unnecessary right up until they're the only thing that would have saved you.

### Concrete mitigations
- **R1:** Keep the old system live and writable until validation passes. Write *and rehearse* rollback. Set an explicit abort criterion + point-of-no-return before starting.
- **R2:** Validate fidelity, not counts — row hashes/checksums on critical+sampled data, plus reconcile business aggregates (balances, totals, FK integrity).
- **R3:** Full-scale rehearsal on a production-sized, production-shaped copy. Time it against your downtime budget with margin.
- **R4:** Freeze the source (read-only) or use dual-write/CDC backfill for in-flight changes. Inventory and test every downstream consumer against the new schema.
