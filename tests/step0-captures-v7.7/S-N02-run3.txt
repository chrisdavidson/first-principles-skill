The first-principles agent ran a full pre-mortem on the migration. Here's what it surfaced:

## The core move
It inverted your confidence: **assume the migration already failed, then work backward.** The confidence itself is the most dangerous assumption — it silently presumes a set of preconditions are satisfied that nobody has actually verified.

## Five structural weaknesses (where failure concentrates)

1. **Data reality differs from the model** — nulls, orphans, duplicate keys, encoding/timezone surprises, precision drift between engines. The schema doc lies; the production data tells the truth.
2. **No trustworthy reconciliation** — "the job exited 0" is not "the data is correct." Counts can match while contents are corrupted. And you can't check post-migration numbers without a pre-migration baseline.
3. **The rollback is fiction** — a rollback that's written but never executed is a belief, not a safety net. Especially dangerous if the source gets mutated in place or new writes are accepted before reconciliation passes.
4. **Cutover under pressure** — runs longer than the window, untested manual steps, wrong dependency ordering, and nobody who owns the abort decision.
5. **Downstream blast radius** — reports, integrations, caches, CDC/replication, and *undocumented* consumers reading the old store directly.

## The single highest-leverage action
**A full-scale, timed dry-run that includes reconciliation and an actual rollback execution.** It directly tests weaknesses 1–4 and surfaces most of 5. The agent's pointed note: the most common root of post-migration surprises is "staging is close enough" — it rarely is.
