## Pre-Mortem: Authentication Rollout

The analysis reframed your request as a pre-mortem (plan exists, timeline not yet locked) and worked up from ground truths to failure clusters.

**Ground truths it anchored on:**
- Auth is on every team's critical path — failure is a total outage, not a degradation
- Coordinated cross-team changes fail at the *slowest* team, not the average
- Credential/session migration is typically **one-way** — rollback is harder after cutover than before
- Auth failures are high-blast-radius and surface at peak login traffic, not at deploy

**Four failure clusters to prepare for:**

| Cluster | Failure mode | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Long tail dominates** | One or two teams with legacy integrations / service accounts can't migrate on schedule, forcing indefinite dual-running | Per-team auth-integration inventory *before* locking the date; named owner + drop-dead date for laggards |
| **B — Rollback is a myth** | Problem surfaces hours post-cutover, no clean path back because sessions already migrated | Rehearse rollback at each stage; make any one-way point an explicit gated decision |
| **C — Fails at peak, not deploy** | Green deploy, then token-refresh/expiry/rate-limits break under real volume | Realistic peak load-test; canary team in a low-traffic window; watch refresh/expiry paths specifically |
| **D — Timeline rigidity** | External date forces teams to ship half-migrated state | Decide *now* whether date or scope flexes |

**Adversarial items it flagged** (the "would I suppress this in the meeting?" risks): overconfidence in functional equivalence (untested consumer edge cases), top-down sizing to fit the quarter rather than bottom-up readiness, no clear owner of cross-team shared dependencies (IdP config, shared session store, DNS/certs), and a runbook never executed end-to-end against production-like data.

**Bottom line:** The dominant weakness is *structural, not technical* — the collision between a locked timeline and an unverified per-team readiness assumption (Clusters A + D). Before locking, it recommends (1) a bottom-up per-team integration inventory, and (2) an explicit decision on whether date or scope flexes. Two things to verify first, both high-stakes: rollback reversibility past cutover, and functional equivalence for **non-human auth** (service accounts, long-lived tokens).
