## Bottom line
Three structural weaknesses dominate — they're not isolated risks, they're properties of the plan itself:

- **P1 — No staged rollout removes your cheapest failure detector.** Every defect is discovered at 100% blast radius. The first contact with real production load, data variety, and edge cases happens for *all* EU customers simultaneously.
- **P2 — Friday timing maximizes time-to-recovery exactly when failure is most likely.** The post-launch danger window overlaps the weekend, when engineering, on-call, and Stripe/bank vendor support are all at skeleton coverage.
- **P3 — Stateful cutover with no proven rollback is a one-way door.** In-flight charges, retries, refunds, disputes, and subscription cycles all exist at the cutover instant. If rollback was assumed-but-never-rehearsed, invoking it at 2am Saturday may orphan data that exists only in the new system.

## Highest-leverage changes (in order)
1. **Add a staged rollout** — even a compressed 1%→10%→50%→100% canary with an automated success-rate gate vs. the legacy baseline. This directly contradicts the stated "no staged rollout" constraint; the agent recommends challenging that constraint with stakeholders. Single biggest risk reduction.
2. **Move the cutover off Friday** to a staffed business morning (Tue/Wed). If the date is fixed, pre-position full on-call + vendor escalation for the weekend.
3. **Rehearse rollback end-to-end and define the point-of-no-return.** If a true rollback is actually impossible, say so out loud — it changes the entire risk calculus.
4. **Run shadow/mirror traffic** against the new service in production before cutover for evidence without customer exposure.
5. **Verify four high-risk migrations** before launch: Stripe token/payment-method portability, recurring-subscription state, reconciliation-key continuity, and PSD2/SCA + 3DS coverage across EU banks.
6. **Ship monitoring parity first** — real-time side-by-side success-rate, decline-reason, and revenue dashboards before any traffic moves.

## The one question that most changes the analysis
**Does the rewrite still use Stripe under the hood, or move to a different processor/PCI vault?**
- If it still uses Stripe → token-portability risk (Cluster C) drops sharply.
- If it moves processors → data/state migration becomes your dominant threat.
