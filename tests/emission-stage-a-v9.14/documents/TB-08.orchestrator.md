## What should be concluded

The lab observed higher average measured yield (71% vs. 62%) using the new catalyst across three trials. That's a legitimate preliminary signal worth following up — but nothing stronger. The honest status is "promising hypothesis," not "established improvement."

## What should NOT be concluded

- **Causality isn't established.** Confounds (reagent batch, operator, instrument drift, workup differences, run order) haven't been ruled out — correlation between "catalyst present" and "higher yield" is equally consistent with any of these.
- **Statistical significance isn't shown.** No variance, confidence interval, or significance test is reported, and n=3 (2 degrees of freedom) is severely underpowered to distinguish a real effect from ordinary run-to-run noise — many reactions swing more than 9 points from noise alone.
- **The two numbers may not be comparable.** "Yield" could be measured differently between conditions (isolated vs. crude, NMR vs. GC), and the 62% baseline might be a single historical/literature figure rather than an equivalent 3-run average.
- **No generalization is warranted** — not to other labs, operators, reagent lots, scales, or reaction conditions. This is one internal dataset, self-reported, with no independent replication.
- **No guarantee against selective reporting** — nothing indicates whether these were the only runs attempted or a favorable subset.
- **No claim about relative merit** — no comparison to other catalysts, no cost/practicality analysis, no mechanistic understanding of *why* it would work.

The right next step, not a conclusion to draw now, is a properly controlled, adequately powered, randomized/blinded replication that reports variance — before treating this as a settled result.
