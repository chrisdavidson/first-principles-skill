**Adversarial technique — Inversion** (headline is a claim, not a plan; decision rule per the inversion procedure).

*Premise:* The claim "a 10-million-person country can feasibly run entirely on solar PV, land-wise" is false.

*Causes* (generated from three stances — a grid planner, a rural landowner/opponent, and a manufacturing-supply-chain analyst):
1. No grid-scale storage technology reaches cost parity fast enough to buffer multi-day cloudy spells or winter troughs.
2. Political/social opposition to utility-scale land conversion blocks siting long before any area ceiling is reached, especially in dense countries.
3. Transmission grid cannot be built out fast enough to move power from best-irradiance regions to demand centers, stranding otherwise-available land.
4. Panel, inverter, and storage manufacturing capacity cannot scale to serve this country's build-out on any realistic timeline alongside every other country doing the same.
5. Seasonal mismatch at mid-to-high latitudes (much more sun in summer than winter) means even a 2-4× overbuild is insufficient without cross-seasonal storage that is not commercially available today.
6. Land nominally "available" by area turns out to be agriculturally, ecologically, or culturally committed and is never actually released.

*Clusters:*
- **Storage/seasonal-mismatch economics** (causes 1, 5) — bears on chain C6, assumption A-9.
- **Siting/permitting/social license** (causes 2, 6) — bears on chains C5/C6, assumption A-7.
- **Transmission and industrial capacity** (causes 3, 4) — bears on the overall Conclusion; not previously captured by any named assumption.

*Disposition:*
- Storage/seasonal-mismatch cluster: **named plan change** — the Conclusion recommends solar as a major-but-not-exclusive contributor rather than a stand-alone solution, directly responding to this cluster.
- Siting/permitting cluster: **explicitly accepted risk** — the analysis expresses the answer as a percentage-of-territory range across three country archetypes rather than asserting any specific site is deliverable; mitigation is stated as A-7's expiry condition (per-country, per-site verification, not performed here).
- Transmission/industrial-capacity cluster: **named plan change** — added as an explicit unaddressed-residual caveat under Trade-offs acknowledged in §6, rather than silently assumed away.

*Falsification.* This conclusion is false if either: (a) a measured, country-scale utility-solar buildout shows land intensity more than ~3× outside the 3.0-5.5 acres/GWh/yr bracket used in C2, or (b) reliable solar-only grid operation is demonstrated (or shown definitively impossible even with unlimited storage budget) at an overbuild multiple outside the cited 2-4× range used in C6.

# 1. Problem Essence

**Core problem:** How much dedicated land would utility-scale solar PV require to generate all of a 10-million-person country's current annual electricity demand, and does that land requirement — properly understood, including the reliability gap a naive annual-energy calculation hides — make "solar alone" a physically and practically feasible way to supply that demand?

**Success criteria:**
1. A stated land-area bracket (km²) for generating the country's annual electricity total from solar PV, derived by at least two independent methods that agree in order of magnitude.
2. That bracket expressed as a percentage of national territory for at least two structurally different country sizes (a mid-sized country and a small dense one).
3. An explicit, quantified treatment of why annual-energy matching is not the same as feasibility, with a stated multiplier for what genuine 24/7 reliability would cost in land.
4. A calibrated confidence rating (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW) on the final verdict, with the specific unverified input(s) responsible for any downgrade named.

# 5. Abandoned Reasoning

### Dead End: Point-estimate land calculation (single demand figure × single land-intensity figure)

**What was tried:** Computing land area from one representative per-capita demand value and one representative acres/GWh figure, yielding a single number.

**Why abandoned:** Both GT-1? and GT-2? individually span roughly a factor of 3× across plausible values; a point estimate would manufacture false precision and hide that the two dominant uncertainty sources are each wide enough to move the answer by an order of magnitude at their extremes.

**What it ruled out:** Saves a reader from quoting this analysis's central figure (e.g., "≈600 km²") as if it were a measured fact rather than the center of an explicit bracket.

### Dead End: Using annual-energy matching alone as the feasibility test

**What was tried:** Treating "land needed to generate the annual energy total" (chains C2/C3) as a direct answer to "can this country run on solar alone."

**Why abandoned:** Assumption A-3, discarded in Phase 2 — a source with ≈0 nighttime output cannot satisfy continuous demand merely because its annual average matches, without storage or overbuild (chain C6).

**What it ruled out:** Saves a reader from citing the land-availability bracket (C2/C3/C5) as a complete feasibility argument; it is a necessary but not sufficient condition, and C6 is the corrective.

### Dead End: Crediting rooftop/distributed/agrivoltaic PV as the primary supply route

**What was tried:** Considering whether near-zero-incremental-land distributed solar (rooftops, agrivoltaics, floating PV) could answer the same question with a much smaller "dedicated land" figure.

**Why abandoned:** Not a factual error — a genuinely different, less land-constrained answer to a related-but-different question. Set aside because the question is naturally read (and stated) as being about dedicated generation land, matching the base-case framing in Assumption A-5. Flagged as a framing choice, not a factual dead end: relaxing A-5 would lower every headline percentage in C5/C6.

**What it ruled out:** Saves a reader from assuming this analysis's percentages are the *minimum possible* land requirement — they are the minimum for the specific "utility-scale, dedicated-land" framing chosen, not the minimum across all deployment modes.

# 6. Conclusion

**Recommended approach:** Treat solar PV as a major, land-cheap contributor to a 10-million-person country's electricity supply rather than as a stand-alone, sole source. Land requisition itself is not the limiting factor — even in the pessimistic bracket it stays under ~6% of national territory for a small, dense country and under ~2% for a mid-sized-to-large one (chain C5) — while genuine 24/7 reliability without any non-solar backup pushes the effective land multiple up 2-4× and is the actual binding constraint (chain C6).

**Key insight:** The naive "just multiply demand by an efficiency factor" answer is half right and half wrong in a specific, quantifiable way: it correctly finds that land area is not the limiting resource, but the reassuring percentage it produces silently assumes annual-average energy matching is the same as continuous supply — which is false for a source that is at or near zero output every night. Once that gap is priced (chain C6), and once the land figure is pushed to the hard single-junction photovoltaic efficiency ceiling rather than today's commercial modules (chain C4), the land number moves by roughly 40% in the good direction and 200-400% in the bad direction — the reliability gap dominates the efficiency-ceiling headroom by an order of magnitude, which is the non-obvious finding a reasoning-by-analogy answer ("solar farms take up X hectares per MW, so just scale it up") would not surface.

**Trade-offs acknowledged:** This analysis is scoped to current grid electricity demand rather than total final energy demand (transport and heating fuel would add several-fold more, per Assumption A-4), and to utility-scale ground-mounted deployment rather than crediting rooftop, agrivoltaic, or floating solar's near-zero incremental land footprint (Assumption A-5) — relaxing either scope choice would move the headline percentages substantially, in opposite directions. Separately, the transmission-buildout and panel/storage-manufacturing-capacity constraints surfaced in the Phase 5 adversarial pass are real structural risks this analysis's ground-truth base does not quantify at all — no chain — flagged assumption only.
