Here's the completed first-principles analysis.

## Problem Essence

The real question isn't "REST vs GraphQL" — it's: **which architectural intervention closes the data-aggregation gap causing 4–6 round trips on the 3 mobile clients' screens, at lowest cost/disruption to the 6 non-mobile clients who report no such problem?**

## Ground Truths (verified)

- GraphQL lets clients traverse nested relations in one query (graphql.org)
- HTTP/2 multiplexing reduces latency/head-of-line blocking for *concurrent* requests — it does nothing for *sequentially dependent* calls (MDN)
- The Backend-for-Frontend (BFF) pattern is a documented way to get aggregated, screen-shaped payloads in one round trip **without leaving REST** — with known drawbacks: duplication across BFFs, org-coupling risk (samnewman.io)
- Naive GraphQL nested resolution risks N+1 query blowups; DataLoader-style batching mitigates but adds engineering complexity (graphql/dataloader)
- REST gets HTTP/CDN caching for free via resource URLs; GraphQL has no equivalent global cache identity out of the box (graphql.org)
- Unverified (flagged, not assumed): the exact client count, whether the 4–6 mobile calls are independent or sequentially dependent, and whether a BFF/gateway already exists — these matter and should be checked.

## Key finding: the "GraphQL fixes this" assumption doesn't survive inversion

For "replace REST with GraphQL" to be correct, four things would need to be true: most of the 9 clients (not just 3) would benefit; the team has/can cheaply get GraphQL fluency; losing REST's native caching is immaterial; and no narrower fix would work. **Three of four fail or are unverified** — the complaint is scoped to 3/9 clients, caching loss is a real cost, and cheaper fixes exist.

## Trade-off analysis (weighted, 5 options scored on efficacy, blast radius, incrementality, cost, flexibility, caching, risk)

| Option | Score |
|---|---|
| **Mobile-scoped REST/JSON BFF** | **112** |
| Composite REST endpoints (no new service) | 109 |
| HTTP/2 + batch endpoint (network-only) | 104 |
| Do nothing | 97 |
| Mobile-scoped GraphQL gateway | 94 |

Full REST→GraphQL replacement across all 9 clients is **knocked out entirely** — it forces 6 clients with zero complaint to migrate for no benefit.

Notably, a plain REST BFF beats even a *scoped* GraphQL gateway — GraphQL loses on caching and operational risk, not on round-trip efficacy (they tie there).

## Recommendation (Medium confidence)

1. **Don't replace REST with GraphQL.**
2. Ship a single composite REST endpoint for the worst mobile screen first; formalize into a mobile-owned BFF once 2–3+ screens need it. The 6 non-mobile clients change nothing.
3. Keep BFF ownership with (or tightly co-owned by) mobile teams — a centrally-owned BFF just recreates today's bottleneck.
4. **Before committing budget:** instrument the mobile screens to check if the 4–6 calls are independent or sequentially dependent. If independent, HTTP/2 + parallel fetching alone could beat the BFF option (119 vs 112 in the scoring) at near-zero cost.

**Single biggest risk to this analysis:** that one unmeasured fact — call dependency structure — is what the whole recommendation hinges on, and it's cheap to check before building anything.
