## Land needed to run a 10-million-person country on solar PV alone

**Bottom line:** Land is not the constraint. Reliability is.

**The numbers:**
- Annual electricity demand for 10M people: ~27–80 TWh/yr (central ≈50 TWh/yr), depending on how industrialized the country is.
- Dedicated utility-scale solar land to generate that much energy over a year: **~500–2,000 km²** (central ≈600 km²), cross-checked two independent ways — (a) real-world land-intensity benchmarks (~3–5.5 acres/GWh/yr from US utility-scale fleet data) and (b) a bottom-up build from solar irradiance × panel efficiency × ground-coverage ratio. Both land in the same ballpark, which is a good sign the estimate isn't way off.
- As a share of national territory: **under 2%** for a Portugal- or Sweden-sized country, **~0.5–6%** for a small dense country like Belgium.
- Even pushing efficiency to the hard physical ceiling for single-junction cells (Shockley-Queisser, 33%) only shrinks this by ~40% — the deeper limit is the energy density of sunlight itself, not manufacturing quality.

**The catch:** all of that assumes you only need to match demand *on an annual average* — but solar output is ~zero every night. A grid that's actually solar-only, 24/7, needs several days of storage and/or 2–4× generation overbuild to cover nights, cloudy runs, and seasonal troughs. That pushes the land figure to roughly **1,200–2,400 km²**, i.e. **4–8% of territory** for a small dense country — still not physically impossible, but a much bigger bite of land, plus storage infrastructure the land figure doesn't even capture.

**Verdict (low-to-medium confidence):** Solar can plausibly supply the *majority* of a 10-million-person country's electricity on land alone — it's not land-constrained even in pessimistic cases. But "solar alone, no other source" is a much harder claim: the binding constraint isn't acreage, it's storage economics and multi-day/seasonal reliability, which today's grid-storage technology doesn't cheaply solve. Confidence is capped at LOW specifically on that reliability multiplier (2–4× is a literature range I didn't independently verify this session) — the land-availability half of the claim is more solid (~MEDIUM) on its own.

**Caveats on scope:** this is grid *electricity* demand only (not heating/transport, which would multiply total energy needs several-fold), and it counts only new dedicated ground-mounted solar farms — rooftop and agrivoltaic solar would cut the "dedicated land" figure further without changing the reliability problem at all.
