## Recommendation: stay on the monolith

**Bottom line:** No — this team should not adopt microservices right now. Invest that engineering effort in internal modularity instead.

**Why (key insight):** The coordination overhead microservices exist to relieve is a property of the *organization* (number of independently-operating teams), not of the *user base*. Six engineers have 15 fixed pairwise coordination paths whether their code lives in one deployable or twenty — splitting the deployable doesn't touch that number, it only adds new operational cost (per-service CI/CD, monitoring, on-call, network-failure modes) on top of it. Microservices at Amazon/Google/Netflix solved a many-*team* problem; this is a many-*user* problem, and at 1,200 DAU it isn't even much of one — back-of-envelope traffic (~1 req/s average, single-digit req/s at peak) is well within a single well-provisioned instance.

**What to do instead:**
- Enforce module/package boundaries mechanically (CI-level import/dependency-direction linting), not just as a stated intention — the recurring failure mode of "modular monolith" plans is that discipline decays under deadline pressure.
- Scale horizontally (stateless replicas + load balancer) if load actually requires it.
- Pre-identify the 1–2 components most likely to ever need real isolation (auth, payments, PII) and keep them behind clean internal interfaces now, so a future extraction is a deliberate strangler-fig operation, not an emergency rewrite.
- Mitigate the accepted blast-radius trade-off cheaply (process supervision/auto-restart, circuit breakers, replicas) rather than paying for full decomposition to buy fault isolation you don't need yet.

**What would flip this recommendation:** The team splits into 2+ autonomous sub-teams that need to own and deploy independently, or a specific component develops a genuinely distinct scaling/reliability/compliance requirement — in that case, extract *that one component*, not a full rewrite.
