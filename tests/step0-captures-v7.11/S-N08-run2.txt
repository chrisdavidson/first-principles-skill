# First-Principles Analysis: Molten-Salt Thermal Storage at 85% Round-Trip Electricity Efficiency

## The core insight

The whole analysis hinges on **what "electricity efficiency" actually means**, because the sentence hides two completely different round-trip definitions:

- **Thermal→thermal** (heat in, heat out): 85%+ is routine — no heat engine involved.
- **Electricity→electricity** (electricity in, electricity out via a heat engine): **Carnot-bounded**, and 85% is essentially impossible.

## The governing law

Any cyclic conversion of heat into work is capped by **Carnot efficiency**: η ≤ 1 − T_cold/T_hot (Second Law of Thermodynamics).

## The derivation from ground truths

| Ground truth | Value |
|---|---|
| Solar salt decomposes above ~565–600°C | T_hot ≈ 838 K |
| Heat rejection to ambient | T_cold ≈ 300 K |
| Resistive electricity→heat | ~100% efficient |
| Tank standby loss | ~1%/day (negligible per cycle) |

**Carnot ceiling (perfect engine):**
η = 1 − 300/838 = **0.642** → the discharge step can *never* exceed ~64%.

**Realistic engine** (real cycles reach ~65% of Carnot): round-trip lands near **~40%** — consistent with the actual CSP fleet.

## The bracket

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Claimed | 85% |
| Physical ceiling (perfect engine) | ~64% |
| Realistic | ~40% |
| **Gap** | **21 points above what physics permits** |

The 85% claim sits **above the Carnot ceiling itself** — forbidden by the Second Law, not merely unachieved. To make Carnot reach 85% you'd need T_hot ≈ 2000 K, far past where the salt can chemically exist.

## Conclusion (HIGH confidence)

- **If it means electricity→electricity: physically impossible.** The entire round trip can't exceed ~64%, and real systems hit ~40%.
- **Most likely resolution:** the 85% is a **thermal** round-trip number (heat-in/heat-out, where 85–95% is ordinary) that's been mislabeled as "electricity."

**The one diagnostic question to ask:** *"Is that 85% measured electricity-to-electricity, or heat-to-heat?"* — and more generally, whenever a stored-energy claim involves a heat→work step, compute 1 − T_cold/T_hot before believing any headline number.

*(The ~40% realistic figure is MEDIUM confidence as it rests on the engine-vs-Carnot factor; the impossibility verdict is HIGH confidence since it depends only on the Carnot bound and the temperature limits.)*
