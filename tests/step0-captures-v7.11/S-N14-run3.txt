## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-A:** Molten salt solidifies below its freeze point; solid salt in a pipe is a blockage that can trap and over-pressurize trapped liquid. (Physical — phase behavior.)
- **GT-B:** Nitrate salt thermally decomposes above its ceiling, releasing gas and becoming corrosive/oxidizing. (Physical — chemistry.)
- **GT-C:** Freeze-thaw of trapped salt in a closed volume generates hydraulic pressure (salt expands on melting after contracting on freezing; trapped liquid pockets pressurize). (Physical — thermal expansion in constrained volume.)
- **GT-D:** Commissioning is the first time the integrated system runs hot — every interface is unproven simultaneously. (Definitional.)
- **GT-1? / GT-2? / GT-3?:** Salt chemistry, freeze-protection scheme, instrument ratings — unverified, flagged.

## 4. Failure Enumeration (the core deliverable)

Organized by category so the team can scan for coverage, then clustered into structural weaknesses.

### A. Thermal / Freeze failures
1. **Cold spot freeze-off during first heat-up.** A valve, instrument tap, drain leg, or pipe support acts as a heat sink; salt freezes there before the line is hot. Most common single cause of TES commissioning delay. *(from GT-A)*
2. **Heat-trace gap or controller failure** leaves a section below freeze point. Trace zones often don't map 1:1 to the actual cold spots. *(GT-A, GT-2?)*
3. **Freeze in an instrument dead-leg or impulse line** — small-bore lines freeze first and are easy to overlook.
4. **Over-temperature excursion** during heat-up decomposes salt or damages liners/gaskets. *(GT-B)*
5. **Thermal shock / differential expansion** cracks tank welds, nozzles, or refractory on first hot fill. *(GT-D)*
6. **Trapped-liquid hydraulic overpressure** between two frozen plugs during a thaw — can rupture a line. *(GT-C)*

### B. First-fill / hydraulic failures
7. **Salt quality off-spec** (moisture, chloride contamination) → unexpected freeze point, corrosion, or gas evolution on melt.
8. **Air/moisture in lines** reacts or causes steam flashing on contact with hot salt.
9. **Pump cavitation or dry-running** on first circulation (NPSH miscalculated hot, or sump level wrong).
10. **Drain-down can't complete** — if a freeze event needs emergency drain and the drain path itself freezes, you lose the tank.

### C. Instrumentation / control failures
11. **Level/temperature instruments read wrong hot** (cold-calibrated, wrong fill medium density assumed). *(GT-3?)* — dangerous because every other decision trusts these readings.
12. **Trip setpoints untested at temperature** — first real over-temp is the first test of the trip.
13. **Control logic untested under transients** — heat-up ramp, pump start, recirculation handoff.

### D. Procedural / human / organizational failures
14. **Heat-up ramp rate too aggressive** to hit schedule → thermal shock or freeze chasing.
15. **Commissioning sequence assumes a clean handoff** from construction, but punch-list items (untraced lines, missing insulation) aren't closed.
16. **No rehearsed freeze-recovery procedure** — when a freeze happens (it will), the team improvises under time pressure.
17. **Schedule pressure compresses hold points** — skipping a soak/verification step to make a milestone.
18. **Operator unfamiliarity** — first time anyone has run this specific system hot.

### E. External / environmental
19. **Power loss during heat-up or hot hold** → freeze-protection lost → progressive freeze-off (this is the catastrophic, plant-losing scenario).
20. **Ambient cold-weather commissioning** raises heat-sink load beyond trace capacity.

### Structural weaknesses (the clusters — these matter more than any single line)

Three clusters recur across the list and represent the real signal:

- **Cluster 1 — Cold spots / freeze-protection coverage** (modes 1, 2, 3, 6, 10, 19, 20). The single largest structural weakness in nearly every molten-salt commissioning. The plan should be stress-tested specifically on: trace-zone-to-cold-spot mapping, redundant power to freeze protection, and a *rehearsed* drain/recovery path.
- **Cluster 2 — Instruments trusted before proven hot** (modes 11, 12, 13). Every operational decision rides on readings that have never been validated at temperature. If level/temp is wrong, mitigations for Cluster 1 fire late or not at all.
- **Cluster 3 — Schedule pressure eroding hold points** (modes 14, 15, 16, 17). The organizational failure mode that converts a recoverable single fault into a cascade. This is the one most likely to be suppressed in a group setting — nobody wants to be the person slowing the milestone.
