# Pre-Mortem: Payments-Rewrite Big-Bang EU Launch

**The core issue is structural, not code quality.** Four properties stack into one of the highest-risk launch shapes possible: money-moving + regulated region + 100% blast radius + no escape hatch + worst calendar slot.

## Ground truths
- A big-bang cutover exposes **100% of EU traffic** to any defect simultaneously — blast radius = entire customer base.
- Removing legacy removes the fast escape hatch **unless an explicit, tested rollback is built**.
- Payments are stateful and money-moving — failures lose funds, double-charge, or strand transactions; not idempotently undoable.
- EU payments are regulated (PSD2/SCA, GDPR) — non-compliance hard-fails transactions at the issuer.
- Friday before a weekend minimizes both staffing during the incident window and recovery runway before peak weekend volume.

## Dominant failure chains
1. **No rollback (most likely fatal)** — a defect hits all EU customers with no escape hatch; only path is fix-forward under live fire. "No staged rollout" + "replacing legacy" almost guarantees this.
2. **Load collapse** — new service meets EU peak for the first time in production → cascading auth failures.
3. **Data/state corruption (most expensive)** — migrated mandates/subscriptions/saved-cards or webhook idempotency mismatches → double-charges, unreconcilable ledgers.
4. **SCA/regulatory rejection** — wrong 3DS flow for some EU issuers → a silent subset simply cannot pay.
5. **Blind incident** — partial failure undetected for hours without segmentable observability.
6. **Timing amplifier** — any of the above lands with skeleton weekend staffing.

**Sharpest finding:** the plan likely assumes a rollback that nobody has actually tested. "No staged rollout, replacing legacy" is exactly the shape where everyone assumes someone verified the way back, and no one did.

## Required plan changes (priority order)
1. **Disprove the rollback assumption first** — confirm a tested, rehearsed rollback exists and keep legacy live and reconciling in parallel. If you can't roll back, don't ship big-bang.
2. **Replace big-bang with a staged rollout** — canary by country / small % / internal accounts first.
3. **Move off Friday** — Tue/Wed early in the day.
4. **Verify before cutover** — peak-load test, per-issuer SCA matrix, migration/reconciliation dry-run, webhook idempotency replay.
5. **Stand up segmentable real-time observability** (country/card type/issuer) + staffed incident runbook.
6. **Run the security/PCI review** before commit.

**Confidence: HIGH** that rollout *shape* is the dominant risk; **MEDIUM** on which chain fires first (depends on unverified assumptions).

> If you do only one thing: **guarantee a rollback (or keep legacy as a live fallback) and stage the rollout** — that removes the two properties (irreversibility, full blast radius) that make this plan dangerous.
