## Bottom line
The decisive second-order effect is **dependency coupling**: real-time sync makes checkout availability a function of your inventory system's availability/latency. Combined with the hard deadline stripping out any production-load soak (staging can't reproduce real concurrency or third-party rate limits), this creates a dangerous third-order consequence — **a checkout incident during your biggest customer's spotlight week, inverting the very goal the deadline serves.**

## The effects, traced

**Second-order**
- Real-time sync adds a synchronous dependency in checkout → if inventory source is slow/down, checkout degrades *(adverse)*
- Deadline removes slack for a production soak → first true production-scale test happens with live customers watching *(adverse)*
- A successful demo strengthens the customer/renewal narrative *(favourable)*
- Shipping on schedule preserves team credibility and momentum *(favourable)*

**Third-order**
- Coupling makes a high-visibility checkout outage possible — potentially in front of the customer it was meant to impress *(adverse, goal-inverting)*
- Post-launch stability work pulls on-call/eng capacity away from the next committed work *(adverse)*
- Bimodal payoff: holds up → cheap production validation; fails → maximally public failure
- Raised customer confidence also raises the baseline — future inventory bugs judged more harshly *(mixed)*

## The contradiction it flagged
The deadline exists to impress the customer, yet shipping a checkout-coupled feature with no production-load validation creates a credible path where the feature *harms* checkout during exactly that week. This should send the decision back for one clarifying question.

## Two moves that still hit Friday
1. **Decouple the demo from production** — confirm whether Friday actually needs live production traffic. If it can run behind a feature flag or controlled environment, you ship *to the demo* without exposing 100% of checkout. Highest leverage.
2. **Add a kill-switch / circuit-breaker** — if sync is synchronous in checkout, ensure a timeout + graceful fallback to non-real-time so an inventory hiccup can't block purchases.

**The single question that resolves the most uncertainty:** *Does Friday's demo run against live production checkout, or a controlled path?* Answering it moves confidence on "ship this week" from LOW to HIGH.
