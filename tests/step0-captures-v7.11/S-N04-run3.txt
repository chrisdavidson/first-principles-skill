### The core risk
**"All teams" + "one fixed quarter"** is the load-bearing assumption nearly every failure routes through. Combined with the fact that **auth is a hard dependency** (if auth fails, dependent apps are *down*, not degraded), a fixed all-at-once window removes the recovery slack you need when something slips.

The worst outcome isn't *missing the deadline* — it's getting **stuck at partial migration**, which is more expensive than the status quo because you run and secure two auth systems indefinitely.

### Failure clusters
- **A — Big-bang sequencing:** cutover stall cascade (one team's slip compresses everyone behind it); untested rollback that fails at 2am; everyone cutting over in the final weeks when slack is lowest.
- **B — Identity data & parity:** silent permission drift (over-privilege = security incident, under-privilege = lockout flood); edge cases missed in parity audit — service accounts, M2M tokens, legacy protocol clients, break-glass admin paths — that fail *after* cutover.
- **C — Capacity & adoption:** rollout starving on borrowed team capacity; support channel overwhelmed because auth touches every user.
- **D — Security & trust:** new attack surface incident from a compressed security review; trust collapse after one high-profile leadership/revenue-team lockout stalls the rollout socially.

### What to prepare before locking the timeline
1. **Convert big-bang to phased waves with a pilot** — and if the timeline can't survive being phased, that's itself the finding.
2. **Test the rollback, don't assume it** — rehearse a failed cutover + clean restore before any real team migrates.
3. **Run a parity + identity-data audit now** — explicitly covering service accounts, M2M tokens, legacy clients, break-glass paths.
4. **Pre-fund protected support/rollout capacity** — not borrowed from team roadmaps.
5. **Gate cutover on a completed security review** — don't let timeline pressure compress it.
6. **Add explicit slack and a "halt and hold" criterion** so partial migration can pause cleanly instead of cascading.
