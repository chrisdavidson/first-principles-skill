## Pre-Mortem: Site Drainage Structural Weakness

The agent ran a structured first-principles pre-mortem and flagged that it needs three specifics to move from a general framework to your actual site, but delivered a best-effort analysis regardless.

**Ground truths it anchored on:**
- Water moves to the lowest point and accumulates where it can't drain (gravity)
- Soil loses shear strength as pore-water pressure rises (effective-stress principle)
- Undersized conveyance backs up and overtops once inflow exceeds capacity (mass balance)
- Trapped water imposes hydrostatic — and where it freezes, expansion — load
- Erosion rises sharply with flow velocity at unprotected outfalls

**The failure chains it derived:**
- **A — Foundation undermining** (severe if a structure is downhill): pooling → saturation → lost bearing capacity → differential settlement/cracking. *High confidence in mechanism.*
- **B — Capacity exceedance / overtopping** (likely, recurring): first storm past the weak design capacity floods low areas/roads/below-grade space, and recurs more often than the nominal return period. *High confidence.*
- **C — Slope/retaining-wall instability** (severe, slope-dependent): pore pressure → wall movement or slope slump.
- **D — Outfall erosion** → scour, sediment migration onto neighbors or watercourses, plus permit/environmental liability.
- **E — Freeze-thaw deterioration** (slow, compounding): cracks worsen the original weakness each cycle.

**The highest-signal finding** (its words): a drainage weakness rarely fails as "water in the wrong place" — it fails by routing water into load-bearing soil, structures, or slopes, where consequences jump from nuisance to safety and liability. And the most-suppressed point: **proceeding with a *documented* structural weakness is the single costliest decision in the chain — it converts a later failure from an insurable accident into alleged negligence.** Its default recommendation is remediation-before-build, with the as-built decision documented by the responsible engineer.
