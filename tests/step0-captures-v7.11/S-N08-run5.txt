## First-Principles Decomposition: 85% Round-Trip Efficiency in Molten-Salt Storage

**The core move:** Don't accept the headline number until you pin down the conversion path. The phrase "round-trip *electricity* efficiency" hides the entire question — is there a heat engine in the loop or not?

### The decisive fork

| Path | Governing law | Is 85% possible? |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| **electricity → electricity** (Carnot battery) | Second Law / Carnot limit | **No — impossible** |
| **electricity/heat → delivered heat** (thermal) | Insulation losses only | **Yes — even modest** |

### The derivation

1. **Carnot ceiling (HIGH confidence):** For a typical two-tank salt system — hot side ~565°C (838 K), condenser ~40°C (313 K):

   η_C = 1 − 313/838 = **0.626**

   Even a *perfect* heat engine discharging this storage converts at most ~63% of stored heat to electricity. So electrical round-trip is **≤63% before any other losses**.

2. **Realistic electrical round-trip (MEDIUM confidence):** Real Rankine turbines capture ~60–75% of their Carnot limit, giving ~38–47% at discharge, then × charging (~99%) × thermal retention (~95%) → **~35–45% electricity-to-electricity**.

3. **What 85% must actually mean (HIGH confidence):** *Thermal* round-trip (heat in → heat out, no engine) genuinely reaches 85–99% because only insulation/parasitic losses apply. So an honest "85%" almost certainly refers to thermal round-trip or electricity→delivered-heat.

### The reusable lesson
**Whenever an energy-storage efficiency sounds high, ask whether a heat engine sits anywhere in the loop.** If it does, the Second Law — not the spec sheet — sets the ceiling, and you sanity-check against Carnot (computed from the actual hot/cold temperatures) before believing any number.
