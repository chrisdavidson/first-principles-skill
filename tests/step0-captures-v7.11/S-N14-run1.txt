## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1** (physical law): Solar salt freezes at ~220-240°C. Below this, it solidifies in pipes/valves, blocking flow and risking equipment rupture on re-melt due to thermal expansion of trapped solid. Source: NREL/Sandia molten-salt property data.
- **GT-2** (physical law): Water/moisture contacting molten salt (>~250°C) flashes to steam instantaneously — a steam explosion hazard. Any trapped moisture in vessels, lines, or salt itself is a violent-failure precursor.
- **GT-3** (chemistry): Nitrate salt decomposes above ~565-600°C, releasing NOₓ and forming nitrites/oxides that change melt point and corrosivity. Local hot spots from uneven heating exceed this.
- **GT-4** (chemistry): Molten nitrate salt is corrosive to carbon steel and accelerates with temperature and impurities (chlorides). Corrosion is slow but commissioning thermal cycling/contamination accelerates it.
- **GT-5** (thermo-mechanical): Large temperature swings during commissioning impose thermal-expansion stress on tanks, piping, and welds — the highest-stress events of asset life often occur during first heat-up.
- **GT-6** (operational): Commissioning is when procedures are first executed by humans on live equipment — the gap between as-designed and as-operated is widest here.

## 4. Focused Pre-Mortem Analysis

Working backward from "it failed," the failure modes cluster into six structural weaknesses. I've ordered them by severity (irreversible/safety first).

### Cluster A — Freeze-up (the dominant TES commissioning failure)
*Root: GT-1. This is statistically the most common molten-salt commissioning failure.*

1. **Cold spot freezes the salt** at an untraced valve, instrument tap, drain leg, or pipe support thermal bridge. Plug forms; flow stops; thawing is slow and risky. → *Adversarial check: "Would I have suppressed this?" — Teams routinely assume heat tracing is complete because the design specified it, without auditing as-built coverage. High-signal.*
2. **Heat-trace circuit fails undetected** — single failed circuit with no temperature alarm or redundancy on a critical leg.
3. **Re-melt of a frozen plug ruptures the line** — trapped solid expands, or differential heating cracks the pipe (GT-1 + GT-5).
4. **Drain-down can't complete** before freeze during an unplanned trip/power loss — salt solidifies in the worst possible location.

### Cluster B — Moisture / steam explosion (irreversible, safety-critical)
*Root: GT-2.*

5. **Inadequate vessel/line dry-out** leaves residual moisture; first salt contact flashes to steam. Catastrophic.
6. **Wet salt batch** — hygroscopic nitrate salt absorbed moisture in storage; charged without drying.
7. **Rain/condensation ingress** into open tanks during the fill window.

### Cluster C — Overheating / salt degradation
*Root: GT-3.*

8. **Heater hot spots exceed decomposition temp** due to low flow/stagnant zones during initial circulation, or immersion-heater sheath film boiling.
9. **Control immaturity** — temperature control loops untuned at commissioning overshoot setpoints.

### Cluster D — Corrosion / contamination (latent, surfaces post-commissioning)
*Root: GT-4. Dangerous because it's invisible during commissioning and shows up as a "passed" sign-off that fails months later.*

10. **Chloride/impurity contamination** of the salt batch accelerates corrosion — off-spec salt accepted without assay.
11. **Construction debris/FOD** not flushed before fill, damaging pumps/valves.

### Cluster E — Mechanical / thermal-stress integrity
*Root: GT-5.*

12. **Weld/joint failure on first heat-up** — the highest thermal-stress event reveals a latent fabrication defect.
13. **Pump (vertical cantilever molten-salt pump) failure** — bearing/seal issues at temperature; often long-lead spares.
14. **Tank foundation/insulation failure** under first full thermal load.

### Cluster F — Human / procedural (the cross-cutting amplifier)
*Root: GT-6. This cluster turns recoverable events into failures.*

15. **Procedures not dry-run** — first execution on live hot salt reveals gaps; operator error during melt/fill.
16. **No rehearsed emergency response** to a freeze or leak — slow reaction lets a recoverable event become irreversible (e.g., trip → no fast drain → freeze).
17. **Optimistic commissioning timeline** compresses dry-out, salt assay, and heat-trace verification — the exact steps that prevent Clusters A/B/D. *This is the structural amplifier: schedule pressure systematically attacks the safeguards.*
18. **Single points of dependency** — one heat-trace vendor, one salt supplier, one pump spare. Long lead times mean any failure is a multi-month slip.
