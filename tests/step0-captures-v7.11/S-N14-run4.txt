## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1:** Solar salt (60/40 NaNO₃/KNO₃) has a freezing point of ~220-240°C. Below this it is solid. (Phase chemistry, measured.)
- **GT-2:** A frozen plug in a pipe or valve is locally solid while surrounding salt may be liquid; localized re-melt is slow and a plugged line cannot be drained. (Conduction physics.)
- **GT-3:** Nitrate salt decomposes/oxidizes progressively above ~565°C (accelerating toward 600°C+), releasing gases and forming corrosive species. (Thermal decomposition chemistry.)
- **GT-4:** Molten nitrate salt density is ~1800-1900 kg/m³; a large tank exerts substantial hydrostatic load on floor and foundation. (Measured property.)
- **GT-5:** Thermal expansion of steel tank and salt during first heat-up imposes mechanical stress; differential heating creates gradients steel structures resist poorly. (Thermal-mechanical, physical law.)
- **GT-6:** Molten nitrate salt is a strong oxidizer; contact with organics, oils, or incompatible metals can produce violent exothermic reactions. (Redox chemistry.)
- **GT-7?:** Thermowells/sensors lag and may read wall or stratified-layer temperature rather than bulk salt. (Unverified for your specific instrumentation.)
- **GT-8?:** Chloride contamination (>~0.5-1%) and absorbed moisture dramatically accelerate corrosion of containment steels and can cause steam flashing on melt. (Unverified for your delivered salt.)

## 4. Focused Pre-Mortem Analysis — Failure Modes

Following the pre-mortem procedure: premise restated ("commissioning has failed — what caused it?"), independent enumeration, adversarial interrogation, clustering.

**Cluster A — Freeze/solidification (the dominant irreversible cluster)**
- **A1. Freeze plug in piping/valves/drain lines.** Cold spot from a heat-trace gap, failed trace circuit, or un-traced flange/instrument tap solidifies salt. `GT-2`: a plugged drain line means the tank *cannot be emergency-drained* — turning a recoverable event into a catastrophic one. Derivation: `GT-1 + GT-2 → any unheated wetted surface → local solidification → blocked drain → loss of emergency recovery path`.
- **A2. Heat-trace single-point failure.** `requires-your-input`: is trace power redundant? Are circuits independently monitored? A single breaker trip overnight during fill can freeze a line before anyone notices.
- **A3. Stratified/false-temperature melt.** `GT-7?`: instrument reads "above freeze" while bulk or a cold layer is still solid; operators advance the procedure into a partially-frozen system.

**Cluster B — Thermal-mechanical (the catastrophic-but-quiet cluster)**
- **B1. First-fill thermal shock to tank floor/foundation.** `GT-4 + GT-5`: cold tank receiving hot salt (or hot tank receiving cooler salt) creates gradients that warp the floor, crack the foundation insulating firebrick, or fatigue weld seams. This is the most expensive irreversible failure. Mitigation hinges on a *slow, monitored* heat-up ramp — challenge your plan's ramp rate against vendor limits.
- **B2. Differential expansion buckling.** Roof, shell, and internal components expand at different rates; restraint points concentrate stress.

**Cluster C — Chemistry/contamination**
- **C1. Moisture/steam flashing on first melt.** `GT-8?`: water absorbed in salt or left in the tank flashes to steam as temperature crosses 100°C and again near melt — pressure events, splashing of oxidizer.
- **C2. Overtemperature decomposition.** `GT-3`: a hot spot near an immersion heater or an over-aggressive ramp pushes local salt past ~565°C, decomposing it, generating gas, and degrading the entire inventory's chemistry.
- **C3. Organic/incompatible-material contact.** `GT-6`: residual construction oils, grease, gaskets, or debris left in the tank react with the oxidizer.

**Cluster D — Procedural / organizational (the cluster pre-mortems exist to surface)**
- **D1. First-of-kind crew error.** `requires-your-input`: this is the item the team is most likely to suppress in a group setting. Commissioning procedures assume competencies the crew may not yet have for molten salt specifically.
- **D2. No rehearsed emergency-drain procedure.** If A1 forecloses the drain path and there's no tested fallback, a recoverable freeze becomes a write-off.
- **D3. Schedule pressure overriding the ramp.** The single most common root across failed commissionings: a slipping schedule pressures the team to accelerate the heat-up ramp (→ B1) or skip a hold point (→ A3). This cluster *amplifies every other cluster*.

**Adversarial interrogation result:** The items most likely suppressed in a group review are D1 (crew readiness) and D3 (schedule-pressure-driven ramp acceleration). These are the highest-signal findings precisely because they're socially uncomfortable to raise before sign-off.

**Clustering / structural weaknesses (not isolated risks):**
1. **Loss of the emergency-drain path** is the recurring catastrophic amplifier — A1, D2 all converge here. If the drain can freeze and there's no rehearsed alternative, a single cold spot escalates to total loss.
2. **The heat-up ramp** is the shared root of B1, A3, and C2 — and D3 attacks it directly.
3. **Verification of "molten"** (GT-7?) gates the whole procedure; a false temperature read propagates into every downstream step.
