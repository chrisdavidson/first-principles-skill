## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1:** Solar salt freezes at ~220-240°C; below this it is solid and will plug piping. (Physical property, salt-dependent — verify exact value: GT-1?)
- **GT-2:** Nitrate salts thermally decompose above ~600°C, releasing nitrite/nitrate gases and degrading capacity. (Chemistry)
- **GT-3:** Frozen salt is a thermal and electrical insulator; a frozen plug blocks both flow and impedance-heating current paths. (Physics)
- **GT-4:** Heat-trace failure anywhere along a wetted line creates a localized cold spot that propagates a freeze plug. (Heat transfer)
- **GT-5:** Thermal expansion of salt on melting (~5-10% volume) and of steel piping over a 200°C+ commissioning ramp imposes large mechanical loads. (Physics, magnitude GT-5?)
- **GT-6:** Instrumentation (level, thermocouples, flow) must be calibrated *in molten salt*; ambient calibration does not transfer. (Measurement)

## 4. Focused Pre-Mortem Analysis

**Premise: It is 18 months from now. The molten-salt TES commissioning failed. What caused it?** Here is the raw failure list, interrogated and clustered.

### Cluster A — Freeze plugs (the dominant structural weakness)
- **A1. Heat-trace dead zones at valves, flanges, instrument tees, and pumps.** These high-thermal-mass / hard-to-trace components freeze first. A single frozen valve body can strand the whole fill. → *Corrective:* thermal-mapping audit of every component; redundant trace zones with independent controllers at all valves/flanges; verify trace coverage on as-built, not P&IDs.
- **A2. Freeze during the melt-in itself.** Cold salt charged onto under-heated tank walls/piping freezes locally before bulk melt. → *Corrective:* staged melt-in procedure with hold points; minimum wall-temperature interlocks before salt admission.
- **A3. Heat-trace control/power single points of failure.** Loss of a single controller, breaker, or power feed during a multi-day melt-in causes irreversible plug (GT-3: frozen plug can't be impedance-cleared). → *Corrective:* N+1 power, UPS/generator backup sized for full trace load, alarmed temperature trending. **This is the failure I'd flag as most likely to have been suppressed in a group review** — "the heat trace is rated, it'll be fine" is exactly the assumption that fails at 3 a.m. on day two.

### Cluster B — Thermal/mechanical
- **B1. Differential thermal expansion cracks welds or buckles piping** during the first hot ramp (GT-5). → *Corrective:* validate expansion-loop / cold-spring design against actual commissioning ramp rate; instrument pipe stress at known hot spots.
- **B2. Tank foundation / liner thermal shock** on first hot-salt admission. → *Corrective:* controlled ramp rate, foundation thermocouples, hold points.

### Cluster C — Chemistry / contamination
- **C1. Moisture/contaminant ingress in as-delivered salt or wetted system** causes foaming, off-gassing, or accelerated corrosion on first melt. → *Corrective:* salt assay on receipt; dry-out/bake-out of the system before fill.
- **C2. Local overheating past decomposition (GT-2)** from heat-trace hot spots or fired-heater excursions degrades salt and generates gas. → *Corrective:* high-temperature interlocks below 600°C with margin; verify trace can't exceed it on bare/dry-fire.

### Cluster D — Instrumentation / control (the silent failure)
- **D1. Level and flow instruments read wrong in molten salt** (GT-6), so operators make fill/transfer decisions on bad data — overfill, dry-pump, or false "all clear." → *Corrective:* hot-calibration plan; cross-checks with independent measurement; treat first-fill instrument readings as suspect until validated.
- **D2. Thermocouple placement misses the actual cold spots,** so a forming freeze plug is invisible until flow stops. → *Corrective:* TC placement review driven by the A1 thermal map, not by convenience.

### Cluster E — Procedure / human / organizational
- **E1. No defined abort/recovery procedure for a partial freeze** mid-commissioning. The team improvises under pressure and makes it worse. → *Corrective:* pre-written freeze-recovery and safe-hold procedures with decision authority named.
- **E2. Commissioning schedule pressure compresses hold points,** and the melt-in is rushed across a weekend with a thin crew (recurring root: schedule over-optimism). → *Corrective:* protect hold points contractually; staff the melt-in window fully.

### Recurring patterns (structural weaknesses, not isolated risks)
1. **Single points of failure in the heat-trace system** (A1, A3, D2) — the spine of the whole commissioning. This is the #1 structural weakness.
2. **Trusting ambient/design data where molten-salt-validated data is required** (D1, D2, A1) — instruments, trace coverage, and TC placement all assumed-good rather than verified-on-as-built.
3. **Schedule pressure eroding hold points** (E2, A2) — the organizational root that converts a recoverable situation into an irreversible plug.
