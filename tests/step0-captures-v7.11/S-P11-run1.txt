## Fermi Estimate: Piano Tuners in Chicago

**Answer: ~100 tuners**, bracketed **[40, 125, 375]**.

### The four-step chain

The whole thing is a demand-divided-by-capacity calculation. You build up annual tuning *demand*, then divide by what one tuner can *supply* in a year.

| Step | Quantity | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Households (9.4M metro ÷ 2.5 per household) | ≈ 3.76M |
| 2 | Pianos (× ownership rate ~1/30 — folds in homes, schools, churches, venues) | ≈ 125,000 |
| 3 | Annual tuning demand (× ~1 tuning/piano/yr) | ≈ 125,000 tunings/yr |
| 4 | One tuner's capacity (250 days/yr × 4 tunings/day) | ≈ 1,000 tunings/yr |

**Multiply out:**
```
tuners = 125,000 tunings/yr ÷ 1,000 tunings/tuner/yr ≈ 125 tuners
```

The units cancel cleanly: `(households × pianos/household × tunings/piano/yr) ÷ (tunings/tuner/yr) → tuners`.

### The bracket (why a range, not a point)

Flexing the three softest inputs — piano ownership rate (1/50 → 1/20), tuning frequency (0.7 → 1.5×/yr), and tuner productivity — gives **[~40, ~125, ~375]**. That's roughly one order of magnitude, centered in the low hundreds. The takeaway a Fermi estimate is designed to deliver: it's **~100, not 10 and not 1,000.**
