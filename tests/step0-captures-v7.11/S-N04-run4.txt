## How the auth rollout could go badly

The agent ran a **pre-mortem** ("it's end of quarter, the rollout failed — what caused it?") and clustered the failure modes into **three structural weaknesses** rather than a flat risk list:

### 1. All-teams-at-once simultaneity (the dominant structural weakness)
Rolling out to everyone at once removes your ability to learn cheaply. There's no clean cohort to halt at if a defect appears, the most-custom team's integration becomes the critical path for everyone, and a shared cutover date means failures are correlated — when it slips, it slips for all.

### 2. The lockout / no-rollback trap (highest severity)
Auth is uniquely unforgiving — it's in the critical path of every action and doesn't degrade gracefully. The killer detail: **the rollback itself may require authentication**, so if auth is what's broken, your recovery tooling is unreachable. Break-glass paths are typically the least-tested part of the plan.

### 3. Discovery-cost masquerading as fixed-cost
Migration and per-team integration are sized as if known, but you don't learn the real size until you start. Identity-data messiness (duplicate accounts, forgotten service accounts/CI/cron identities, email-as-key assumptions) is almost always discovered in production.

### The unsaid risks it flagged (adversarial pass)
- "We can't actually roll back, and nobody wants to say so."
- The single most-custom team's timeline *is* the real timeline.
- The quarter deadline will, under pressure, cause someone to skip security validation — the failure mode where "on time" and "got breached" are the same project.

### Recommended plan changes before locking the timeline
1. **Phase into cohorts** — pilot → wave of 2-3 representative teams → rest. Keep the quarter as a target, not a same-day cutover.
2. **Prove the rollback/break-glass path on real infrastructure** before any team migrates; run old + new in parallel post-cutover.
3. **Run a real-data migration dry-run** whose findings *become* your estimate.

> If you do only one, do the rollback proof.
