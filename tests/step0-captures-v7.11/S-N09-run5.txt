### The load-bearing failure chain (highest confidence)
Salt freezes ~220–240 °C → if trace/power fails with no automatic drain-to-cold-tank, an unattended line freezes → re-melt is slow and can rupture pipe from differential expansion → **freeze-on-fill is the single most likely project-ending failure.**

### Concrete asks before on-site build begins
1. **C1 (top priority):** Redundant trace power; automatic drain-to-cold-tank interlock on power/trace loss; thermocouple + impulse-line walkdown targeting the *coldest* geometry (valve bonnets, dead legs, drain legs, instrument taps — not just pipe runs); 24/7 trace-status alarm with named on-call response.
2. **C3:** No-bypass policy on safety interlocks during commissioning (bypass requires written MOC); mandatory integrated hot-run gate before readiness sign-off; salt-specific crew training + emergency procedures (critically: **never water on molten salt** — explosive steam).
3. **C2:** Physical walkdown confirming expansion guides/anchors/hangers are unlocked and set; staged instrumented heat-up ramp with hold points and live expansion/strain monitoring.
4. **C4:** Schedule float specifically around the melt/fill milestone; pre-positioned critical long-lead spares (trace controller, high-temp instruments, special valves); confirm heater/trace utility power is sized and redundant.

**Most-suppressed, highest-signal items** the agent flagged (the ones a group would politely skip): thermocouple/impulse-line placement on the I&C package, bypassing interlocks "to save time," and whether the crew is steam-trained rather than salt-trained (an unverified but load-bearing assumption — GT-7).
