## 3. Ground Truths

- **GT-1:** Solar salt (60/40 NaNO₃/KNO₃) freezes at ~220-240°C; nitrate salt decomposes above ~565-600°C. (Physical/chemical property.)
- **GT-2:** A frozen plug in a pipe blocks flow and cannot be cleared without external heat; localized freeze can rupture components on re-melt expansion. (Physical.)
- **GT-3:** Water/moisture contacting hot nitrate salt flashes to steam — violent expansion, ejection risk. (Physical.)
- **GT-4:** Nitrate salt is a strong oxidizer; contact with organics/incompatible metals drives exothermic reaction. (Chemical.)
- **GT-5:** Commissioning is sequential — each step gates the next; a missed gate propagates downstream. (Process structure.)

## 4. Focused Pre-Mortem Analysis

Working backward from "commissioning failed," organized by the recurring root clusters that emerged.

### Cluster A — Freezing (the dominant structural weakness)
The single largest source of molten-salt commissioning failure. Derivation: `GT-1 + GT-2 → salt below freezing point anywhere it sits → plug or rupture → commissioning halts`.

- **Heat-trace dead zones:** valves, flanges, instrument tappings, drain legs, pump casings, and dead-legs that the trace map missed. These freeze first.
- **Trace-heating control failure:** undersized circuits, failed thermocouples reading air not metal, single-zone control masking a cold spot, no redundancy on critical runs.
- **Preheat ramp too fast / uneven:** differential expansion cracks refractory, liners, or welds before salt ever enters.
- **First-fill freeze:** salt introduced through a line/tank not fully at temperature → immediate plug.
- **Power loss during fill or hold:** no backup for trace heating = clock starts ticking toward a system-wide freeze.

### Cluster B — Moisture and contamination
`GT-3 + GT-4 → moisture or organics in a hot-salt path → steam ejection / exothermic event → safety stop`.

- Residual hydrotest water, condensation, or wet insulation in lines before first fill.
- Salt batch off-spec (chlorides, moisture content) — supplier QC not independently verified.
- Construction debris, oil, organic residue left in tanks/piping (incomplete flush/clean gate).

### Cluster C — Instrumentation and control validity
`Instruments calibrated cold + read at 290-565°C → false readings → operators trust bad data → wrong action`.

- Level/temperature/flow instruments not validated at operating temperature.
- Thermocouples measuring sheath/air rather than metal/salt.
- DCS interlocks untested under hot conditions; trips not proven before they're needed.

### Cluster D — Mechanical / thermal-cycle integrity
- Salt pumps (vertical cantilever) seizing or vibrating at temperature; first hot start untested.
- Thermal-expansion accommodation: spring hangers pinned, guides binding, bellows mis-set → pipe stress on first heat-up.
- Tank foundation / firebrick / liner not surviving first full thermal cycle.

### Cluster E — Procedure, people, and sequencing (`GT-5`)
- Commissioning procedure assumes a step is complete when it is only signed-off-on-paper.
- No clear hold-point criteria — team proceeds past a gate that wasn't actually met.
- Shift handover loses state during a multi-day preheat/fill.
- Emergency response (freeze recovery, salt spill) not rehearsed before live salt.

### Second-order extension
`Freeze plug → forced shutdown → uncontrolled cooldown of the rest of the system → secondary freezes during recovery → schedule slip → pressure to skip remaining hold-points → latent defect signed off`. This is the contradiction worth flagging: schedule pressure created by a *first* failure tends to erode the very gates (Cluster E) designed to catch the *next* one. The clusters are coupled, not independent.
