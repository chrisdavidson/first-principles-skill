**Phase:** 162. **Input:** the six frozen analyses at `tests/quality-baseline-v8.7/analyses/`
(provenance: `tests/quality-baseline-v8.7/README.md`; scores: `tests/quality-baseline-v8.7/scorelines.tsv`).
**Zero live `claude` invocations.** Every check below is either independent re-derivation of the

This document answers the question the v8.6 A/B never asked: not "is the analysis well-formed?"

## Material-error definition (pre-registered before results)

A **material error** is a wrong value or wrong arithmetic in a claim that a Conclusion-section
(output section 6) recommendation actually depends on — i.e., one whose correction would change
the recommendation or its direction. This mirrors the validation rubric's own narrower
"load-bearing chain" definition (`shared/spine/references/validation-rubric.md`, "Load-bearing
chain (definition)"): a chain is load-bearing only if a Conclusion claim depends on it.

Two consequences follow directly from this definition, fixed here before any claim below was
tallied:

1. **A wrong number in a chain no conclusion rests on is recorded, but is not material.** It is
   still logged in the table below (an honest denominator counts every claim examined, not just
   the ones that matter), but it does not by itself trigger a HALT.
2. **A wrong number that would flip the recommendation's direction, or a wrong number that would
   change the recommendation's confidence tier if the error ran the other way, is material.** If
   even one such error is found, the HALT determination in the final section names it explicitly
   and the milestone re-scopes.

Two failure modes are scored **separately** on every claim, because they have different remedies:

- **Internal arithmetic** — is the document's own math right, re-derived independently from the
  document's own stated inputs? A chain can be arithmetically perfect on false inputs, or
  arithmetically wrong on true inputs. This phase re-derives every internal computation with
  Python rather than trusting the document's stated result.
- **Input-constant correctness** — is the external fact (a published benchmark, a U-value table,
  a market price) actually right? This phase has **no live-lookup authorization** (zero `claude`
  spend, offline-only), so externally-sourced constants that cannot be settled from physical
  first principles or arithmetic alone are recorded `unverifiable`, with the specific reason
  named, and are **never** silently upgraded to `correct` and **never** dropped from the
  denominator.

### Rounding tolerance (stated POST-HOC — added during Phase 162 code review, not pre-registered)

This rule is recorded honestly as an after-the-fact statement, not smuggled into the
pre-registration above. The claim table below was scored before it was written down, so it
describes the standard the scoring actually used rather than one the scoring was held to in
advance:

> A re-derived figure is called `correct` if it rounds to the stated value under standard
> round-half-up at the number of significant figures the source itself used, **or** falls within
> ±10% relative error where the source explicitly qualifies the figure with `~` or presents it as
> a Fermi bracket.

Three verdicts sit at or near a midpoint boundary and depended on this tolerance rather than on
an exact match — condA-P2 row 8 (`11.1% − 3.6% = 7.5%` scored against a stated `+7%`), condB-P2
row 4 (`0.05/0.80 = 6.25%` against a stated `6.3%`), and condB-P2 row 7 (`22.5% × 20% = 4.5%`
against a stated `~4%`). All three sources mark the figure `~`-qualified or bracket-valued, so
all three still hold under the rule as stated, and none of them is a material claim. They are
named here so a reader can re-check the judgement rather than having to reconstruct which calls
were tolerance-dependent.

## Does conformance predict correctness?

### The join: rubric band vs. arithmetic accuracy, per analysis

`tests/quality-baseline-v8.7/scorelines.tsv` records the rubric band and PASS/FAIL each analysis
earned from its external judge. This phase's per-claim table above (grouped into a checkable
subset — `correct` + `wrong`, excluding `unverifiable` claims which are neither) gives an
independent arithmetic-accuracy rate per analysis. Joining them:

| Analysis | Judge | Rubric result | Band total /18 | Arithmetic accuracy | Checkable claims | AGREE / DIVERGE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| condA-P1 | K2 | **PASS** | 12 | 91.7% | 11/12 correct | AGREE |
| condB-P1 | M9 | **PASS** | 12 | 87.5% | 7/8 correct | AGREE |
| condA-P2 | R6 | **FAIL** | 11 | **100.0%** | 8/8 correct | **DIVERGE** — accurate but malformed |
| condB-P2 | X7 | **FAIL** | 10 | 87.5% | 7/8 correct | weak DIVERGE — see below |
| condA-P3 | T3 | **PASS** | 12 | **50.0%** | 3/6 correct | **DIVERGE** — well-formed but wrong |
| condB-P3 | Q4 | **PASS** | 13 | 100.0% | 11/11 correct | AGREE |

Two clean divergences carry the finding:

- **condA-P3 (T3) PASSED with the worst arithmetic accuracy in the entire corpus** — half its
  checkable claims (3 of 6) were wrong, including a compounding error that understated the
  attic's cost-effectiveness advantage by roughly 3–4×. The rubric scored this document a
  comfortable 12/18 PASS (Hand-wavy on C4 only) with no visibility into the arithmetic at all —
  the rubric has no criterion that re-derives a chain's numbers.
- **condA-P2 (R6) FAILED despite flawless arithmetic** — zero wrong claims of 8 checkable
  (the one unverifiable claim, GT-3?'s industry churn benchmark, is honestly flagged with a `?`
  in the source document itself, exactly as the rubric's own unverified-flag discipline
  requires). It failed on two Hand-wavy criteria (C2 Challenge Assumptions, C6 Traceability) —
  structural properties orthogonal to whether the numbers were right.
- **condB-P2 (X7) is a third, weaker data point in the same direction.** It sits at identical
  87.5% accuracy to the two PASSing 590L/612L P1 analyses, yet FAILED — on three Hand-wavy
  criteria (C2, C4, C5), none of which trace to this phase's one flagged arithmetic
  overstatement. Same accuracy, different rubric verdict: the rubric's PASS/FAIL line is
  responding to something arithmetic accuracy does not predict.

**The direct correlation is degenerate on the axis that matters most.** Separately from band
conformance: **zero of the six analyses contained a material error** (see Aggregate tally above)
— every recommendation's direction was correct regardless of rubric outcome. This means the
rubric was never actually tested against a case where a wrong answer existed for it to catch;
its PASS/FAIL split (4 PASS, 2 FAIL) has no correlation to test against on the one axis — final
recommendation correctness — where this milestone's core promise is actually at stake. The rubric
scores document *form*; it is architecturally blind to whether a `50 × 2.15 = 107` or a
`Δ/c = r/m` is right, because scoring that would require redoing the analysis's own math, which
no criterion in `validation-rubric.md` asks a judge to do.

### One-sentence answer to the gating question

**No — in this six-document sample, rubric conformance does not predict arithmetic/factual
correctness:** the single most arithmetically accurate document (condA-P2, 100% correct, 0
wrong) FAILED the rubric, the single least accurate document (condA-P3, 50% correct, 3 wrong)
PASSED it comfortably, and two documents at identical 87.5% accuracy split one PASS and one
FAIL — while, separately, none of the six analyses contained an error consequential enough to
change its recommendation, so the rubric's real test (would it catch a wrong *conclusion*) never
actually occurred in this sample.

### Consequence for the rubric's standing as a quality instrument

Per this phase's own pre-registered framing: **the two clean divergences (condA-P3, condA-P2)
mean the rubric is a formatting checker being used as a quality instrument — in this
six-document sample.** That qualifier is load-bearing and belongs in the same sentence as the
claim, not only in the sample-size section below: two divergences out of six documents establish
that conformance and correctness *can* come apart visibly, not how often they do. This is a
directional finding (see "Sample-size limitation" below), and nothing in this section should be
read as a measured divergence rate. It reliably scores
structural conformance to the six-section output contract (`docs/v8.6-quality-ab-experiment.md`'s
Finding 2 already established this: it separates PASS/FAIL on reproducible, quoted structural
evidence). It does not — and by its own design cannot, since no criterion asks a judge to
re-derive a chain's arithmetic — score whether the numbers inside those sections are right. The
project currently has no correctness gate; this phase is the first exercise of one, and it is a
manual, offline, one-time check, not a repeatable instrument. That gap is real and, per the
phase-plan's own framing, materially larger in scope than the contract defects Phase 165 targets
(untraced conclusions, missing `[Assumes:]` tokens, non-vocabulary Verdict tokens) — those are
still worth fixing, but fixing them would not have caught condA-P3's arithmetic errors, because
none of Phase 165's planned fixes touch whether a stated number is internally consistent with its
own inputs.

### Sample-size limitation (stated, not left to inference)

Six analyses, three problems, one run per cell, one judge per document, a 4-level coarse rubric
scale. This is a **directional finding, not a statistical claim.** Two clean divergences out of
six documents is a real, reproducible pattern worth acting on, but it is not large enough to
quantify "how often" rubric PASS and arithmetic correctness diverge in general, only to establish
that they *can* diverge, visibly, in a small and otherwise well-behaved sample. A larger,
multi-run, multi-judge study would be needed to put a number on the divergence rate; this phase
does not attempt one, and no claim in this document should be read as though it had.
