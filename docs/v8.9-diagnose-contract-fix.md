### Purpose

### D-03(a): Evidence criteria — what counts as an attempted §6→§4 closure ledger

An analysis document is judged to have **attempted** a section-6 (Conclusion) to section-4
(Derivation Chains / Ground Truths) closure ledger for a given conclusion claim if the claim's
sentence or bullet in section 6 contains **at least one** of the following, appearing in the claim's
own text (not merely present elsewhere in the document):

1. A chain identifier that also appears as a chain label inside section 4 (e.g. a token of the form
   `chain C` followed by a number, or an equivalent bracketed/parenthetical chain tag used
   consistently by that document's section 4).
2. Two or more ground-truth identifiers (however that document labels ground truths — numbered,
   lettered, or named) that also co-occur together inside a single section-4 chain block.
3. Prose language that explicitly points backward at section 4 as the claim's justification (e.g.
   "as shown in the chain above," "per the ground truths cited in §4," "following from the
   derivation in section 4") even where no bare identifier is repeated, provided the referenced
   section-4 content is locatable and actually supports the claim.

A claim with **none** of the above is judged **not attempted** — a bare assertion with no traceable
link back to section 4, regardless of how confidently it is worded.

### D-03(b): Form taxonomy — recognized attempt forms

Every attempted closure ledger recognized under (a) falls into exactly one of three forms. Each
form has a one-line recognition test so a reader can classify which form (if any) a given analysis
used, without needing to consult the detector:

1. **Explicit ledger table** — Recognition test: the claim sits inside (or is immediately followed
   by) a Markdown table row, or an explicit bulleted "claim → chain/ground-truth" mapping list, whose
   columns/structure exist specifically to pair conclusion claims with their section-4 support.
2. **Inline per-claim citation** — Recognition test: the claim's own sentence or bullet directly
   embeds a chain identifier or ≥2 co-occurring ground-truth identifiers (per D-03(a) criteria 1–2),
   with no separate table or ledger structure — the citation travels with the claim itself.
3. **Prose cross-reference** — Recognition test: the claim's own sentence uses backward-pointing
   natural-language phrasing (per D-03(a) criterion 3) that names or clearly locates the supporting
   section-4 content, without a bare identifier token and without a table structure.

A claim judged "not attempted" under (a) is classified as **no form** (none of the three applies).

### D-03(c): Detector-judging criteria — correct vs false-positive

The detector under judgment is `scripts/check-quality-harness.py`'s `untraced` defect family:
`detect_defects()` slices an analysis into its six output-template sections, extracts conclusion
claims from section 6, and calls `_claim_is_traced(claim_text, chain_ids, chain_blocks)` for each
claim. `_claim_is_traced` returns true iff the claim text names a chain identifier that appears in
`chain_ids` (extracted from section 4), **or** the claim names two or more ground-truth identifiers
that also co-occur together inside a single section-4 chain block (the "D-20" rule cited in the
phase context). A claim for which `_claim_is_traced` returns false is counted in `untraced_claims`,
and `untraced_flag` is set to 1 for the whole document the moment `untraced_claims >= 1` (i.e. one
untraced claim flags the entire document, regardless of how many other claims were traced).

For a given flagged claim (one the detector counted into `untraced_claims`), the detector's verdict
is judged, against the D-03(a)/(b) criteria above and the actual claim text:

- **CORRECT** — the claim, read under the D-03(a) evidence criteria, has **no** traceable link back
  to section 4 in any of the three D-03(b) forms. The detector and the hand-read agree: the claim
  genuinely lacks a §6→§4 trace.
- **FALSE-POSITIVE** — the claim **does** satisfy at least one D-03(a) criterion (i.e. a competent
  human reader classifies it into one of the three D-03(b) forms), but `_claim_is_traced` still
  returned false for it. This happens when the claim uses a form `_claim_is_traced` cannot detect —
  most plausibly the **prose cross-reference** form (D-03(b) form 3), which `_claim_is_traced` has
  no mechanism to recognize at all (it only pattern-matches literal chain-identifier tokens and
  ground-truth-identifier co-occurrence, per its code above), or a **single** ground-truth
  identifier that legitimately supports the claim on its own but falls under the ≥2-co-occurrence
  threshold the D-20 rule requires.

This is a per-claim judgment, not a per-document one — a single document can carry both correctly-
flagged claims and false-positive claims; D-02 below defines how a document's claims reduce to one
document-level signal.

### D-01: Verdict rule (majority-with-mixed-band) — fixed before the read

Each of the six analyses (`Q-P1-run1`, `Q-P1-run2`, `Q-P2-run1`, `Q-P2-run2`, `Q-P3-run1`,
`Q-P3-run2`) is classified, per D-02 below, as **mechanism-signal** or **measurement-signal**. The
verdict is then decided by this rule, fixed now and applied mechanically once the six
classifications exist:

- **≥ 4 of 6** docs classified mechanism-signal → verdict = **mechanism**.
- **≥ 4 of 6** docs classified measurement-signal → verdict = **measurement**.
- Anything else — including a clean 3/3 split, or any distribution reaching neither threshold →
  verdict = **mixed**.

This rule is one-way: once the DIAG-02 read begins, it cannot be revised without becoming a
post-hoc rule dressed as a pre-registered one. Whatever the six classifications turn out to be, this
threshold arithmetic is what computes the verdict — no discretionary override.

### D-02: Per-doc reduction rule — fixed before the read

`tests/quality-baseline-v8.7-postfix/defect-incidence.tsv` records all six documents with
`untraced_flag=1` (this global fact is known and permitted under D-06; no per-doc column value has
been read). Because every document already carries at least one flagged claim, each document
reduces to exactly one binary signal, decided by applying the D-03(c) per-claim judgment to every
one of that document's flagged claims:

- **mechanism-signal** — the document has **at least one** flagged claim judged CORRECT under
  D-03(c) (i.e. the agent genuinely failed to produce a §6→§4 trace for a claim it should have
  traced). One genuinely-untraced claim is sufficient to make the whole document mechanism-signal,
  even if every other flagged claim on that document is a false-positive.
- **measurement-signal** — the document is measurement-signal **only if every one** of its flagged
  claims is judged FALSE-POSITIVE under D-03(c) — i.e. the agent adequately traced all of its
  flagged claims under the D-03(a)/(b) criteria, and the detector flagged the document anyway
  because `_claim_is_traced` could not recognize the form used.

This rule is what resolves partial-trace documents such as the one the phase context flags as
pivotal (a document where most, but not all, conclusion claims are traced): a document is
measurement-signal only if **all** its flagged claims are false-positives; a single genuinely-
untraced claim tips that document to mechanism-signal, regardless of how many other claims in the
same document trace cleanly.
