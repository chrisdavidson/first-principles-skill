### Purpose

a preferred story (the v8.7 lesson: "pre-register thresholds ahead of results; label post-hoc rules

### D-03(a): Evidence criteria — what counts as an attempted §6→§4 closure ledger

An analysis document is judged to have **attempted** a section-6 (Conclusion) to section-4
(Derivation Chains / Ground Truths) closure ledger for a given conclusion claim if the claim's
sentence or bullet in section 6 contains **at least one** of the following, appearing in the claim's
own text (not merely present elsewhere in the document):

1. A chain identifier that also appears as a chain label inside section 4 (e.g. a token of the form
   `chain C` followed by a number, or an equivalent bracketed/parenthetical chain tag used
   consistently by that document's section 4).
2. Two or more ground-truth identifiers (however that document labels ground truths — numbered,
   lettered, or named) that also co-occur together inside a single section-4 chain block.
3. Prose language that explicitly points backward at section 4 as the claim's justification (e.g.
   "as shown in the chain above," "per the ground truths cited in §4," "following from the
   derivation in section 4") even where no bare identifier is repeated, provided the referenced
   section-4 content is locatable and actually supports the claim.

A claim with **none** of the above is judged **not attempted** — a bare assertion with no traceable
link back to section 4, regardless of how confidently it is worded.

### D-03(b): Form taxonomy — recognized attempt forms

Every attempted closure ledger recognized under (a) falls into exactly one of three forms. Each
form has a one-line recognition test so a reader can classify which form (if any) a given analysis
used, without needing to consult the detector:

1. **Explicit ledger table** — Recognition test: the claim sits inside (or is immediately followed
   by) a Markdown table row, or an explicit bulleted "claim → chain/ground-truth" mapping list, whose
   columns/structure exist specifically to pair conclusion claims with their section-4 support.
2. **Inline per-claim citation** — Recognition test: the claim's own sentence or bullet directly
   embeds a chain identifier or ≥2 co-occurring ground-truth identifiers (per D-03(a) criteria 1–2),
   with no separate table or ledger structure — the citation travels with the claim itself.
3. **Prose cross-reference** — Recognition test: the claim's own sentence uses backward-pointing
   natural-language phrasing (per D-03(a) criterion 3) that names or clearly locates the supporting
   section-4 content, without a bare identifier token and without a table structure.

A claim judged "not attempted" under (a) is classified as **no form** (none of the three applies).

### D-03(c): Detector-judging criteria — correct vs false-positive

The detector under judgment is `scripts/check-quality-harness.py`'s `untraced` defect family:
`detect_defects()` slices an analysis into its six output-template sections, extracts conclusion
claims from section 6, and calls `_claim_is_traced(claim_text, chain_ids, chain_blocks)` for each
claim. `_claim_is_traced` returns true iff the claim text names a chain identifier that appears in
`chain_ids` (extracted from section 4), **or** the claim names two or more ground-truth identifiers
that also co-occur together inside a single section-4 chain block (the "D-20" rule cited in the
phase context). A claim for which `_claim_is_traced` returns false is counted in `untraced_claims`,
and `untraced_flag` is set to 1 for the whole document the moment `untraced_claims >= 1` (i.e. one
untraced claim flags the entire document, regardless of how many other claims were traced).

For a given flagged claim (one the detector counted into `untraced_claims`), the detector's verdict
is judged, against the D-03(a)/(b) criteria above and the actual claim text:

- **CORRECT** — the claim, read under the D-03(a) evidence criteria, has **no** traceable link back
  to section 4 in any of the three D-03(b) forms. The detector and the hand-read agree: the claim
  genuinely lacks a §6→§4 trace.
- **FALSE-POSITIVE** — the claim **does** satisfy at least one D-03(a) criterion (i.e. a competent
  human reader classifies it into one of the three D-03(b) forms), but `_claim_is_traced` still
  returned false for it. This happens when the claim uses a form `_claim_is_traced` cannot detect —
  most plausibly the **prose cross-reference** form (D-03(b) form 3), which `_claim_is_traced` has
  no mechanism to recognize at all (it only pattern-matches literal chain-identifier tokens and
  ground-truth-identifier co-occurrence, per its code above), or a **single** ground-truth
  identifier that legitimately supports the claim on its own but falls under the ≥2-co-occurrence
  threshold the D-20 rule requires.

This is a per-claim judgment, not a per-document one — a single document can carry both correctly-
flagged claims and false-positive claims; D-02 below defines how a document's claims reduce to one
document-level signal.

### D-01: Verdict rule (majority-with-mixed-band) — fixed before the read

Each of the six analyses (`Q-P1-run1`, `Q-P1-run2`, `Q-P2-run1`, `Q-P2-run2`, `Q-P3-run1`,
`Q-P3-run2`) is classified, per D-02 below, as **mechanism-signal** or **measurement-signal**. The
verdict is then decided by this rule, fixed now and applied mechanically once the six
classifications exist:

- **≥ 4 of 6** docs classified mechanism-signal → verdict = **mechanism**.
- **≥ 4 of 6** docs classified measurement-signal → verdict = **measurement**.
- Anything else — including a clean 3/3 split, or any distribution reaching neither threshold →
  verdict = **mixed**.

This rule is one-way: once the DIAG-02 read begins, it cannot be revised without becoming a
post-hoc rule dressed as a pre-registered one. Whatever the six classifications turn out to be, this
threshold arithmetic is what computes the verdict — no discretionary override.

### D-02: Per-doc reduction rule — fixed before the read

`tests/quality-baseline-v8.7-postfix/defect-incidence.tsv` records all six documents with
`untraced_flag=1` (this global fact is known and permitted under D-06; no per-doc column value has
been read). Because every document already carries at least one flagged claim, each document
reduces to exactly one binary signal, decided by applying the D-03(c) per-claim judgment to every
one of that document's flagged claims:

- **mechanism-signal** — the document has **at least one** flagged claim judged CORRECT under
  D-03(c) (i.e. the agent genuinely failed to produce a §6→§4 trace for a claim it should have
  traced). One genuinely-untraced claim is sufficient to make the whole document mechanism-signal,
  even if every other flagged claim on that document is a false-positive.
- **measurement-signal** — the document is measurement-signal **only if every one** of its flagged
  claims is judged FALSE-POSITIVE under D-03(c) — i.e. the agent adequately traced all of its
  flagged claims under the D-03(a)/(b) criteria, and the detector flagged the document anyway
  because `_claim_is_traced` could not recognize the form used.

This rule is what resolves partial-trace documents such as the one the phase context flags as
pivotal (a document where most, but not all, conclusion claims are traced): a document is
measurement-signal only if **all** its flagged claims are false-positives; a single genuinely-
untraced claim tips that document to mechanism-signal, regardless of how many other claims in the
same document trace cleanly.

## DIAG-03: Reconciliation Against the Machine Record

### Method

`_untraced_claims_text` was derived for each of the six frozen analyses by importing
`scripts/check-quality-harness.py` as a module (via `importlib`) and calling
`detect_defects(analysis_text, analysis_id)` directly against each file's on-disk text — an
offline, read-only scratch invocation; the harness module was imported, never edited, and
`git status --porcelain scripts/check-quality-harness.py` is confirmed empty below. The returned
`conclusion_claims`/`untraced_claims`/`chain_blocks` counts were checked against
`tests/quality-baseline-v8.7-postfix/defect-incidence.tsv` and matched on all six rows exactly
(8/8, 1/10, 7/7, 8/9, 6/6, 3/4) — confirming the scratch invocation reproduces the frozen,
committed TSV rather than a drifted or hand-miscounted figure.

Reading `_chain_ids()`, `_chain_blocks()`, `_conclusion_claims()`, and `_claim_is_traced()`
(`scripts/check-quality-harness.py` ≈L1983–2116) against the six documents' actual text exposes
the exact mechanism behind every untraced flag. Two independent detector limitations are in play,
plus one extraction-scope ambiguity:

1. **Chain-label-format extraction failure.** `_chain_ids()` only recognizes a chain label
   matching `(?:[A-Z]{2}-\d+|Chain\s+[A-Za-z0-9]+)` — a two-letter-hyphen-number form (`DC-1`) or
   the literal word "Chain" followed by an id (`Chain A`, `Chain C1`). **Q-P1-run1** and
   **Q-P3-run1** label their section-4 chains with bare `C1`–`C5` (no "Chain" word, no hyphen) —
   this matches neither alternative, so `_chain_ids()` returns `[]` for both documents (confirmed
   empirically: `chain_ids: []`, `chain_blocks: 1` for both). No claim in either document could
   ever trace via chain id, regardless of how it was worded, because the label convention itself
   is invisible to the extractor.
2. **Citation-abbreviation / pluralization mismatch.** `_claim_is_traced()` requires the exact
   stored chain-id *string* to appear as a literal substring of the claim text
   (`any(cid in claim_text for cid in chain_ids)`). **Q-P2-run1**'s chains are correctly extracted
   as `Chain C1`–`Chain C4`, but every section-6 citation abbreviates to bare `(C1)`/`(C2)`/
   `(C3, C4)` — the literal string `Chain C1` never appears in the claim text, so all 7 claims
   fail the substring check despite unambiguously referencing those chains. **Q-P2-run2**'s
   embedded ledger cites `chain **C1**` (lowercase "chain", label bolded separately) — literal
   `Chain C1` (capital C) is not a substring of that text either. **Q-P3-run2**'s
   `(Chains C2, C3)` fails for a related reason: the plural "Chains" breaks the singular `Chain C2`
   substring match character-for-character.
3. **Claim-extraction scope ambiguity.** `_conclusion_claims()` mechanically extracts *any*
   bold-colon-lead-in or list-item line passing a length/punctuation filter — it does not
   distinguish a declarative, evidence-bearing verdict sentence from an imperative
   next-step recommendation ("Confirm there is an SLO...", "Run a parallel churn root-cause
   diagnosis...", "Establish whether latency is a binding constraint at all...") or from a
   restatement/summary sentence ("Bottom line: Insulation now...", "Before revisiting the
   decision, close the four unverified preconditions cheaply:"). D-03(a)'s own framing — "a
   section-6 (Conclusion) to section-4 ... closure ledger for a **given conclusion claim**" —
   most naturally describes a declarative assertion resting on the Derivation Chains, not an
   imperative instruction to go verify something. Every zero-citation item found across all six
   documents (see table below) falls into this bucket — none is a fresh, uncited *empirical*
   verdict.

### Resolving Q-P1-run2 (the crux)

`_untraced_claims_text` for Q-P1-run2 names exactly one flagged claim (1 of 10 — the other 9,
including the entire five-item explicit ledger, trace cleanly via literal `Chain X` citations):

> **Establish whether latency is a binding constraint at all** — tie it to an SLO, a user-facing
> metric, or revenue. If nothing is breaching, the migration is optimizing an unconstrained
> variable.

Judged against the flagged claim itself, verbatim: this sentence names **no chain identifier**
(no "Chain A"–"Chain E" token anywhere in it), **no ground-truth identifier** (zero `GT-\d+`
matches — not even a single one below the co-occurrence threshold, unlike several other
documents' near-misses), and **no prose that explicitly points backward at section 4** ("as shown
in," "per the chain above," etc. — the sentence is a forward-looking instruction, not a
backward-derived assertion). Under the strict text of D-03(a), this claim genuinely has zero
traceable evidence of any of the three recognized forms — the CORRECT/FALSE-POSITIVE test in
D-03(c) turns entirely on whether an imperative "go verify this" sentence is the kind of
"conclusion claim" D-03(a) means to police at all, since it is not an empirical assertion resting
on a chain the way the surrounding ledger items are.

This is the same limitation-3 pattern (claim-extraction scope ambiguity) found in five of the
other six documents' zero-citation items (Q-P1-run1's two, Q-P2-run1's two, Q-P3-run1's one,
Q-P3-run2's one) — Q-P1-run2 is not a special case; it is the cleanest single instance of the
pattern, isolated because the other nine claims in the same document already trace cleanly (no
limitation-1 or limitation-2 noise obscuring it).

**Resolution:** under a reading of D-03(a) that limits "conclusion claim" to declarative,
evidence-bearing verdicts (the plain sense of "closure ledger for a claim" — an assertion that
rests on a derivation, not an instruction to perform one), this flagged claim is a
**DETECTOR ARTIFACT** — the document is **measurement-signal**: the claim is a next-step
recommendation, not an empirical assertion requiring its own `Chain X` citation, and the
detector's `_conclusion_claims()` extraction has swept it in regardless of its imperative
grammatical mood. Under the stricter, literal reading of D-03(a) that treats every extracted
sentence identically regardless of mood, this claim has zero qualifying evidence and the document
is **mechanism-signal** — a genuine, if narrow, gap: the agent could have written "...tie it to
an SLO (see GT-2/GT-8, which the doc's own Ground Truths already flag as absent)" and did not.

Both readings are recorded here, undecided between them by this plan (D-01/D-02's arithmetic and
the final verdict are DIAG-04's province, plan 03) — but the same limitation-3 pattern recurs
identically in five of the other six documents' zero-citation items, so however this reading
question is resolved for Q-P1-run2, it must be resolved the same way for the corresponding
zero-citation items across the rest of the corpus, for consistency.

## DIAG-04: Verdict and Costed Recommendation

### Resolving the interpretive fork (post-read disambiguation — labeled as such)

DIAG-03 left one question genuinely under-specified by the DIAG-01 protocol: **does D-03(a)'s
"conclusion claim" include imperative next-step recommendations and restatement/summary
sentences, or only declarative, evidence-bearing verdicts that rest on a Derivation Chain?**
D-03(a)'s own text defines *evidence criteria* for a claim once one exists ("if the claim's
sentence or bullet ... contains at least one of the following") but never defines what counts as
a "conclusion claim" in the first place — so this scope question was not, in fact, pre-registered.
Per the v8.7 lesson this project carries forward ("pre-register thresholds ahead of results; label
post-hoc rules post-hoc"), the disambiguation below is made **after** the DIAG-02/03 read and is
explicitly a **post-hoc reading**, not a pre-registered rule. It is recorded honestly as such
rather than silently applied.

**Adopted reading: declarative-only.** A "conclusion claim" subject to D-03(a)'s tracing
requirement is a declarative, evidence-bearing assertion that purports to rest on section 4 —
not an imperative next-step instruction, a section-intro label, a restatement of an
already-cited verdict, or a logical corollary entailed by adjacent already-cited claims. Three
independent reasons support this reading over the alternative (every extracted sentence counts
identically regardless of mood):

1. **Textual fit.** D-03(a)'s own phrase — "a closure ledger for a **given conclusion claim**" —
   describes an assertion resting on a derivation, not an instruction to go perform one. An
   imperative sentence such as "Run a parallel churn root-cause diagnosis..." does not purport to
