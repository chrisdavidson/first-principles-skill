[docs/v8.5-byte-freeze-relaxation.md](v8.5-byte-freeze-relaxation.md) §4, and it follows that

named in §2 below stays frozen and gating by default. Silence never means permission.

This record is separate from [docs/v9.4-gate-retirement.md](v9.4-gate-retirement.md), which
follows a different predecessor — [docs/v8.7-constraint-teardown.md](v8.7-constraint-teardown.md)

merged; neither predecessor is edited in place by this record or by the other successor.

The [PROCESS.md](PROCESS.md) §1.1 conversion this record authorises (analysis §7.1, executed by
Phase 37's later plans) is REACH: it points an existing product guard, HARN-01
(`scripts/check-act-limb.py`), at the same product surface it already reads, more accurately —
whitespace-insensitive matching and narrower scoping do not add a guard whose subject is another
guard. What §7.1 alone does not do is authorise treating non-semantic rewording of a pinned
literal as permitted by default; that authorisation is this record's own act.

**The M3 default policy.** Reformatting and non-semantic rewording of pinned prose are permitted
by default. Adjacency is asserted only where a named argument requires it. For a pinned literal,
"non-semantic rewording permitted by default" is defined by the following rule, stated verbatim
as this record's own definition:

> a pinned literal is the smallest span that carries its asserted property, with agreement-bearing (inflecting) words dropped.

A **trim** is a contiguous substring of the current literal. A literal is never split into two
anchors. An inflecting word interior to the span that carries the property stays in the literal,
and the fact is recorded.

This policy is **enacted only on HARN-01** (`scripts/check-act-limb.py`) in this phase. Every
other pin-holding script's pins stay frozen until their own evidence or analysis §7.2 (Phase 38,
gated). Silence on them is not permission.

## 2. What Is Relaxed

Exactly three items. No more.

### Item 1: whitespace-insensitive matching

Every literal-presence and literal-count test in HARN-01's `_check_body_text` and
`_check_rubric_text` becomes whitespace-flexible. So does the anchor-membership test inside
`_paragraph_containing`.

The matcher mirrors `_flat`/`_flex_pattern`/`_count_flex` from
`scripts/check-focused-parity.py`. It is copied with attribution, not imported — the same
convention that script's own docstrings already use for `scripts/check-loop-closure.py`.

**Named exception — a Claude's-Discretion resolution, not a locked decision.** The
slice-boundary heading constants (`_PHASE3_START`, `_PHASE4_START`, `_CRIT2_START`,
`_CRIT3_START`, `_CRIT4_START`, `_CRIT5_START`, `_CRIT6_START`) stay exact-match. D-01 locks
whitespace-insensitive matching "for every literal" and does not itself address `_slice`'s
heading lookups; CONTEXT.md is silent on them, so the planner resolved the gap. `_slice` locates
a single-line Markdown ATX heading by its exact line — a heading is not reflowable prose (a
newline inside it would end the heading, so flex matching could only ever admit a text that is no
longer the heading) — and the heading constants are structural locators rather than asserted
literals, so keeping them exact relaxes nothing and pins no asserted property. A reviewer who
reads D-01 as covering the headings too may reopen it; this carve-out is disclosed, not
pre-authorized.

### Item 2: section scope for incidental sub-assertions

The I-1 table (`tests/pin-classification-v9.4/README.md`, as corrected by its Erratum 1) is the
specification. The planner's conversion rule, stated here verbatim:

> The 11 load-bearing rows keep block scope, unchanged. Each incidental sub-assertion converts
> from its block (`para` or `fix_note`) to its section (`phase3` or `crit3`) only if every literal
> it tests has zero occurrences, flex-counted, in the section outside its own block on the live
> emitted tree. Otherwise it keeps block scope, and the row, the count and the control whose I-2
> it would lose are recorded.

This departs from CONTEXT.md's "keep block adjacency only on load-bearing rows." The reason: a
literal that recurs outside its block cannot fail by name under section scope when its block copy
is removed — a section-scoped presence test can no longer distinguish "present in this
sub-assertion's own block" from "present somewhere else in the section." Control (d), which tests
Body-5's population intent, is the known instance: converting Body-5 to section scope would make
Body-9 fire instead of Body-5 when the population intent is stripped, losing Body-5's own I-2.

None of the five live `_paragraph_containing` call sites (`:656`, `:840`/`:841`, `:875`, `:974`),
and none of their "exactly 1 block" guards, is removed or restructured by this item.

Each converted check's failure message is reworded to name the scope it actually reads (WR-09) —
for example, "Phase 3 section missing" rather than "step paragraph missing." The check-ID prefix
and every sub-item detail token stay byte-identical; only the scope-naming clause of the message
changes.

**D-03's acceptance bar, both clauses verbatim.** A trimmed span is accepted only if both hold:

1. It occurs exactly once in its scope — the Phase 3 section for body literals, the Criterion 3
   slice for rubric literals — on the live emitted tree, under flex normalization.
2. Its originating-defect control still fails by name, with the same check ID.

**D-03's fallback.** If trimming breaks either clause, keep the longer literal for that row,
recorded as keeping its current cost, with the reason. Never fall back to block scope to rescue a
trim.

**Two logically forced keeps, pre-announced. Neither is a trial result:**

- Any literal whose current flex count in its scope already exceeds 1 cannot yield a unique trim,
  because a substring occurs at least as often as its superstring. Those constants are kept, with
  the reason "not unique in scope."
- `_PRE05_REGRESSION_SUBSTITUTIONS` is kept, because its `repaired` halves must match the live
  text exactly for control (y) to reconstruct the historical pre-01-05 defect. Trimming them
  changes the reconstruction, which is an I-2 loss for Body-13's divergent-predicate row.

The per-constant outcomes — which trims held, which fell back, and why — are appended in the
"Landing readings" section when measured. The current control count and roster are a generated
fact; read them from [docs/gates/HARN-01.md](gates/HARN-01.md) rather than from a digit carried
in this record.

## 4. Relaxing Anything Further

A future milestone that wants to relax anything beyond §2 must follow this procedure:

1. **Name the specific constant, sentinel, or disposition** to be relaxed — no broad or
   ambiguous grants.
2. **State the measured evidence** motivating the change — the same honesty-not-score discipline
   `docs/v8.5-byte-freeze-relaxation.md` follows.
3. **Author a successor governing record** that names this document and states exactly what it
   supersedes.
4. **Leave this record byte-intact.** In-place edits to a prior milestone's terminal or governing
   record are never an acceptable substitute for a successor record.

Enacting the D-01 policy stated in §1 on any script other than HARN-01 is "relaxing further"
under this procedure — it needs its own named constants and its own evidence, not a reading of
this record's silence as a grant.
