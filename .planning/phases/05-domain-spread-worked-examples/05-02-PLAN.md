    Scenario: a 3-year-old B2B SaaS project-management tool, $2.4M ARR, 240 paying teams at ~$10K/year each, no free tier currently, primarily outbound-sales + referral acquisition. The product team wants to add a free tier (up to 5 users, limited projects) driven by the stated assumption "all our competitors have a free tier, so we need one to compete."

    Section 1: state the core question — whether this product's own economics support a free tier — and checkable success criteria. Keep this section concise; it is not the deepest section.

    Section 2 (deepest, per D-03 — the densest, widest section): a 6-8 row Classified Assumptions Table (Assumption / Type / Treatment / Verdict / Verification). Every row's Verification cell must be non-generic. Required rows at minimum: "a free tier drives top-of-funnel growth" (untested belief, Challenge); "free users convert to paid at a meaningful rate" (untested belief, Challenge, Verification "unverified — flagged"); "competitors' free-tier economics are profitable" (untested belief, Challenge); "all our competitors have a free tier, so we need one" (convention, Challenge, Verdict Discard — this is the analogy-as-evidence row); "adding a free tier has no opportunity cost" (untested belief / false, Discard); and the ICP-self-serve assumption. Every Type drawn from exactly the four-type scheme; every Treatment matches the prescribed treatment for its Type.

    Section 3: 4 ground truths GT-1..GT-4 with specific source citations. GT-4 is the verified gap — "free-to-paid conversion rate for this product in this ICP segment is unknown and has not been measured." Note: GT-4 is verified-as-absent (we know we lack the data), NOT an unverified belief used in a chain — do NOT give it a `GT-N?` suffix. The `GT-N?` notation does not appear anywhere in this file (D-04 — EX-02 has no genuine GT-N? input; that belongs only to EX-04).

    Section 4: 2-3 derivation chains, each `GT-N + GT-M → [intermediate] → [conclusion]` with at least one genuine intermediate step. The main chain must show how the verified data gap (GT-4) forces the conclusion to be "run a time-boxed pilot to generate the missing conversion data" rather than a binary yes/no. Confidence HIGH for the "run a pilot" conclusion (it follows from the verified gap; no chain consumes a GT-N? input).

    Section 5: exactly 1 dead-end using What-was-tried / Why-abandoned / What-it-ruled-out.
